# Chapter 31 — Sector Primers: What Drives Value in Fourteen Industries

A DCF is a template; each industry fills it differently. This chapter gives a short primer for fourteen sectors: what drives revenue, margins and reinvestment; which valuation method is natural; and what traps to avoid. The remarks are qualitative orientation, not data; for any real engagement, replace them with current industry research and company disclosures.

## How to read these primers

For each sector the primer answers five questions:

1. **Economics:** how does the business make money?
2. **Key value drivers:** what determines growth, margin and capital intensity?
3. **Preferred methods:** which valuation approach fits best, and which cross-checks?
4. **Cash-flow and discount-rate specifics.**
5. **Common traps.**

## 31.1 Fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) and packaged foods

- **Economics:** branded, repeat-purchase products sold through distributors and retail; high gross margins, heavy advertising and distribution spend; modest capex.
- **Value drivers:** volume growth (penetration, new categories), pricing and mix (premiumisation), input costs (agricultural commodities, packaging, energy), advertising intensity, distribution reach, working-capital discipline (negative or low NWC for leaders).
- **Methods:** FCFF DCF with long fade period for strong brands; P/E and EV/EBITDA as cross-checks (leaders trade at high multiples reflecting durable ROIC).
- **Specifics:** low beta; high terminal ROIC defensible only with a proven brand moat; treat brand spending as an investment when analysing ROIC.
- **Traps:** extrapolating margin expansion without commodity cycles; ignoring distributor-credit risk; assuming perpetual pricing power; mixing leaders' multiples with a regional player.

*Case reference: Meridian Foods (Chapters 3–16).*

## 31.2 Pharmaceuticals

- **Economics:** branded and generic drugs; high R&D and regulatory costs; patents and exclusivities create time-limited advantages.
- **Value drivers:** product pipeline and launches, patent expiries ("cliffs"), regulatory inspections (plant compliance), US generic price erosion, domestic chronic-therapy share, R&D productivity, working capital (receivables, inventory).
- **Methods:** FCFF DCF with product-level or segment-level forecasts; risk-adjusted NPV (rNPV) for pipeline assets with probabilities of success by stage; relative valuation on EV/EBITDA.
- **Specifics:** capitalise R&D for ROIC analysis; model patent cliffs explicitly rather than smooth growth; mid-year discounting.
- **Traps:** assuming a pipeline succeeds without probability weighting; ignoring regulatory risk (warning letters, import alerts); extrapolating a launch year's earnings.

*Case reference: Aster Pharma (Chapter 27).*

## 31.3 IT services and software

- **Economics:** people-based services (billing rates, utilisation, attrition) or products/platforms (subscriptions, licences).
- **Value drivers:** headcount growth, pricing, utilisation, offshore mix, deal wins and renewals, currency (USD revenue vs INR costs), wage inflation.
- **Methods:** FCFF or FCFE DCF (asset-light; often net cash), P/E and EV/EBIT; for SaaS, EV/ARR and rule-of-40 analysis.
- **Specifics:** very low reinvestment; stock-based compensation is real cost; currency assumptions drive reported margins; treat cash as surplus.
- **Traps:** forgetting that high ROIC attracts competition; ignoring technology disruption; ignoring wage inflation; using USD growth with INR costs without a currency view.

*Case reference: IndiaTech Services (Chapter 28, Case A).*

## 31.4 Banks and NBFCs

- **Economics:** interest-rate spread on loans over funding, fees, credit costs, regulatory capital limits growth.
- **Value drivers:** loan growth, net interest margin, credit cost, cost-to-income, capital adequacy, funding mix (CASA), asset quality (GNPA/NNPA), ROE.
- **Methods:** excess-return model and P/B tied to (ROE − g)/(Ke − g); DDM/FCFE after regulatory capital; P/E.
- **Specifics:** do not un-lever beta; terminal ROE vs Ke is the key judgement; stress test credit cost.
- **Traps:** FCFF; ignoring the capital that growth consumes; peak ROE in terminal.

*Case reference: Chapter 18; Kaveri Finance (Chapter 28, Case C).*

## 31.5 Insurance

- **Economics:** collect premiums, invest the float, pay claims; life insurers sell long-term policies with embedded profit emerging over years; general insurers earn underwriting result plus investment income.
- **Value drivers:** new business volumes and margins (VNB), persistency, combined ratio, investment yield, solvency margin, mortality/claims experience.
- **Methods:** embedded value (life), appraisal value, P/B and ROE, DDM; for general insurers P/B and combined ratio analysis.
- **Specifics:** discount rate (risk discount rate) assumptions drive embedded value; regulatory capital restricts distributions.
- **Traps:** treating policyholder funds as equity; ignoring persistency; mixing embedded-value bases.

## 31.6 Automobiles and ancillaries

- **Economics:** cyclical demand tied to income, interest rates and fuel prices; high fixed costs; capital-intensive; model-cycle capex.
- **Value drivers:** volumes by segment, realisations, commodity costs, product launches, market share, operating leverage, working capital, electrification transition costs.
- **Methods:** DCF on mid-cycle margins; EV/EBITDA and P/E across the cycle; SOTP for conglomerates with financing arms.
- **Specifics:** explicit cycle for 3–5 years then mid-cycle; capex lumpiness; technology transition capex and stranded asset risks.
- **Traps:** valuing at peak-cycle margins; ignoring the cost of electrification; neglecting finance-subsidiary leverage.

## 31.7 Capital goods, engineering and construction

- **Economics:** order-book driven revenue; project execution; working capital heavy (receivables, retention money, advances).
- **Value drivers:** order inflow and backlog conversion, margin on orders, execution, working-capital cycle, government capex.
- **Methods:** DCF built from order book to revenue; EV/EBITDA; P/E; order-book-to-sales.
- **Specifics:** model working capital explicitly (large swings); guarantee and contingent liabilities; arbitration receivables.
- **Traps:** treating the order book as revenue without execution risk; ignoring stuck receivables and contingent claims.

## 31.8 Cement, steel, metals and chemicals (commodity cyclicals)

- **Economics:** price-taking producers; cost position on the industry cost curve determines through-cycle returns.
- **Value drivers:** commodity price and spread, volumes, capacity utilisation, energy and raw material costs, expansions.
- **Methods:** DCF with explicit cycle and mid-cycle terminal; EV/tonne of capacity vs replacement cost; EV/EBITDA on mid-cycle EBITDA.
- **Specifics:** beta over a full cycle; leverage amplifies cycle risk.
- **Traps:** extrapolating the current spread; ignoring capacity additions by competitors; ignoring covenant risk at the trough.

*Case reference: Rajmandir Cement (Chapter 28, Case B).*

## 31.9 Power, utilities and renewable energy

- **Economics:** regulated or contracted tariffs; high capital intensity; long-lived assets; leverage-friendly cash flows.
- **Value drivers:** regulated equity base and allowed return, PPA tariffs, plant load factor, fuel pass-through, receivables from distribution companies, capex pipeline.
- **Methods:** FCFE/DDM for regulated returns; project-level DCF for contracted plants; EV/RAB (regulatory asset base) for regulated utilities; SOTP for portfolios.
- **Specifics:** finite concession or asset life; counterparty receivable risk; regulatory change; for renewables, tariff, curtailment, resource risk and refinancing.
- **Traps:** perpetual growth on a finite-life asset; ignoring receivable delays; assuming returns above the regulated allowed ROE.

## 31.10 Telecommunications and digital infrastructure

- **Economics:** subscriber-based revenue, heavy spectrum and network capex, high operating leverage, regulatory and licence cost.
- **Value drivers:** ARPU, subscriber base and mix, churn, spectrum payments, network capex, tariff actions, data growth.
- **Methods:** DCF with explicit spectrum and capex cycles; EV/EBITDA; EV/subscriber (cross-check only); SOTP for towers and fibre.
- **Specifics:** large lease and licence liabilities as debt-like items; regulatory dues; consolidation structure.
- **Traps:** ignoring deferred spectrum payments and adjusted gross revenue (AGR)-type dues; extrapolating ARPU growth.

## 31.11 Real estate and infrastructure developers

- **Economics:** project-based cash flows; land, approvals and sales velocity dominate; long cycles; regulatory compliance (RERA).
- **Value drivers:** pricing, absorption, construction cost, land bank value, approvals, financing cost.
- **Methods:** NAV by project DCF; cap-rate valuation for rental assets; P/NAV.
- **Specifics:** collections vs recognised revenue; customer advances; land at market not cost.
- **Traps:** valuing land at book; ignoring approvals timing; double-counting customer advances.

## 31.12 Retail and consumer internet

- **Economics:** retail: store economics (sales per square foot, rent, labour); e-commerce: unit economics (contribution per order), CAC, retention.
- **Value drivers:** same-store growth, store rollouts, gross margin, lease costs (Ind AS 116 treatment), inventory turns, delivery costs, take-rate.
- **Methods:** DCF by cohort/store; EV/EBITDA post-lease consistency; EV/GMV or EV/revenue for early-stage platforms; scenario analysis.
- **Specifics:** leases are debt-like — be consistent; store-level maturity curves; negative working capital for some.
- **Traps:** mixing pre- and post-Ind AS 116 metrics; assuming new stores mature as fast as old ones; ignoring cash burn to reach scale.

## 31.13 Healthcare delivery (hospitals and diagnostics)

- **Economics:** capacity-based (beds, centres), occupancy and ARPOB (average revenue per occupied bed), payor mix; long gestation for new facilities.
- **Value drivers:** occupancy, ARPOB, new-bed additions, maturity profile, doctor retention, regulatory price controls.
- **Methods:** DCF by facility cohort (mature vs ramping); EV/bed; EV/EBITDA.
- **Specifics:** ramp-up losses on new facilities; land and building ownership vs lease; doctors' compensation.
- **Traps:** applying mature-hospital margins to new hospitals immediately; ignoring capital intensity.

## 31.14 Airlines, hotels and hospitality

- **Economics:** high fixed costs and operating leverage, volatile demand, capital-intensive (aircraft, properties), leases.
- **Value drivers:** load factor/occupancy, yield/RevPAR, fuel, currency, fleet/room additions, seasonality.
- **Methods:** DCF on mid-cycle; EV/EBITDAR (before rent) when leases are material; NAV for owned hotel real estate.
- **Specifics:** leases/aircraft financing as debt; asset-light vs asset-heavy models in hotels; shocks (pandemics, fuel spikes).
- **Traps:** peak-year extrapolation; ignoring lease obligations; underestimating shock frequency.

## Chapter 31 summary

- Method follows business model: FCFF for operating firms, FCFE/excess return for financials, NAV for real-estate and holdings, cohort/unit-economics DCF for growing platforms and facilities.
- The characteristic traps are sector-specific: peak margins in cyclicals, unprobabilised pipelines in pharma, perpetual growth on finite assets, lease inconsistency in retail and airlines.
- Beta tendencies and capital intensity are starting points for bottom-up analysis, never substitutes.
