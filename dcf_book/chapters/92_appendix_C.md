# Appendix C — Glossary {.unnumbered}

**Adjusted present value (APV).** Value of the unlevered firm plus the present value of financing side effects (tax shields, less distress costs).

**Asset beta.** Beta of a firm's operating assets, free of the effect of financial leverage. Also called unlevered beta.

**Beta (β).** A measure of systematic risk: the covariance of a security's returns with market returns divided by the variance of market returns.

**Bottom-up beta.** A beta built by un-levering and averaging betas of comparable firms, then re-levering to the target capital structure.

**Bridge (equity bridge).** The adjustments from enterprise value to equity value: subtract debt and other claims; add cash and non-operating assets.

**CAPM.** Capital Asset Pricing Model: Ke = Rf + β × ERP.

**Capitalisation rate (cap rate).** Net operating income divided by property value; the reciprocal of a value multiple.

**Capital charge.** Invested capital multiplied by WACC; the dollar cost of the capital employed.

**Capex.** Capital expenditure on fixed assets.

**Cash conversion cycle.** Receivable days + inventory days − payable days.

**Circularity.** A situation in which value depends on WACC, which depends on value (through market-value weights); resolved by iteration.

**Competitive-advantage period.** The time over which a firm can earn returns above its cost of capital.

**Control premium.** The additional value a buyer pays to obtain control of a company over the value of a minority stake.

**Cost of debt (Kd).** The current rate at which the firm can borrow long-term; pre-tax unless stated.

**Cost of equity (Ke).** The return required by shareholders for bearing the risk of the equity.

**Country risk premium (CRP).** An addition to the equity risk premium reflecting the additional risk of investing in a particular country.

**DCF.** Discounted cash flow: valuation by discounting expected future cash flows at a risk-adjusted rate.

**DDM.** Dividend discount model.

**Debt beta.** Beta of a firm's debt; typically close to zero for investment-grade debt.

**Debt-like items.** Obligations that behave like debt and are deducted in the equity bridge (pension deficits, lease liabilities, deferred consideration, etc.).

**Discount factor.** 1 ÷ (1 + r)ⁿ.

**DLOC.** Discount for lack of control.

**DLOM.** Discount for lack of marketability.

**EBIT.** Earnings before interest and taxes (operating profit).

**EBITDA.** EBIT plus depreciation and amortisation.

**Enterprise value (EV).** The value of operating assets: equity value plus debt and other claims, less cash and non-operating assets.

**Equity risk premium (ERP).** The extra return investors require from equity over the risk-free rate.

**EVA.** Economic value added: NOPAT − WACC × invested capital.

**Excess-return model.** An equity valuation as book value plus the present value of returns above the cost of equity.

**Fair market value.** The price at which property would change hands between a willing buyer and a willing seller, neither under compulsion and both reasonably informed.

**Fair value (Ind AS 113).** The price that would be received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants.

**FCFE.** Free cash flow to equity: cash available to shareholders after reinvestment and debt service.

**FCFF.** Free cash flow to the firm: after-tax operating cash flow after reinvestment, available to all capital providers.

**First Chicago method.** A valuation that probability-weights success, sideways and failure scenarios.

**Going-concern value.** Value of a business that will continue to operate.

**Gordon growth model.** Value of a growing perpetuity: C₁ ÷ (r − g).

**Growth identity.** g = reinvestment rate × ROIC.

**Hamada equation.** Relationship between levered and unlevered beta: βₗ = βᵤ[1 + (1 − t)D/E].

**Hurdle rate.** The minimum acceptable return on a project or investment.

**Interest tax shield.** Tax saved because interest is deductible: t × interest.

**IRR.** Internal rate of return: the discount rate at which NPV is zero.

**Invested capital.** Operating capital employed: net fixed assets, working capital, intangibles and other operating assets (or, equivalently, equity plus debt less cash and non-operating investments).

**LBO.** Leveraged buy-out: acquisition financed largely with debt.

**Liquidation value.** Net proceeds from selling a business's assets and settling its liabilities.

**Marginal tax rate.** The tax rate applicable to the next rupee of income.

**Mid-year convention.** Discounting cash flows as if received half-way through each year.

**Monte Carlo simulation.** Repeated random sampling of uncertain inputs to produce a distribution of valuation outcomes.

**MVA.** Market value added: market value of the firm minus invested capital.

**Net working capital (operating).** Operating current assets less operating current liabilities (excluding cash and debt).

**NOPAT.** Net operating profit after tax: EBIT × (1 − t).

**NPV.** Net present value: sum of discounted cash flows, net of the investment.

**Non-operating assets.** Assets not used in the core business (surplus cash, investments, idle land).

**Pre-money / post-money value.** Value of a company before / after a new investment; post = pre + investment.

**Premise of value.** The assumption on which value is estimated: going concern or liquidation.

**Real option.** The right, not the obligation, to take a business action at a future time.

**Relative valuation.** Valuation by reference to the pricing of comparable assets, using multiples.

**Reinvestment rate.** Net investment divided by NOPAT.

**Reverse DCF.** A DCF solved for the assumptions implied by the market price.

**Risk-free rate (Rf).** Return on an asset with no default or reinvestment risk, in the currency of the cash flows.

**ROIC.** Return on invested capital: NOPAT ÷ invested capital.

**ROE.** Return on equity: net income ÷ book equity.

**Sales-to-capital ratio.** Incremental revenue per unit of incremental invested capital; the inverse is capital intensity.

**Scenario analysis.** Valuation under a small set of coherent alternative futures.

**Sensitivity analysis.** Measuring the change in value when one or more inputs change.

**Standard of value.** The type of value being estimated (fair market value, fair value, investment value, etc.).

**Stub period.** A partial first period when the valuation date is not a fiscal year-end.

**Sum-of-the-parts (SOTP).** Valuation of a diversified firm as the sum of its parts' values.

**Terminal value (TV).** The value at the end of the explicit forecast period of all cash flows beyond it.

**Unlevered cost of equity (Ku).** The required return on equity if the firm had no debt.

**Value driver.** An operating variable (growth, margin, reinvestment, ROIC) that determines value.

**Value in use.** Present value of cash flows expected from continuing use of an asset (Ind AS 36).

**WACC.** Weighted average cost of capital: E/V × Ke + D/V × Kd × (1 − t).
