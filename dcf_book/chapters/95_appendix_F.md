## Selected conceptual answers

**Chapter 1, Q4 — Five steps of DCF.** (1) Understand the business and industry; (2) forecast free cash flows; (3) estimate the discount rate; (4) estimate the terminal value; (5) discount, sum, bridge to equity, and test with sensitivities and cross-checks.

**Chapter 3, Q2 — FCFE at WACC overstates value.** The WACC (a blend of the cost of equity and the cheaper after-tax cost of debt) is lower than the cost of equity. Discounting equity cash flows at a lower rate than the equity holders require raises the present value. The debt in the capital structure has already been serviced in FCFE, so using a rate that also includes the cost of debt double-counts the benefit of debt.

**Chapter 12, Q2 — ROIC = WACC.** When new investment earns exactly the cost of capital, growth neither creates nor destroys value. TV = NOPAT × (1 − g/WACC) ÷ (WACC − g) = NOPAT ÷ WACC, in which g cancels.

**Chapter 13, Q5 — DLOC and control premium.** DLOC = 1 − 1/(1 + CP). A 30% DLOC implies 1/(1 + CP) = 0.70, so CP = 1/0.70 − 1 = **42.9%**.

**Chapter 16, Q1 — Stable P/E.** P₀ = D₁ ÷ (Ke − g) = EPS₁ × payout ÷ (Ke − g); dividing by EPS₀: P/E = payout × (1 + g) ÷ (Ke − g).

**Chapter 22, Q2 — Rupee flows at a dollar rate.** The dollar rate omits the expected depreciation of the rupee against the dollar, which is roughly the inflation (or interest-rate) differential. The resulting discount rate is too low and the value too high.
