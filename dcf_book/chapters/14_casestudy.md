# Chapter 14 — Complete Case Study: Valuing Meridian Foods Ltd

This chapter assembles everything from Parts I–IV into one complete valuation. It is a template: the sequence, exhibits and checks are those that a professional valuation report would contain.

## 14.1 Engagement summary

- **Subject:** Meridian Foods Ltd, a packaged-foods manufacturer (fictional).
- **Valuation date:** 31 March 2026.
- **Purpose:** Estimate the intrinsic value of 100% of the equity for a strategic investor evaluating a majority purchase.
- **Standard of value:** Fair market value, going-concern premise, control basis (no minority discount applied).
- **Method:** Primary: DCF (FCFF, WACC). Cross-checks: trading multiples, reverse DCF, sensitivity and scenarios.
- **Currency and units:** ₹ crore, nominal, end-year discounting (mid-year variant shown).

## 14.2 Business and industry review

Meridian operates in the Indian packaged-foods market, which has grown at roughly 9–10% a year in nominal terms on rising incomes, urbanisation and formalisation of the grocery value chain. Meridian has regional leadership in biscuits and growing franchises in ready-to-cook mixes. Strengths: brand equity, distribution, plant efficiency. Risks: input-cost volatility (wheat, edible oil, packaging), competition from national and private-label brands, and distributor-credit exposure.

## 14.3 Historical analysis (summary)

Revenue growth 11–13% a year; EBIT margin up from 10.0% to 12.0% over three years; ROIC up from 10.8% to 12.4%; capex intensity falling from 8.0% to 7.2% of revenue; NWC stable at about 10% of revenue. Cash conversion has been healthy (CFO ≈ 95–100% of EBITDA after tax). No unusual items beyond those normalised in Chapter 8.

Observe that when ROIC = WACC, value barely responds to g (as theory predicts); with ROIC above WACC, higher g adds value.

## 14.14 Conclusion

**Concluded value of 100% of the equity of Meridian Foods Ltd as at 31 March 2026: ₹2,300–₹2,900 crore (₹46–₹58 per share), with a point estimate of ₹2,563 crore (₹51 per share), on a controlling, marketable, going-concern basis.** The range reflects the base-case DCF with reasonable sensitivity to WACC (±0.5 point) and terminal growth (4.5–5.5%) and is supported by cross-checks against peer multiples.

The valuer should record: (i) the key assumptions that drive the answer (margin expansion, terminal ROIC); (ii) the portion of value in the terminal period (81.5%); (iii) limitations (reliance on management's plan, peer comparability); and (iv) subsequent-event considerations.

## 14.15 What the case study teaches

1. **Most of the value is in the terminal value**, so terminal assumptions deserve most of the attention.
2. **Margins and returns, not revenue growth, drive value.** Growth at ROIC close to WACC adds little.
3. **Cross-checks matter.** The implied P/E and EV/EBITDA tell whether the DCF is in the market's neighbourhood and, if not, where to look.
4. **Sensitivity tables are part of the answer, not an appendix.**
5. **Every number has a source** — a schedule, a note, a benchmark — and every judgement is stated.
