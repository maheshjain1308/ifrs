# Chapter 22 — Additional Practice Problems with Solutions

These fifteen new problems mirror the style of the syllabus questions and cover every method in the book. Work each before reading the solution. Amounts are in ₹ crore unless stated.

## Problems

**P1 — Net asset value and NRV.** A company has fixed assets (net) ₹900, net current assets ₹400 and long-term debt ₹500; 10 crore shares. Realisable values: fixed assets ₹1,000, net current assets ₹360; liquidation expenses ₹30. Compute value per share on book and on net-realisable bases.

**P2 — Earnings capitalisation and P/E.** Profits after tax of the last four years: 40, 46, 52, 58. Use weights 1, 2, 3, 4 to find maintainable profit. (a) Capitalise at 12.5%. (b) The company has 8 crore shares; a listed peer trades at P/E 15; apply a 25% discount for non-listing and compute the price per share.

**P3 — Beta, Ke and WACC.** The asset beta of a business is 0.9. The company plans a capital structure of D/E = 50% with a tax rate of 30%. Risk-free 6%; market return 12%; pre-tax cost of debt 10%. Compute the equity beta, cost of equity and WACC.

**P4 — Three-year DCF.** FCFF: 80, 90, 100 for years 1–3; WACC 10%; growth 4% after year 3. Debt 300; surplus cash 50; 10 crore shares. Compute EV, equity and value per share.

**P5 — Enterprise value.** 12 crore shares at ₹250; debt ₹800; cash ₹150; EBITDA ₹500. Compute EV and EV/EBITDA.

**P6 — Chop-shop.** Segment A: sales 800, assets 600, operating income 120; peer capitalisation ratios: sales 0.9, assets 0.7, income 7.5. Segment B: sales 500, assets 400, income 80; ratios 1.2, 1.0, 8.0. Compute the chop-shop value.

**P7 — EVA and MVA.** EBIT ₹650; tax 30%; invested capital ₹3,000 (equity 1,800 and debt 1,200); Ke 15%; pre-tax Kd 10%; market value of the firm ₹4,500. Compute NOPAT, WACC, EVA and MVA.

**P8 — Exchange ratio.** A: net asset value per share ₹120, earning-capitalisation value ₹200. B: ₹45 and ₹70. Use weights 1 (NAV) and 2 (earnings). Compute the exchange ratio.

**P9 — Distressed valuation.** (a) Going-concern equity ₹500, distress-sale equity ₹40, probability of distress 30%: value of equity. (b) Unlevered value ₹1,800; PV of tax benefits of debt ₹200; distress sale value ₹1,000; probability of distress 25%: APV.

**P10 — Venture capital method.** Expected exit value ₹250 in 4 years; investor puts in ₹20 and wants 5× its money. Compute post-money, pre-money, ownership and the equivalent IRR.

**P11 — ESG.** FCFF next year ₹200; WACC 10%; growth 3%. A carbon cost of ₹25 (pre-tax; tax 25%) will apply every year, growing at 3%. Compute the change in EV (a) by cash-flow adjustment, (b) by adding 0.5% to the discount rate.

**P12 — Going versus liquidation.** A business has a DCF value of ₹450. Its assets could be sold for ₹560, with selling costs of ₹30 and taxes on gains of ₹10; liabilities ₹0 (ignore). What should the owners do?

**P13 — Digital platform.** Platform A: valued at ₹480 with 8 crore users. Platform B has 5 crore users but 20% lower ARPU. Value B on A's value per user, adjusted for ARPU.

**P14 — Professional firm.** Fees ₹9.0; operating costs (excluding partners' pay) ₹4.2; three partners each paid ₹0.9 but market pay is ₹0.7; tax 25%; required return 22%. Compute the value.

**P15 — Two-stage FCFF.** FCFF year 1 = ₹60, growing 15% a year for three years (60, 69, 79.35), then 5% for ever. WACC is 12% for years 1–3 and 10% in the stable phase. Compute the value of the firm.

## Solutions

**P1.** *Book:* 900 + 400 − 500 = **₹800** → ₹80 per share. *NRV:* 1,000 + 360 − 30 − 500 = **₹830** → ₹83 per share. NRV exceeds book because the fixed assets are worth more than their book value (+100) by more than the shortfall on current assets (−40) and costs (−30).

**P2.** Maintainable profit = (40×1 + 46×2 + 52×3 + 58×4) ÷ 10 = (40 + 92 + 156 + 232) ÷ 10 = **52.0**.
(a) Capitalised value = 52 ÷ 0.125 = **₹416 crore**.
(b) EPS = 52 ÷ 8 = ₹6.50; adjusted P/E = 15 × 0.75 = 11.25; price = 6.50 × 11.25 = **₹73.13** → equity ₹585 crore. The P/E value is higher than the capitalisation value because the 12.5% rate implies a P/E of only 8, while the peer implies 11.25 — the market is pricing in growth.

**P3.** βₑ = 0.9 × [1 + (1 − 0.30) × 0.50] = 0.9 × 1.35 = **1.215**. Ke = 6% + 1.215 × (12% − 6%) = **13.29%**. D/E = 0.5 ⇒ E/V = 2/3, D/V = 1/3: WACC = 13.29% × 2/3 + 10% × 0.7 × 1/3 = 8.86% + 2.33% = **11.19%**.

**P4.** PV of FCFF: 80/1.1 + 90/1.21 + 100/1.331 = 72.73 + 74.38 + 75.13 = **222.24**. TV (year 3) = 100 × 1.04 ÷ 0.06 = **1,733.33**; PV = 1,733.33 ÷ 1.331 = **1,302.28**. EV = **1,524.52**. Equity = 1,524.52 − 300 + 50 = **₹1,274.52**; per share = **₹127.45**. TV share = 85%.

**P5.** Market cap = 12 × 250 = ₹3,000. EV = 3,000 + 800 − 150 = **₹3,650**. EV/EBITDA = 3,650 ÷ 500 = **7.3×**.

**P6.**
| Basis | Segment A | Segment B | Total |
|-------|----------:|----------:|------:|
| Sales (A: 800 × 0.9; B: 500 × 1.2) | 720 | 600 | **1,320** |
| Assets (600 × 0.7; 400 × 1.0) | 420 | 400 | **820** |
| Income (120 × 7.5; 80 × 8.0) | 900 | 640 | **1,540** |
Average = (1,320 + 820 + 1,540) ÷ 3 = **₹1,226.7**.

**P7.** NOPAT = 650 × 0.70 = **455**. WACC = (1,800 × 15% + 1,200 × 10% × 0.70) ÷ 3,000 = (270 + 84) ÷ 3,000 = **11.8%**. Capital charge = 0.118 × 3,000 = 354. **EVA = 455 − 354 = ₹101.** **MVA = 4,500 − 3,000 = ₹1,500.**

**P8.** A: (120 × 1 + 200 × 2) ÷ 3 = **173.33**. B: (45 × 1 + 70 × 2) ÷ 3 = **61.67**. Exchange ratio = 61.67 ÷ 173.33 = **0.3558** shares of A for each share of B.

**P9.** (a) 500 × 0.70 + 40 × 0.30 = 350 + 12 = **₹362**. (b) Expected bankruptcy cost = (1,800 − 1,000) × 0.25 = 200. APV = 1,800 + 200 − 200 = **₹1,800**: the distress costs exactly offset the tax benefit; debt adds no value at that probability.

**P10.** Post-money = 250 ÷ 5 = **₹50**; pre-money = 50 − 20 = **₹30**; ownership = 20 ÷ 50 = **40%**; IRR = 5^(1/4) − 1 = **49.5%**.

**P11.** Baseline EV = 200 ÷ (0.10 − 0.03) = **₹2,857**. (a) After-tax carbon cost = 25 × 0.75 = 18.75; adjusted FCFF = 181.25; EV = 181.25 ÷ 0.07 = **₹2,589** → −₹268 (**−9.4%**). (b) Discount rate 10.5%: EV = 200 ÷ 0.075 = **₹2,667** → −6.7%. The cash-flow approach shows a larger and more transparent effect.

**P12.** Net liquidation proceeds = 560 − 30 − 10 = **₹520** > DCF value 450. The business is worth more dead than alive: the owners should consider selling the assets (or restructuring to raise the going-concern value above 520). Going-concern value should be preferred only if it exceeds liquidation value.

**P13.** Value per user for A = 480 ÷ 8 = ₹60. B: 5 crore × ₹60 × 0.80 = **₹240 crore**.

**P14.** Normalised PBT = 9.0 − 4.2 − (3 × 0.7 = 2.1) = **2.7**. PAT = 2.7 × 0.75 = 2.025. Value = 2.025 ÷ 0.22 = **₹9.2 crore**. (Reported PBT with actual partner pay of 2.7: 9.0 − 4.2 − 2.7 = 2.1 — normalisation adds ₹0.6 crore.)

**P15.** PV of years 1–3 at 12%: 60/1.12 + 69/1.2544 + 79.35/1.4049 = 53.57 + 55.01 + 56.48 = **165.06**. FCFF₄ = 79.35 × 1.05 = 83.32. TV at year 3 = 83.32 ÷ (0.10 − 0.05) = **1,666.35**; PV = 1,666.35 ÷ 1.4049 = **1,186.08**. **Value = ₹1,351.13 crore.** (TV = 88% of the value.)
