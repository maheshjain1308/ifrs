# Chapter 27 — Valuing Intangibles, Brands and Goodwill

Two themes recur in the source chapter: **goodwill** (in the contemporary approaches) and **unrecorded intangible assets** (the patents in the EVA questions Q15 and Q16, and the licences in the NRV example). This chapter shows how intangibles are valued, how goodwill arises, and why the balance sheet understates the real capital employed by knowledge-based firms.

## 27.1 Why intangibles matter

In a modern enterprise much of the value lies in assets that do not appear on the balance sheet: **brands, patents, licences, customer relationships, technology, human capital, data**. Accounting rules generally require internally generated intangibles to be expensed, so they are absent from the books; acquired intangibles appear at cost.

Consequences for valuation:

| Approach | Effect of unrecorded intangibles |
|----------|--------------------------------|
| **Asset-based** | Book NAV understates value (licences in the NRV example are worth three times their book value) |
| **Income-based** | Earnings capture the benefit of intangibles; value is in the earnings |
| **EVA** | Invested capital is understated unless intangibles are added (Q15, Q16) — EVA is overstated |
| **Relative** | P/B ratios are very high for intangible-heavy firms |

## 27.2 The three families of intangible valuation

| Approach | Idea | Typical techniques |
|----------|------|-------------------|
| **Income approach** | Value = PV of the economic benefits attributable to the asset | Relief-from-royalty; multi-period excess earnings; with-and-without |
| **Market approach** | Prices of comparable transactions | Royalty rates; transaction multiples |
| **Cost approach** | Cost to recreate or replace | Cost-to-duplicate, reproduction/replacement cost less obsolescence |

## 27.3 Relief-from-royalty (brands, trademarks, technology)

The asset is valued as the **royalty the owner is relieved from paying** because it owns the asset rather than licensing it.

1. Forecast the **revenue** attributable to the asset.
2. Select an **arm's-length royalty rate** (from comparable licences).
3. Compute **after-tax royalty savings**.
4. Discount at a rate reflecting the asset's risk (typically WACC plus a premium).
5. Add the **tax amortisation benefit** (TAB) if the asset's cost is tax-deductible over time.

### 27.3.1 Worked example 27.1
A brand supports revenue of ₹800 crore now, growing 10%, 8%, 6%, 5%, 4%, 4%, 3%, 3% over the next eight years, after which the brand is assumed to lose relevance (finite life). Royalty rate 4%; tax 25%; discount rate 14%.

| Year | Revenue | Royalty (4%) | After tax (×0.75) | PV @ 14% |
|-----:|--------:|-------------:|-------------------:|---------:|
| 1 | 880.0 | 35.2 | 26.4 | 23.2 |
| 2 | 950.4 | 38.0 | 28.5 | 21.9 |
| 3 | 1,007.4 | 40.3 | 30.2 | 20.4 |
| 4 | 1,057.8 | 42.3 | 31.7 | 18.8 |
| 5 | 1,100.1 | 44.0 | 33.0 | 17.1 |
| 6 | 1,144.1 | 45.8 | 34.3 | 15.6 |
| 7 | 1,178.4 | 47.1 | 35.4 | 14.2 |
| 8 | 1,213.8 | 48.6 | 36.4 | 12.8 |
| | | | | **144.0** |

PV of after-tax royalties = **₹144.0 crore**. If the brand is amortised over 15 years for tax, the TAB factor at 14% is about 1.114, giving a fair value of **₹160.4 crore**.

*Sensitivity:* a 3% royalty rate gives about ₹108 crore; 5% gives about ₹180 crore — value is proportional to the royalty rate, which is therefore the key judgement and must be supported by comparable licensing data.

## 27.4 Multi-period excess earnings (customer relationships)

For an asset that is the *primary* driver of earnings (e.g., customer relationships), the value is the PV of the **earnings attributable to it after deducting a return on all other contributory assets** (working capital, fixed assets, workforce, brand).

*Worked example 27.2.* Existing customers generate revenue of ₹500 crore now, declining 15% a year through churn. EBIT margin on this revenue 18%; contributory-asset charges 6% of revenue; tax 25%; discount rate 15%.

After-tax excess earnings = revenue × (18% − 6%) × (1 − 0.25) = revenue × 9%.

| Year | Revenue | Excess earnings | PV @ 15% |
|-----:|--------:|----------------:|---------:|
| 1 | 425.0 | 38.3 | 33.3 |
| 2 | 361.3 | 32.5 | 24.6 |
| 3 | 307.1 | 27.6 | 18.2 |
| 4 | 261.0 | 23.5 | 13.4 |
| 5 | 221.9 | 20.0 | 9.9 |
| 6 | 188.6 | 17.0 | 7.3 |
| 7 | 160.3 | 14.4 | 5.4 |
| | | | **112.1** |

Value of the customer relationships ≈ **₹112 crore** (ignoring years beyond 7).

## 27.5 With-and-without (non-compete agreements, key contracts)

Value = PV of cash flows **with** the asset in place − PV of cash flows **without** it. For a non-compete agreement: compare the business with a competitor entering (lost sales, lower margins) against the business protected. If the with-scenario value is ₹900 crore and the without-scenario ₹830 crore, and the probability that the person would actually compete is 50%, the non-compete is worth 0.5 × 70 = **₹35 crore**.

## 27.6 Cost approach

Appropriate for assets with no income stream of their own (assembled workforce, in-house software):
**Value = Cost to recreate − Obsolescence**.
A software platform that would cost ₹25 crore to rewrite with today's tools, but is a legacy architecture with 40% obsolescence, has a cost-approach value of 25 × 0.60 = **₹15 crore**.

## 27.7 Goodwill

**Goodwill** is the excess of the value of a business over the fair value of its identifiable net assets.

### 27.7.1 Accounting goodwill
On acquisition: Goodwill = Consideration − Fair value of identifiable net assets acquired (Ind AS 103). It is tested for impairment annually (Ind AS 36).

### 27.7.2 Valuation goodwill: the super-profit method
**Goodwill = (Maintainable profit − Normal return on net assets) ÷ Capitalisation rate**.

*Worked example 27.3.* Net assets ₹590 crore; normal return 12% → ₹70.8 crore; maintainable profit ₹81.6 crore; super-profit ₹10.8 crore; capitalisation rate 14% → **goodwill ₹77.1 crore**; total value = **₹667 crore** (as in Chapter 19).

### 27.7.3 The goodwill-based approach (retail hotspot)
Goodwill valued by a multiple of annual sales or footfall (Chapter 9): suitable where location is the key asset.

### 27.7.4 Components of goodwill
Going-concern value; workforce; synergies; location; reputation. Under Ind AS 103 identifiable intangibles (customer relationships, brands) are separated from goodwill, so the residual goodwill is smaller.

## 27.8 Purchase price allocation (PPA): an example

*Pioneer* acquires *Target* for ₹950 crore. Fair values of identifiable net assets: tangible net assets ₹560 crore; brand ₹160 crore; customer relationships ₹112 crore; technology ₹40 crore; deferred tax liability on the intangibles (25% × 312) ₹78 crore.

| | ₹ crore |
|---|---:|
| Tangible net assets at fair value | 560 |
| Brand | 160 |
| Customer relationships | 112 |
| Technology | 40 |
| Less: deferred tax liability (25% of 312) | (78) |
| **Fair value of identifiable net assets** | **794** |
| Consideration | 950 |
| **Goodwill** | **156** |

The PPA shows why intangible valuation matters for reporting: a higher brand value reduces goodwill (not tested for amortisation but for impairment) and increases amortisation charges on identifiable intangibles.

## 27.9 Including intangibles in EVA: Herbal Gyan revisited

In Question 15 (Chapter 21) the patent of ₹40 lakh was unrecorded; adding it raised invested capital from 100 to 140, turning EVA from ₹−3 lakh [12 − 0.15 × 100 = −3] to **−₹9 lakh**. The general rule: **invested capital for EVA should include the market value (opportunity cost) of unrecorded intangibles**; otherwise returns on capital are overstated.

## 27.10 Practical guidance

1. **Identify** all material intangible assets (brands, customer lists, technology, licences).
2. **Choose** the technique to match the asset (royalty for brands; excess earnings for customer relationships).
3. **Support** royalty rates and lives with market data.
4. **Avoid double-counting**: the same cash flows cannot be attributed to two intangibles; use contributory-asset charges.
5. **Test reasonableness**: the sum of values of all assets should reconcile to the enterprise value (with goodwill as residual).

## Chapter 27 summary

- Intangibles are largely absent from the balance sheet but central to value.
- Techniques: relief-from-royalty, multi-period excess earnings, with-and-without, cost, market.
- Goodwill = excess of value (or price) over fair value of identifiable net assets; super-profit method: (profit − normal return) ÷ rate.
- EVA invested capital must include unrecorded intangibles.
