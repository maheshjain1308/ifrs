# Chapter 19 — Normalisation and an Integrated Case: Valuing One Company by Every Method

The previous chapters introduced the methods one at a time. In practice a valuer applies several to the *same* company and must reconcile the answers into a defensible range. This chapter first covers **normalisation** — the clean-up of the numbers on which every method depends — and then values a single fictional company, **Sunrise Textiles Ltd**, by the asset, income, DCF, EVA and market approaches.

## 19.1 Normalisation: cleaning the numbers

Whatever the method, reported figures are rarely a perfect basis. Normalisation adjusts them to represent the **maintainable** level of earnings and the **true** level of assets and liabilities. It also makes firms comparable on equal footing (the point made for professional firms in Chapter 17).

### 19.1.1 Income-statement adjustments

| Adjustment | Treatment |
|------------|-----------|
| **Extraordinary/exceptional items** (gain on sale of asset, litigation settlement) | Remove (after tax); consider if recurring |
| **Non-operating income** (interest on surplus funds, rent on idle property) | Remove; value the asset in the bridge |
| **Prior-period items** | Reallocate or remove |
| **Related-party transactions** at non-market terms | Restate to market |
| **Owner/promoter remuneration** above/below market | Replace with market rate |
| **Accounting policy differences** (depreciation, inventory) | Align to a common basis |
| **Non-cash charges** (provisions, ESOP) | Treat as cost if real; add back if purely book entry |
| **Full-year effect of acquisitions/closures** | Pro-forma |
| **Cyclical peaks and troughs** | Use mid-cycle margin |

### 19.1.2 Balance-sheet adjustments
Remove fictitious assets (accumulated losses, preliminary expenses), add unrecorded assets (brands, licences), revalue land and plant, recognise unrecorded liabilities (contingent liabilities, deferred consideration), and separate operating from non-operating assets.

### 19.1.3 The tax effect
Every adjustment to profit changes taxable profit; apply the tax rate to each. An adjustment of ₹10 crore pre-tax is ₹7.5 crore after tax at 25%.

### 19.1.4 Example
Reported PBT ₹120 crore includes: gain on sale of a flat ₹15 crore; insurance receipt for fire ₹5 crore; one-time legal cost ₹8 crore; promoter's salary ₹3 crore above market.
Normalised PBT = 120 − 15 − 5 + 8 + 3 = **₹111 crore**. At 25% tax: PAT = ₹83.25 crore.

## 19.2 Sunrise Textiles Ltd: the facts

*Sunrise Textiles Ltd* is a fictional integrated textile manufacturer. All amounts in ₹ crore; 10 crore equity shares; valuation date: end of the latest financial year (FY0). Purpose: a strategic investor is considering acquiring 100% of the equity.

**Profit and loss (FY0, normalised):**

| | |
|---|---:|
| Revenue | 1,200 |
| EBITDA | 190 |
| Depreciation | (50) |
| **EBIT** | **140** |
| Interest on debt of 300 at 9% | (27) |
| PBT | 113 |
| Tax @ 25% | (28.25) |
| **PAT** | **84.75** |
| NOPAT = EBIT × 0.75 | 105 |

**Profit after tax history (normalised):** FY−3: 62; FY−2: 70; FY−1: 78; FY0: 85.

**Balance sheet (book values):**

| Assets | | Liabilities and equity | |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Fixed assets (net) | 600 | Long-term debt | 300 |
| Net current assets (operating) | 250 | Equity (net worth) | 590 |
| Non-operating investments | 40 | | |
| **Total** | **890** | **Total** | **890** |

**Other information.**
- *Realisable values:* fixed assets 780 (land is worth much more than book); net current assets 215 (debtors and old stock have to be written down); investments 40; liquidation expenses 15.
- *Replacement cost:* fixed assets 840; net current assets 250; investments 40.
- *Operating invested capital:* 600 + 250 = **850**.
- *Peer listed company:* P/E 12; EV/EBITDA 5–6× for textile manufacturers.
- *Cost of capital:* risk-free 7%, market premium 6%, asset beta for textile pure-plays 0.85 (equity beta at the company's low D/E ≈ 0.85 used); cost of debt 9% pre-tax; target debt-to-total-capital of 25% → WACC taken as **12%**.
- *Growth:* NOPAT grows 8%, 8%, 7%, 6%, 6% over five years; incremental ROIC 14%; terminal growth 5% with ROIC 13%.

## 19.3 Asset-based approach

| Basis | Calculation | Value of equity | Per share |
|-------|-------------|----------------:|----------:|
| **Net asset value (book)** | 600 + 250 + 40 − 300 | **590** | ₹59.0 |
| **Net realisable value** | 780 + 215 + 40 − 15 (costs) − 300 | **720** | ₹72.0 |
| **Replaceable value** | 840 + 250 + 40 − 300 | **830** | ₹83.0 |

*Comment.* Book value (590) is the lower limit; NRV is higher because the land is undervalued; replacement cost (830) is the **maximum price** an acquirer would pay for equivalent assets. The asset approach gives a **range of ₹590–₹830 crore** — but says nothing about earning power.

## 19.4 Income-based approach

### 19.4.1 Capitalisation of earnings
Weighted-average PAT (weights 1, 2, 3, 4): (62 + 140 + 234 + 340) ÷ 10 = **77.6**. This looks backward; the latest year's PAT is 85. Using 77.6 is conservative; it adds an economy-of-scale adjustment of +4 → maintainable PAT **81.6**.

| Capitalisation rate | Value | Per share |
|--------------------:|------:|----------:|
| 13% | 627.7 | ₹62.8 |
| **14%** | **582.9** | ₹58.3 |
| 15% | 544.0 | ₹54.4 |

(CAPM gives Ke = 7% + 0.85 × 6% = 12.1% for equity; a 14% rate allows for the fact that the firm will not grow forever and is not listed. This method implicitly assumes **no growth**, so it understates a growing firm.)

### 19.4.2 P/E method
Peer P/E 12; **20% reduction** for non-listing → 9.6. Maintainable EPS = 81.6 ÷ 10 = ₹8.16.
Price = 8.16 × 9.6 = **₹78.3** → equity **₹783 crore**.

### 19.4.3 Goodwill (super-profit)
Net assets (book) 590; normal return 12% → 70.8; maintainable PAT 81.6 → super profit 10.8; capitalised at 14% → goodwill 77.1; value = 590 + 77.1 = **₹667 crore**.

## 19.5 Cash-flow approach (DCF)

**Forecast of FCFF (₹ crore).** NOPAT grows 8%, 8%, 7%, 6%, 6% from 105. The reinvestment each year funds the *next* year's growth at an incremental ROIC of 14% (reinvestment = NOPAT × next-year growth ÷ 0.14).

| | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 | Y4 | Y5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| NOPAT | 113.4 | 122.5 | 131.0 | 138.9 | 147.2 |
| Net reinvestment | (64.8) | (61.2) | (56.2) | (59.5) | (52.6) |
| **FCFF** | **48.6** | **61.2** | **74.9** | **79.4** | **94.7** |
| PVF @ 12% | 0.8929 | 0.7972 | 0.7118 | 0.6355 | 0.5674 |
| PV | 43.4 | 48.8 | 53.3 | 50.5 | 53.7 |

PV of explicit FCFF = **249.7**.

**Terminal value.** Growth 5%; ROIC 13% ⇒ reinvestment rate 5/13 = 38.5%. FCFF₆ = 147.2 × 1.05 × (1 − 0.385) = **95.1**. TV at year 5 = 95.1 ÷ (0.12 − 0.05) = **1,359.2**; PV = 1,359.2 × 0.5674 = **771.2**.

| | ₹ crore |
|---|---:|
| PV of explicit FCFF | 249.7 |
| PV of terminal value | 771.2 |
| **Enterprise value** | **1,020.9** |
| Less: debt | (300.0) |
| Add: non-operating investments | 40.0 |
| **Equity value** | **760.9** |
| **Per share** | **₹76.1** |

TV share of EV = 75.5%.

### Sensitivity (₹ per share)

| WACC \ g | 4% | 5% | 6% |
|---------|---:|---:|---:|
| 11% | 89.5 | 93.8 | 99.4 |
| **12%** | 74.2 | **76.1** | 78.4 |
| 13% | 62.2 | 62.8 | 63.5 |

If terminal ROIC equals WACC (12%) — no excess returns — value per share falls to ₹72.1.

## 19.6 EVA and MVA

Invested capital rises from 850 with the reinvestments: 914.8, 976.0, 1,032.2, 1,091.7, 1,144.3.

| | FY0 | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 | Y4 | Y5 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| NOPAT | 105.0 | 113.4 | 122.5 | 131.0 | 138.9 | 147.2 |
| Opening invested capital | 850.0 | 850.0 | 914.8 | 976.0 | 1,032.2 | 1,091.7 |
| Capital charge @ 12% | 102.0 | 102.0 | 109.8 | 117.1 | 123.9 | 131.0 |
| **EVA** | **3.0** | **11.4** | **12.7** | **13.9** | **15.0** | **16.2** |

- **FY0 EVA = 105 − 0.12 × 850 = ₹3.0 crore** — the firm barely covers its cost of capital today (ROIC 12.35% vs WACC 12%).
- EVA rises as the firm grows at an ROIC above WACC.
- **Value by EVA** = Opening invested capital + PV of future EVA (including terminal) ≈ 850 + 189 = **≈ ₹1,039 crore**, within 2% of the DCF enterprise value (the small gap arises because the terminal EVA assumes a different reinvestment pattern). Equity ≈ 1,039 − 300 + 40 = ₹779 crore (₹77.9 per share).
- **MVA** (at the DCF value) = 1,020.9 − 850 = **₹170.9 crore**: the value the market (or the acquirer) is expected to put on the future EVAs.

*Interpretation.* The business today earns just its cost of capital; **the entire premium over invested capital (₹171 crore) depends on the forecast improvement** — a message the DCF alone does not make as visible.

## 19.7 Relative valuation

EV/EBITDA of peers 5–6×; EBITDA 190.

| Multiple | EV | Less debt | Add investments | Equity | Per share |
|---------:|---:|----------:|----------------:|-------:|----------:|
| 5× | 950 | (300) | 40 | 690 | ₹69.0 |
| 6× | 1,140 | (300) | 40 | 880 | ₹88.0 |

The DCF-implied EV/EBITDA is 1,020.9 ÷ 190 = **5.4×**, inside the peer range — a comfort check.

## 19.8 Putting the methods together

| Method | Equity value (₹ crore) | Per share (₹) | Comment |
|--------|-----------------------:|--------------:|---------|
| Net asset value (book) | 590 | 59 | Lower limit |
| Net realisable value | 720 | 72 | Floor for a seller |
| Replaceable value | 830 | 83 | Upper limit for a buyer of assets |
| Capitalisation of earnings (13–15%) | 544–628 | 54–63 | No growth assumed |
| P/E (adjusted) | 783 | 78 | Peer-based |
| Super-profit (goodwill) | 667 | 67 | Blend of asset and income |
| **DCF** (WACC 11–13%, g 4–6%) | 622–994 | 62–99 | **Central ₹76** |
| EVA-based | 779 | 78 | Consistent with DCF |
| EV/EBITDA 5–6× | 690–880 | 69–88 | Market cross-check |

### 19.8.1 Reading the range
- The **asset-based** floor is ₹59–72 per share; no rational seller would accept less than the NRV of ₹72 *unless* the business loses money (it does not).
- The **market-based** (P/E and EV/EBITDA) and **DCF/EVA** estimates cluster around ₹70–₹80 per share.
- The **capitalisation** method is low because it ignores growth.
- The **replacement cost** (₹83) is the ceiling an asset-buyer would pay.

### 19.8.2 Conclusion (following the three-point approach of Chapter 13)

1. **Valued by many methods:** asset (book, NRV, replacement), income (capitalisation, P/E, goodwill), DCF, EVA, multiples.
2. **Commented on each:** the capitalisation method understates a growing business; the asset values give floor and ceiling; the DCF depends heavily on terminal assumptions (75% of value) and on a WACC of 12%.
3. **Supplementary information:** the firm earns only its cost of capital today (EVA ₹3 crore); the buyer's synergy potential is not included; the land has development potential not captured in the operating forecast.

**Concluded range for 100% of equity: ₹700–₹800 crore (₹70–₹80 per share); point estimate ₹750 crore (₹75 per share).** The seller's minimum is the NRV of ₹720 crore; the buyer's maximum (before synergies) is the DCF of ₹761 crore and the replacement cost of ₹830 crore. The **bargaining zone** is roughly ₹720–₹800 crore.

## 19.9 What the case teaches

1. **No single number**: methods differ by up to 50% (₹54–₹99); the *range* and its reasons matter.
2. **Triangulation** gives confidence: three independent families (asset, income, market) overlap at ₹70–₹80.
3. **DCF and EVA tell the same story** from different angles; EVA reveals the base-year performance.
4. **Normalisation** matters: the base-year PAT (85) and the weighted history (77.6) lead to different capitalised values.
5. **Terminal value and WACC** are the key judgements.

## Chapter 19 summary

- Normalise income and balance sheet (and apply tax) before any method.
- Sunrise Textiles: NAV 590, NRV 720, replacement 830, P/E 783, DCF 761, EVA ~779, EV/EBITDA 690–880.
- A reasoned range (₹700–800 crore) arises from the overlap of the families.
- Always state each method's comments and add supplementary information.
