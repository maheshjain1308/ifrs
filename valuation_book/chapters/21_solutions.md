## Question 1 — Acquisition of XYZ Ltd. by ABC Company

**Data.** XYZ has 1.5 crore shares; market price ₹400 per share. ABC's average cost of capital is 12%. XYZ's expected cash accruals for the next three years (₹ crore): Year 1: 250; Year 2: 300; Year 3: 400. PV factors at 12%: 0.893, 0.797, 0.712. **Calculate the range of valuation ABC has to consider.**

**Technique.** Market value (lower bound) versus DCF value (upper bound) — a range for negotiation.

**Solution.**

*Valuation based on market price:* ₹400 × 1.5 crore = **₹600 crore**.

*Valuation based on DCF:*
(250 × 0.893) + (300 × 0.797) + (400 × 0.712) = 223.25 + 239.10 + 284.80 = **₹747.15 crore**.
Per share = 747.15 ÷ 1.5 = **₹498.10**.

| Range of valuation | Per share (₹) | Total (₹ crore) |
|--------------------|--------------:|----------------:|
| Minimum (market) | 400.00 | 600.00 |
| Maximum (DCF) | 498.10 | 747.15 |

**Commentary.** The market price (₹400) is the sellers' minimum — shareholders will not sell below the market price. The DCF value (₹498.10) is the maximum ABC should pay, given its 12% cost of capital. A control premium would be negotiated within the range. (A purist would also add a terminal value if cash accruals continue beyond year 3; the question gives only three years.)

## Question 2 — Eagle Ltd.

**Data.** Eagle Ltd. reported a profit of ₹77 lakh after 30% tax for 2011-12. The income included **extraordinary items of ₹8 lakh** (income) and an **extraordinary loss of ₹10 lakh**. Existing operations (other than extraordinaries) will continue. A new product launch is expected to give: sales ₹70 lakh; material ₹20 lakh; labour ₹12 lakh; fixed costs ₹10 lakh. Required: (i) value of the business at a capitalisation rate of 14%; (ii) market price per equity share, given share capital of 1,00,000 13% preference shares of ₹100 each and 50,00,000 equity shares of ₹10 each and a P/E ratio of 10.

**Technique.** Normalise profit; add incremental profit; capitalise; deduct preference dividend; apply P/E.

**Solution.**

**(i) Business value**

| | ₹ lakh |
|---|---:|
| Profit before tax = 77 ÷ (1 − 0.30) | 110.00 |
| Less: extraordinary income | (8.00) |
| Add: extraordinary loss | 10.00 |
| **Maintainable PBT from existing operations** | **112.00** |
| Profit from new product: 70 − (20 + 12 + 10) | 28.00 |
| **Total expected PBT** | **140.00** |
| Less: tax @ 30% | (42.00) |
| **Future maintainable profit after tax** | **98.00** |
| Capitalisation rate | 14% |
| **Value of the business = 98 ÷ 0.14** | **₹700 lakh** |

**(ii) Market price per equity share**

| | ₹ |
|---|---:|
| Future maintainable profit after tax | 98,00,000 |
| Less: preference dividend (1,00,000 × ₹100 × 13%) | (13,00,000) |
| **Earnings available to equity shareholders** | **85,00,000** |
| Number of equity shares | 50,00,000 |
| **EPS** | **₹1.70** |
| P/E ratio | 10 |
| **Market price per share** | **₹17** |

**Commentary.** Notice (1) the profit is grossed up to PBT before adjusting the extraordinary items (which are stated pre-tax) and (2) the preference dividend is deducted before computing EPS. Forgetting either is the common error.

## Question 3 — ABC Co.'s new sales strategy

**Data.** Last year: Sales ₹20,000; gross margin 20% (₹4,000); administration, selling and distribution 10% (₹2,000); PBT ₹2,000; tax 30% (₹600); PAT ₹1,400. Balance sheet: fixed assets ₹8,000; current assets ₹4,000; equity ₹12,000.
Under the new strategy sales grow **20% a year for three years**; from the 4th year cash flow stabilises. Gross margin, asset-turnover ratio, capital structure and tax rate remain unchanged. **Depreciation is 10% of net fixed assets at the beginning of the year.** The target rate of return is **15%**. **Determine the incremental value due to adoption of the strategy.**

**Technique.** Projected balance sheet and free cash flows; terminal (residual) value; compare with value without the strategy.

**Solution.**

*Ratios.* Fixed assets = 8,000/20,000 = **40% of sales**; current assets = 4,000/20,000 = **20% of sales**. PBT = 10% of sales; PAT = 70% of PBT.

**Projected balance sheet**

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sales | 24,000 | 28,800 | 34,560 | 34,560 |
| Fixed assets (40%) | 9,600 | 11,520 | 13,824 | 13,824 |
| Current assets (20%) | 4,800 | 5,760 | 6,912 | 6,912 |
| Total assets / Equity | 14,400 | 17,280 | 20,736 | 20,736 |

**Projected cash flows (free cash flow to firm)**

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Sales | 24,000 | 28,800 | 34,560 | 34,560 |
| PBT (10% of sales) | 2,400 | 2,880 | 3,456 | 3,456 |
| PAT (70%) | 1,680 | 2,016 | 2,419.20 | 2,419.20 |
| Add: depreciation (10% of opening net fixed assets) | 800 | 960 | 1,152 | 1,382.4 |
| Less: addition to fixed assets | (2,400) | (2,880) | (3,456) | (1,382.4) |
| Less: increase in current assets | (800) | (960) | (1,152) | – |
| **FCF** | **(720)** | **(864)** | **(1,036.80)** | **2,419.20** |

*Notes:* Year 1 addition to fixed assets = 9,600 − 8,000 = 1,600 net, plus depreciation 800 = 2,400 gross. Year 4: sales constant, so gross investment equals depreciation (1,382.4) to maintain 13,824 of net fixed assets; no increase in current assets.

**Present value (15%)**

| Year | FCF | PVF @ 15% | PV |
|-----:|----:|----------:|---:|
| 1 | (720) | 0.870 | (626.40) |
| 2 | (864) | 0.756 | (653.18) |
| 3 | (1,036.80) | 0.658 | (682.21) |
| | | | **(1,961.79)** |

**Residual value** (from year 4, a level perpetuity): 2,419.20 ÷ 0.15 = **16,128**. Present value = 16,128 ÷ (1.15)³ = 16,128 ÷ 1.521 = **10,603.55**.

**Value with the strategy** = 10,603.55 − 1,961.79 = **₹8,641.76**.

**Value without the strategy** (PAT 1,400 perpetual) = 1,400 ÷ 0.15 = **₹9,333.33**.

**Incremental value** = 8,641.76 − 9,333.33 = **−₹691.57**.

**Conclusion.** The strategy is **not financially viable**: it destroys ₹691.57 of value. The reason: sales grow 20% but the business must invest heavily in fixed and current assets (about 60% of incremental sales), generating negative free cash flow for three years; the stable-year cash flow (2,419) is only 73% above the current PAT, not enough to compensate. Total assets must rise from ₹12,000 to ₹20,736 (an increase of ₹8,736) before the PAT of 1,400 grows to 2,419.

## Question 4 — H Ltd. and B Ltd. (exchange ratio)

**Data.** H Ltd. buys the business of B Ltd. effective 1 April 2012. Balance sheets at 31 March 2012 (₹ crore):

| Liabilities | H Ltd. | B Ltd. | Assets | H Ltd. | B Ltd. |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| Equity shares of ₹100 each | 350.00 | – | Net fixed assets | 220.00 | 0.50 |
| Equity shares of ₹10 each | – | 6.50 | Net current assets | 1,020.00 | 29.00 |
| Reserves and surplus | 950.00 | 25.00 | Deferred tax assets | 60.00 | 2.00 |
| **Total** | **1,300.00** | **31.50** | **Total** | **1,300.00** | **31.50** |

Information: (1) weighted-average post-tax maintainable profits of the last four years: H ₹300 crore; B ₹10 crore. (2) Both envisage a capitalisation rate of 8%. (3) H has a **contingent liability of ₹300 crore** as at 31 March 2012. (4) H will issue ₹100 shares to B's shareholders in terms of the exchange ratio on a **fair value basis**, with **weights of 1 and 3** for the values on net-asset basis and earnings-capitalisation basis for both companies.
Required: value of shares under (i) net asset method, (ii) earnings capitalisation method, (iii) exchange ratio on a fair-value basis.

**Solution.**

*Number of shares:* H: 350 ÷ 100 = **3.50 crore**. B: 6.50 ÷ 10 = **0.65 crore**.

**(i) Net asset value per share**

| | Calculation | ₹ per share |
|---|---|---:|
| H Ltd. | (1,300 − 300 contingent liability) ÷ 3.50 | **285.71** |
| B Ltd. | 31.50 ÷ 0.65 | **48.46** |

*Assumption:* the contingent liability of H will materialise at its full amount (stated in the answer).

**(ii) Earnings-capitalisation value per share**

| | Calculation | ₹ per share |
|---|---|---:|
| H Ltd. | 300 ÷ 0.08 = 3,750 ÷ 3.50 | **1,071.43** |
| B Ltd. | 10 ÷ 0.08 = 125 ÷ 0.65 | **192.31** |

**(iii) Fair value (weights 1 : 3)**

| | Calculation | ₹ per share |
|---|---|---:|
| H Ltd. | (285.71 × 1 + 1,071.43 × 3) ÷ 4 | **875.00** |
| B Ltd. | (48.46 × 1 + 192.31 × 3) ÷ 4 | **156.35** |

**Exchange ratio** = 156.35 ÷ 875 = **0.1787**. H Ltd. should issue **0.1787 share for each share of B Ltd.** (Each ₹100-face H share is valued at ₹875.)

## Question 5 — AB Ltd. and XY Ltd.

**Data.** AB Ltd. plans to acquire and absorb the running business of XY Ltd., consideration in equity shares of AB. As at 31.3.2006 AB's paid-up capital: 80 lakh shares of ₹10; highest and lowest quotations in the last six months ₹570 and ₹430; the **price per share is the average of high and low**.
XY's balance sheet at 31.3.2006 (₹ lakh): Sources: Share capital — 20 lakh equity shares of ₹10 fully paid 200; 10 lakh equity shares of ₹10, ₹5 paid 50; Loans 100; total 350. Uses: Fixed assets (net) 150; net current assets 200; total 350.
Merchant bankers' cash-flow estimates (after-tax earnings for equity, ₹ lakh): year ended 31.3.07: 105; 31.3.08: 120; 31.3.09: 125; 31.3.10: 120; 31.3.11: 100, plus a **terminal value estimate of 200**. The business of XY may be valued on the **average of (i) aggregate discounted cash flows at 8% and (ii) net assets value**. PV factors at 8% for years 1–5: 0.93, 0.86, 0.79, 0.74, 0.68. Required: (i) total value; (ii) number of shares to be issued by AB; (iii) basis of allocation among XY's shareholders.

**Solution.**

*AB's share price* = (570 + 430) ÷ 2 = **₹500**.

| Value of XY Ltd. based on future cash flows (₹ lakh) | |
|---|---:|
| 105 × 0.93 | 97.65 |
| 120 × 0.86 | 103.20 |
| 125 × 0.79 | 98.75 |
| 120 × 0.74 | 88.80 |
| (100 + 200) × 0.68 | 204.00 |
| **Value on discounted cash flows** | **592.40** |
| **Value on net assets** (350 − 100 loans = share capital paid-up 250) | **250.00** |
| **Average value** (592.40 + 250) ÷ 2 | **421.20** |

**Number of shares to be issued** = ₹4,21,20,000 ÷ ₹500 = **84,240 shares**.

**Basis of allocation.** Convert partly paid shares to fully-paid equivalents: 20 lakh + (10 lakh × 5/10) = **25 lakh** equivalent shares.
- Fully paid shareholders: 84,240 × 20/25 = **67,392 shares**.
- Partly paid shareholders: 84,240 − 67,392 = **16,848 shares**.

**Commentary.** Using the merchant bankers' recommended weighting (equal weight to DCF and NAV) the value is 421.2 lakh; allocation recognises that ₹5-paid shares carry half the economic interest of fully paid shares.

## Question 6 — Hansel Ltd. (book vs market weights)

**Data.** Expected free cash flow ₹54 lakh next year; expected growth 9%; analyst's value ₹1,800 lakh, but he used **book values** of debt and equity. Cost of equity 20%; post-tax cost of debt 10%. The **value of equity is thrice its book value** and the **market value of debt is nine-tenths of its book value**. Compute the correct value.

**Technique.** Infer the WACC used, back out the book weights, convert to market weights, recompute.

**Solution.** V₀ = FCFF₁ ÷ (Kc − g). 1,800 = 54 ÷ (Kc − 0.09) → Kc − 0.09 = 0.03 → **Kc = 12%** (the WACC the analyst used).

Let X be the weight of debt in *book* terms: 20% × (1 − X) + 10% × X = 12% → 20 − 10X = 12 → X = 0.80. Book weights: **debt 80%, equity 20%**. Take book value of capital = 100 ⇒ debt 80, equity 20.

Market values: equity = 3 × 20 = **60**; debt = 0.9 × 80 = **72**. Total = 132.
Market weights: equity 60/132 = 45.45%; debt 72/132 = 54.55%.

**Correct WACC** = 20% × 60/132 + 10% × 72/132 = 9.0909% + 5.4545% = **14.5455%**.

**Correct value** = 54 ÷ (0.145455 − 0.09) = 54 ÷ 0.055455 = **₹973.7 lakh** (the source rounds to ₹974.73 lakh using WACC 14.5455% → 54 ÷ 0.0554 = 974.7). The analyst overstated value by about ₹826 lakh because the book weights gave too much weight to cheap debt.

## Question 7 — XYZ Ltd. (two-stage DCF)

**Data.** XYZ Ltd. grows at a higher rate for 4 years, then stabilises. Base year: revenue ₹2,000 crore; EBIT ₹300 crore; capital expenditure ₹280 crore; depreciation ₹200 crore.

| | High growth (4 years) | Stable growth |
|---|---|---|
| Growth in revenue and EBIT | 20% | 10% |
| Growth in capex and depreciation | 20% | Capex offset by depreciation |
| Risk-free rate | 10% | 9% |
| Equity beta | 1.15 | 1 |
| Market risk premium | 6% | 5% |
| Pre-tax cost of debt | 13% | 12.86% |
| Debt : equity | 1 : 1 | 2 : 3 |

Working capital is 25% of revenue throughout; corporate tax 30%. **What is the value of the firm?**

**Solution.**

**Cost of capital**

*High growth:* Ke = 10% + 1.15 × 6% = **16.9%**; Kd = 13% × 0.7 = **9.1%**; weights 50:50 → WACC = 0.5 × 16.9 + 0.5 × 9.1 = **13%**.
*Stable growth:* Ke = 9% + 1.0 × 5% = **14%**; Kd = 12.86% × 0.7 = **9.0%**; D/E 2:3 → debt 40%, equity 60% → WACC = 0.6 × 14 + 0.4 × 9 = **12%**.

**Forecast FCFF (₹ crore)**

| | Yr 1 | Yr 2 | Yr 3 | Yr 4 | Terminal year |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 2,400.00 | 2,880.00 | 3,456.00 | 4,147.20 | 4,561.92 |
| EBIT | 360.00 | 432.00 | 518.40 | 622.08 | 684.29 |
| EAT (EBIT × 0.7) | 252.00 | 302.40 | 362.88 | 435.46 | 479.00 |
| Capital expenditure | 336.00 | 403.20 | 483.84 | 580.61 | 456.19 |
| Less: depreciation | (240.00) | (288.00) | (345.60) | (414.72) | (456.19) |
| Net capex | 96.00 | 115.20 | 138.24 | 165.89 | – |
| Δ Working capital (25% of Δ revenue) | 100.00 | 120.00 | 144.00 | 172.80 | 103.68 |
| **FCF = EAT − net capex − ΔWC** | **56.00** | **67.20** | **80.64** | **96.77** | **375.32** |

*(Capex and depreciation grow 20% a year from 280 and 200: 336/240 in year 1, etc. In the terminal year capex equals depreciation.)*

**Present value of explicit FCFs @ 13%**

| FCFF | PVF | PV |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 56.00 | 0.885 | 49.56 |
| 67.20 | 0.783 | 52.62 |
| 80.64 | 0.693 | 55.88 |
| 96.77 | 0.613 | 59.32 |
| | | **217.38** |

**Terminal value** (stable growth 10%; WACC 12%): TV₄ = 375.32 ÷ (0.12 − 0.10) = **18,766 crore**. PV = 18,766 × 0.613 = **₹11,503.56 crore**.

**Value of the firm** = 217.38 + 11,503.56 = **₹11,720.94 crore**.

**Commentary.** (1) The stable-growth WACC (12%) is *lower* than the high-growth WACC (13%) because beta falls to 1 and the debt ratio and cost of debt change. (2) The terminal value is **98%** of the total; WACC − g = 2% makes the result hypersensitive (a 1% rise in the stable WACC to 13% would cut TV to ₹12,510 crore × 0.613 ≈ 7,669 → total 7,886: a 33% fall). (3) Note the discounting of TV by the year-4 factor at the *high-growth* WACC, as the source does; in theory the stable-phase rate applies only from year 5, and the TV already embeds it.

## Question 8 — WXY Ltd. (FCFF and WACC)

**Data.** WXY Ltd. grows at **20% a year for three years**, then 8% in perpetuity. Year ended 31 March 2014 (₹ crore): revenue 7,500; COGS 3,000; operating expenses 2,250; capital expenditure 750; depreciation (included in operating expenses) 600. During the high-growth period revenues and EBIT grow at 20% and **capex net of depreciation grows at 15%**; from year 4 revenues and EBIT grow at 8%, incremental capex is offset by depreciation; net working capital is **25% of revenue**; **WACC 15%**; tax 30%. PVIF at 15%: 0.8696, 0.7561, 0.6575. **Estimate the value using FCFF and WACC.**

**Solution.**

*Base year:* EBIT = 7,500 − 3,000 − 2,250 = **2,250** (depreciation is already in operating expenses). Operating expenses excluding depreciation = 2,250 − 600 = 1,650; depreciation 600; net capex = 750 − 600 = **150**.

| ₹ crore | Yr 1 | Yr 2 | Yr 3 | Terminal year |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 9,000.00 | 10,800.00 | 12,960.00 | 13,996.80 |
| COGS | 3,600.00 | 4,320.00 | 5,184.00 | 5,598.72 |
| Operating expenses (excl. depreciation) | 1,980.00 | 2,376.00 | 2,851.20 | 3,079.30 |
| Depreciation | 720.00 | 864.00 | 1,036.80 | 1,119.74 |
| **EBIT** | **2,700.00** | **3,240.00** | **3,888.00** | **4,199.04** |
| Tax @ 30% | 810.00 | 972.00 | 1,166.40 | 1,259.71 |
| **EAT** | **1,890.00** | **2,268.00** | **2,721.60** | **2,939.33** |
| Net capex (capex − depreciation; +15% a year from 150) | 172.50 | 198.38 | 228.13 | – |
| Δ Working capital (25% of Δ revenue) | 375.00 | 450.00 | 540.00 | 259.20 |
| **FCF** | **1,342.50** | **1,619.62** | **1,953.47** | **2,680.13** |

**PV of explicit FCF @ 15%**

| FCF | PVIF | PV |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 1,342.50 | 0.8696 | 1,167.44 |
| 1,619.62 | 0.7561 | 1,224.59 |
| 1,953.47 | 0.6575 | 1,284.41 |
| | | **3,676.44** |

**Terminal value:** 2,680.13 ÷ (0.15 − 0.08) = **38,287.57**; PV = 38,287.57 × 0.6575 = **25,174.08**.

**Value of the firm** = 3,676.44 + 25,174.08 = **₹28,850.52 crore.**

**Commentary.** In the terminal year, operating expenses (excluding depreciation) grow by 8% like revenue; EBIT = revenue growth × EBIT (8%). The terminal-year working-capital investment is 25% × (13,996.80 − 12,960) = 259.20. The terminal value is 87% of the total.

## Question 9 — Jatayu Limited (EVA)

**Data.** Equity capital ₹160 lakh; reserves and surplus ₹140 lakh; 10% debentures ₹400 lakh. Cost of equity 14%; **financial leverage 1.5 times**; tax rate 30%. Compute EVA.

**Solution.**
*Interest* = 10% × 400 = **₹40 lakh**.
*Financial leverage* = PBIT ÷ PBT: 1.5 = PBIT ÷ (PBIT − 40) → 1.5 PBIT − 60 = PBIT → 0.5 PBIT = 60 → **PBIT = ₹120 lakh**.
*NOPAT* = 120 × (1 − 0.30) = **₹84 lakh**.
*Capital employed* = 160 + 140 + 400 = **₹700 lakh**.
*WACC* = 14% × (300/700) + 10% × (1 − 0.30) × (400/700) = 6.0% + 4.0% = **10%**.
*EVA* = 84 − 10% × 700 = **₹14 lakh**.

## Question 10 — RST Ltd.

**Data.** Net income after tax ₹25,00,000; tax 30%. Debt (coupon 11%) ₹40 lakh; equity (share capital + reserves) ₹125 lakh; invested capital ₹165 lakh. Equity beta 1.36; risk-free 8.5%; market risk premium 9%. Required: (i) WACC; (ii) EVA.

**Solution.**
*Cost of equity* = 8.5% + 1.36 × 9% = **20.74%**. *Post-tax Kd* = 11% × 0.7 = **7.70%**.
*WACC* = 20.74% × 125/165 + 7.70% × 40/165 = 15.71% + 1.87% = **17.58%**.
*Taxable income* = 25,00,000 ÷ 0.70 = **₹35,71,429**. *Interest* = 11% × 40,00,000 = **₹4,40,000**. *EBIT* = **₹40,11,429**.
*NOPAT* = 40,11,429 × 0.70 = **₹28,08,000**.
*Capital charge* = 17.58% × 1,65,00,000 = **₹29,00,700**.
*EVA* = 28,08,000 − 29,00,700 = **−₹92,700**.

**Interpretation.** Negative EVA: the company earns less than its cost of capital, even though it reports a profit of ₹25 lakh.

## Question 11 — Tender Ltd.

**Data.** Net profit ₹15 lakh after 30% tax; interest ₹10 lakh; invested capital ₹95 lakh of which 55% is debt; WACC 13%. (a) Operating income; (b) EVA; (c) 6 lakh equity shares: how much dividend can be paid before the value of the entity starts declining?

**Solution.**
(a) Taxable income = 15 ÷ 0.70 = **₹21.43 lakh**; **operating income (EBIT)** = 21.43 + 10 = **₹31.43 lakh**.
(b) EVA = EBIT(1 − t) − WACC × invested capital = 31.43 × 0.70 − 13% × 95 = 22.00 − 12.35 = **₹9.65 lakh**.
(c) **EVA dividend** = EVA ÷ shares = ₹9,65,000 ÷ 6,00,000 = **₹1.6083 per share**. Dividends up to the EVA do not reduce value: beyond that the company would be distributing capital rather than surplus.

## Question 12 — Orange, Grape and Apple

**Data.** Identical companies except capital structure; invested capital ₹1,00,000 each; tax 35%.

| | Orange | Grape | Apple |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Debt/assets | 0.8 | 0.5 | 0.2 |
| Shares outstanding | 6,100 | 8,300 | 10,000 |
| Pre-tax cost of debt | 16% | 13% | 15% |
| Cost of equity | 26% | 22% | 20% |
| EBIT | 25,000 | 25,000 | 25,000 |

Required: (i) WACC; (ii) EVA; (iii) the best investment by EVA; (iv) price per share at industry P/E 11; (v) estimated market capitalisation.

**Solution.**

**(i) WACC** (post-tax Kd: 16% × 0.65 = 10.40%; 13% × 0.65 = 8.45%; 15% × 0.65 = 9.75%)

| | Debt | Equity | WACC |
|---|---:|---:|------|
| Orange | 80,000 | 20,000 | 10.4% × 0.8 + 26% × 0.2 = **13.52%** |
| Grape | 50,000 | 50,000 | 8.45% × 0.5 + 22% × 0.5 = **15.225%** |
| Apple | 20,000 | 80,000 | 9.75% × 0.2 + 20% × 0.8 = **17.95%** |

**(ii) EVA** = EBIT(1 − t) − WACC × IC = 16,250 − WACC × 1,00,000:
Orange: 16,250 − 13,520 = **2,730**; Grape: 16,250 − 15,225 = **1,025**; Apple: 16,250 − 17,950 = **−1,700**.

**(iii)** **Orange** is the best investment: highest EVA and lowest WACC.

**(iv) Price per share**

| | Orange | Grape | Apple |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| EBIT | 25,000 | 25,000 | 25,000 |
| Interest (debt × Kd) | 12,800 | 6,500 | 3,000 |
| Taxable income | 12,200 | 18,500 | 22,000 |
| Tax @ 35% | 4,270 | 6,475 | 7,700 |
| Net income | 7,930 | 12,025 | 14,300 |
| Shares | 6,100 | 8,300 | 10,000 |
| EPS | ₹1.30 | ₹1.45 | ₹1.43 |
| **Price @ P/E 11** | **₹14.30** | **₹15.95** | **₹15.73** |

**(v) Market capitalisation:** Orange 14.30 × 6,100 = **₹87,230**; Grape 15.95 × 8,300 = **₹1,32,385**; Apple 15.73 × 10,000 = **₹1,57,300**.

**Commentary.** EVA ranks Orange first, yet the P/E-based price ranks it *last*. The P/E of 11 is applied uniformly although the three have different financial risk: Orange (80% debt) deserves a **lower** P/E. This is why P/E must be adjusted for leverage; EVA, which charges for the capital actually used, is not misled.

## Question 13 — Delta Ltd.

**Data.** Net income ₹15,00,000; marginal tax rate 40%; interest expense ₹15,00,000; invested capital ₹1,00,00,000 of which 60% is debt; WACC 12.6%. Delta has 2,50,000 shares. (i) EBIT; (ii) EVA; (iii) dividend per share before the value starts to fall; what if no dividend is paid?

**Solution.**
(i) Taxable income = 15,00,000 ÷ (1 − 0.40) = **₹25,00,000**; EBIT = 25,00,000 + 15,00,000 = **₹40,00,000**.
(ii) EVA = 40,00,000 × 0.60 − 0.126 × 1,00,00,000 = 24,00,000 − 12,60,000 = **₹11,40,000**.
(iii) EVA dividend = 11,40,000 ÷ 2,50,000 = **₹4.56** per share. If Delta pays no dividend, the retained EVA is reinvested, growth and EBIT rise, and **the value of the firm is expected to increase**.

## Question 14 — XYZ Inc. (software consultancy)

**Data (31 Dec 2010, $ million).** Income from consultancy 935; EBIT 180; interest 18; EBT 162; tax @ 35% 56.70; PAT 105.30. Balance sheet: equity stock (10 million shares at $10) 100; reserves 325; loans 180; current liabilities 180 (total 785); land and building 200; computers and software 295; current assets (debtors 150, bank 100, cash 40) 290. WACC 12%; share price $50. Compute (a) EVA and (b) MVA.

**Solution.**
*NOPAT* = 180 × (1 − 0.35) = **117.00**.
*Capital employed* = equity 100 + reserves 325 + loans 180 = **605**. *Capital charge* = 605 × 12% = **72.60**.
**(a) EVA = 117.00 − 72.60 = $44.40 million.**
*Market value of equity* = $50 × 10 million = **$500 million**; *equity funds (book)* = 100 + 325 = **425**.
**(b) MVA = 500 − 425 = $75 million.**

## Question 15 — Herbal Gyan

**Data.** A beauty-cosmetics company; earnings average ₹12 lakh after tax, largely from a **patented** beauty cream. The patent has eight years to run and the company has been **offered ₹40 lakh** for it. Assets include ₹20 lakh working capital and ₹80 lakh property, plant and equipment; the patent is **not shown** in the books. Cost of capital 15%. **What is its EVA?**

**Solution.** Include the patent at its market value in the investment base:

| Total investment | ₹ lakh |
|---|---:|
| Working capital | 20 |
| Property, plant and equipment | 80 |
| Patent rights | 40 |
| **Total** | **140** |

EVA = income − (cost of capital × total investment) = 12 − (0.15 × 140) = 12 − 21 = **−₹9 lakh**.

**Interpretation.** Herbal Gyan has a negative EVA: it would earn more by selling the patent for ₹40 lakh and investing the proceeds at 15%. The *opportunity cost* of the unrecorded patent must be charged.

## Question 16 — Constant Engineering Ltd.

**Data.** A high-tech product that reduces carbon emission has been patented; market value ₹100 crore, **not recorded** in the books. Net worth ₹200 crore; long-term debt ₹400 crore. The product generates **net operating profit after tax of ₹84 crore**. The 365-day government bond rate is 10%; the market portfolio generates 12%; the stock moves in tandem with the market (**β = 1**). Calculate EVA.

**Solution.**
*Cost of equity* (CAPM) = 10% + 1 × (12% − 10%) = **12%**. *Cost of debt:* the question treats debt cost as 10% (government bond/long-term rate) as in the model solution.
*Invested capital* = net worth 200 + long-term debt 400 + patent 100 = **₹700 crore**.
*Weights:* equity = (200 + 100)/700 = 300/700; debt = 400/700.
*WACC* = 12% × 300/700 + 10% × 400/700 = 5.14% + 5.71% = **10.85%**.
*EVA* = 84 − 10.85% × 700 = 84 − 75.95 = **₹8.05 crore**.
