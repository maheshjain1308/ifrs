# Preface {.unnumbered}

This book is a study guide to **business valuation**. It follows, section by section, the syllabus of the chapter on Business Valuation in the Advanced Financial Management paper of the Institute of Chartered Accountants of India (ICAI): the conceptual framework of valuation, the approaches and methods (asset-based, earnings-based and cash-flow-based), the measurement of the cost of equity, relative valuation, other approaches (LBO, chop-shop, EVA, MVA and SVA), fair value, going-concern and non-going-concern valuation, and the valuation of distressed companies, start-ups, digital platforms, professional firms and the impact of ESG.

It is an independent study aid. It is **not** an official ICAI publication and it does not replace the Institute's study material or the latest amendments to the syllabus; the structure of the source chapter is used as a map, and the explanations, derivations, extra examples and exercises are original to this book.

## What this book adds to a syllabus chapter

A syllabus chapter must be compact. A student who meets a formula in two lines, or a numerical illustration without its derivation, often memorises rather than understands. This guide therefore:

1. **Explains the reasoning** behind each method — why it exists, what it assumes, when it fails.
2. **Re-derives every worked illustration** from the source chapter step by step, and checks the arithmetic. Where the original compresses a step (for example, in its illustrative DCF the line labelled "terminal value" is already a present value), the book spells the step out and shows the alternative presentation as well. Knowing exactly what each line represents is part of mastering the method.
3. **Solves every practical question** in the source chapter in full (Chapter 21), with commentary on the technique being tested.
4. **Integrates the methods** in one running case (Chapter 19): a single company valued by the asset, income, DCF, EVA and market approaches, with reconciliation of the answers into a range.
5. **Supplies new practice problems** with solutions (Chapter 22 and Appendix D), a self-test bank (Appendix E) and guidance on how to answer theory questions (Chapter 23).
6. **Extends the syllabus** with supplementary chapters on securities, M&A, cash-flow construction, intangibles, the Indian context and valuation reports (Chapters 24–30).
7. **Adds reference material**: a formula sheet, glossary, discount-factor tables and an index.

## A note on numbers

Amounts follow the source illustrations (₹ lakh, ₹ crore, ₹ thousand, or foreign currency as the question states). Figures are rounded for readability; recomputation may differ in the last decimal. Companies in the new examples are fictional. Market inputs (rates, premiums, multiples) are illustrations, not forecasts. Where the book refers to law, standards or regulations, the references are for orientation: **check the current text** before relying on them in an examination or engagement.

# How to Use This Book {.unnumbered}

**For first reading.** Read Parts I–III in order; they are the foundation. Work each chapter's "Check your understanding" questions before turning to the answers at the end of the chapter.

**For examination revision.** Use the *Exam focus* box at the end of each chapter (what examiners typically test, and the traps), the formula sheet in Appendix A, Chapters 21–23 for practice, and the chapter summaries. Examination questions on this topic are mostly of five kinds: (i) compute value by a named method (NAV, capitalisation of earnings, DCF); (ii) compute cost of equity/WACC with un-levering and re-levering; (iii) compute EVA/MVA; (iv) compute an exchange ratio for a merger; (v) write short notes on a theory topic (distressed firms, start-ups, ESG, going vs non-going concern).

**For practitioners.** The Pitfall boxes and Chapters 13, 14, 19 and 23 are the most relevant.

**Conventions.** Formulas are in **bold**. *Key idea* boxes state the central point. *Pitfall* boxes flag common errors. *Worked example* boxes give fully solved numbers. *Exam focus* boxes list what to remember for the paper.
