14. In the chop-shop method one uses the capitalisation ratios of the target company itself. *(Ch. 10)*
15. EVA is computed after deducting a charge for both debt and equity capital. *(Ch. 11)*

19. For a distressed firm, P/E is the best multiple. *(Ch. 14)*

## Part 2 — Short numerical questions

31. PV of ₹2,000 receivable in 2 years at 10%.
32. Value: profit ₹60 crore; capitalisation rate 12%.
33. Price: EPS ₹7; P/E 11 adjusted 20% for non-listing.
34. Unlevered beta 0.9; D/E 40%; tax 25%; levered beta?
35. WACC: Ke 15%, Kd 8% pre-tax, tax 25%, D/V 25%.
36. NOPAT 200; invested capital 1,000; WACC 12%; EVA?
37. Market value 1,800; invested capital 1,400; MVA?
38. Equity: DCF 400; distress 50; P = 20%.
39. VC: exit 120 in 4 years; investor wants 6×; investment 10. Pre-money?
40. EV: market cap 700; debt 200; cash 90.

## Part 2 answers

31. 2,000 ÷ 1.21 = **₹1,652.9**. 32. 60 ÷ 0.12 = **₹500 crore**. 33. 7 × 11 × 0.8 = **₹61.6**. 34. 0.9 × (1 + 0.75 × 0.4) = **1.17**. 35. 0.75 × 15 + 0.25 × 8 × 0.75 = 11.25 + 1.5 = **12.75%**. 36. 200 − 120 = **₹80**. 37. **₹400**. 38. 320 + 10 = **₹330**. 39. Post = 120 ÷ 6 = 20; pre = **₹10**. 40. 700 + 200 − 90 = **₹810**.

## Part 3 — Short answers

41. Name the three core approaches to valuation.
42. State two limitations of the asset-based approach.
43. What are the steps for valuing an unlisted company by P/E?
44. Why is the terminal value so important in DCF?
45. Differentiate asset beta and equity beta.
46. Write one reason why the chop-shop method may undervalue or overvalue a firm.
47. What does a negative EVA signify?
48. List the four methods for valuing distressed companies.
49. Name three value drivers for start-ups.
50. How is ESG incorporated into cash flows for E, S and G?

## Part 3 answers

41. Asset-based, earnings-based (income) and cash-flow-based (DCF). 42. Ignores earning power and future cash flows; uses historical cost; omits intangibles. 43. Choose the equivalent quoted company's P/E; adjust downward for non-listing; determine maintainable EPS; multiply. 44. It represents the value of all cash flows beyond the explicit period and is often 70–90% of the value; sensitive to growth and discount rate. 45. Asset beta measures only business risk (no leverage); equity beta adds financial risk and is higher for levered firms. 46. Segment peers may not be comparable, allocation of costs is arbitrary, and separation costs/synergy losses are ignored. 47. The firm earns less than its cost of capital; it destroys value. 48. Modified DCF; DCF plus distress value; adjusted present value; relative valuation. 49. Any three of product, management, traction, revenue, industry attractiveness, demand–supply, competitiveness. 50. E: 2-degree scenario analysis and carbon points; S: cost of social measures on revenue (labour conditions, CSR); G: penalties, fines and taxes from poor governance.
