## The Situation

On the morning of Monday, 2 November 2026, Corvane Industrial Holdings launched an unsolicited, all-cash tender offer for every outstanding share of Brightwell Instruments at $32.00 per share. The offer arrived three weeks after the Brightwell board had unanimously declined a private proposal at the same price, delivered by letter on 12 October. The tender is scheduled to expire at 5:00 p.m. on Tuesday, 1 December 2026, unless extended. The Brightwell board has scheduled a special meeting for Monday, 9 November 2026, at which it must adopt a formal recommendation to shareholders. Securities rules in the relevant market require that recommendation, or a statement that the board is unable to take a position, to be filed within ten business days of launch. Today is Wednesday, 4 November 2026.

Brightwell Instruments is a mid-cap designer and manufacturer of precision measurement instruments: laser interferometers, high-resolution torque and force sensors, and the calibration software and recurring service contracts that surround them. Henrik Sandoval, grandson of the 1958 founder, has been chief executive since 2014. Revenue for fiscal 2026, which ends on 31 December, is estimated at $1,200 million. Reported EBITDA is estimated at $240 million, a 20.0 percent margin. Roughly 31 percent of revenue comes from the Calibration Services division, a recurring-revenue business with 78 percent customer retention in its largest contracts; the remainder comes from instrument sales to aerospace, semiconductor equipment, medical device and energy customers. The company has 80.0 million basic shares outstanding, 3.0 million employee stock options with a weighted-average strike price of $18.00, and 1.0 million unvested restricted stock units that the plan documents say will vest in full on a change of control.

Until 2 October 2026, which analysts now call the "undisturbed date," Brightwell shares traded at $25.00. On that date Corvane voluntarily announced that it had accumulated 4.9 percent of the shares at an average cost of $24.10, a stake just under the 5 percent level at which disclosure would have been mandatory and well under the 15 percent trigger of the shareholder rights plan the Brightwell board adopted in 2022. The shares gained 9 percent on the disclosure, then moved to $29.80 on the private proposal leak, and closed at $32.70 on Tuesday, 3 November, the day after the tender launched. A price above the offer is the market's way of saying that it expects a higher bid, a rival, or both.

The people at the table are these.

- **Margaret Oyelaran**, independent chair of Brightwell and a former chief financial officer of a listed engineering group, known for deliberate, documented process.
- **Henrik Sandoval**, chief executive; he and his family trust own 6.5 percent of the shares. His change-of-control agreement is worth $9.6 million in cash plus accelerated equity, which makes him an interested party in the board's debate.
- **Priya Raman**, chief financial officer, who produced the management plan and privately doubts that its last two years are achievable.
- **Thomas Achterberg**, lead independent director and audit chair, who would chair a special committee.
- **Calloway Hart**, Brightwell's financial adviser, paid a fixed fee plus a success fee only if a transaction closes, a common structure and a potential source of bias.
- **Larkspur Capital**, a long-only fund that owns 9.8 percent, bought mostly at about $19, and has said it will "carefully scrutinize any decision to reject a premium of this size."
- **Rolf Vandermeer**, chief executive of Corvane, a diversified industrial group about three and a half times Brightwell's size by enterprise value.
- **Lena Koskinen**, chief executive of Altair Measurement Group, a larger listed instrument group active in adjacent rather than overlapping product categories, who telephoned Ms. Oyelaran on 30 October to say Altair "would be interested in a conversation about a combination on friendlier terms."

The decision is not simply "accept or reject." The board can recommend acceptance; reject and hold; reject and seek a higher price from Corvane; seek a white knight; or reject and put a stand-alone alternative in place, such as a leveraged recapitalization. It must also decide what to do about the rights plan, whether to open the books to Altair, whether to approach Corvane, and what to tell employees, customers and regulators.

You are an analyst at Calloway Hart. Ms. Oyelaran wants a written analysis, in order, of: what the bid is worth and what premium it carries; what Brightwell is worth on its own under different plans; what Corvane can afford to pay; what each defense does to value and to the bidder; how a white knight compares once certainty and timing are included; and what the board's process should look like.

## Industry Context

Precision measurement is a quietly attractive corner of industrial technology. A laser interferometer that calibrates machine tools in an aerospace plant must be traceable to a recognized standard, supported by service engineers who can attend within a day, and accepted by quality auditors who have approved a specific vendor's process. Switching vendors means re-qualification, weeks of lost production and a documentary burden. That is the source of pricing power and of the recurring service and software revenue, and it is why peers trade at higher multiples than general industrials. Five features matter for the case.

**Consolidation pressure.** Customers are consolidating and want one vendor for sensors, software and service. Larger groups can bundle; smaller ones cannot. Independent mid-sized instrument makers are scarce assets, and scarcity supports control premiums. The six precedents in Table 13.8 carried a median premium of 32 percent and a median EV/EBITDA of about 12.1 times.

**Cyclicality at the edges.** Service and software revenue is stable, but instrument sales depend on aerospace, semiconductor-equipment and energy capital spending. Management argues the mix is shifting toward recurring revenue; the adviser agrees it is real but slow. A semiconductor-equipment slowdown in late 2025 cut instrument bookings by 11 percent in one quarter before they recovered.

**Regulatory attention.** Corvane has an interferometry business, acquired in 2019, that overlaps with part of Brightwell's laser line. Altair's portfolio is adjacent rather than overlapping, and its counsel expects a shorter review. These facts feed the certainty-adjusted comparison of offers.

**Technology and capital intensity.** Optical-comb and chip-scale sensing could erode premium interferometer positioning over a decade; Brightwell spends 7.5 percent of revenue on research and development. Capital expenditure runs at 4.5 percent of revenue against depreciation of 4.0 percent, and working capital absorbs about 15 percent of incremental revenue, so cash conversion from EBITDA is good.

**The takeover environment.** In the market where both companies are listed, boards have wide latitude to adopt a rights plan against an unsolicited offer, but must be able to show a reasonable basis for perceiving a threat, a proportionate response and no mere entrenchment. Shareholders can replace directors, and Brightwell's board is staggered into three classes of which one is elected each year. The case supplies the financial analysis any legal standard will depend upon; it does not set out the law.

Voting power now sits largely with institutions, passive funds and activists, who respond quickly to premium offers, and merger-arbitrage funds quickly set the price after a bid. Directors cannot count on a long quiet period, and the post-bid price is not an unbiased estimate of stand-alone value: it blends the probabilities of a deal, a bump, a counterbid and failure. Part of the assignment is to decode what $32.70 is saying.

## Complications and Exhibits

These exhibits arrived in the 72 hours after the tender launched. Some are clean facts; some are ambiguous. Part of the exercise is deciding which belong in the analysis.

**Exhibit A: Process history.** The chief executives met at a conference on 14 September. On 2 October Corvane announced its 4.9 percent stake, and on 12 October it delivered a written proposal at $32.00. The board met on 15 October with Calloway Hart present but without a formal valuation, and declined by letter on 16 October. It formed no special committee and requested no written opinion. The tender launched on 2 November. One director has asked whether the October rejection was adequately informed.

**Exhibit B: Executive change-of-control payments.** Mr. Sandoval's agreement pays $9.6 million in cash if he leaves within 24 months of a change of control; Ms. Raman is entitled to $3.1 million; four other officers share $6.1 million. Unvested equity held by the six executives accelerates and is worth $11.2 million at $32.00. The total of $30.0 million is 1.14 percent of the equity value at the offer. Corvane has said privately that it expects to retain "most of the current management team," but its offer documents describe no arrangement for Mr. Sandoval.

**Exhibit C: Pension.** A closed defined-benefit plan has a projected-benefit-obligation deficit of $64 million pre-tax, or $48 million after tax at 25 percent. It is not in the $400 million of net debt in Table 13.2. Corvane's advisers treat it as debt-like; management's plan does not, and the forecast cash flows contain no special contributions.

**Exhibit D: Calibration Services.** The division has FY2026E revenue of $372 million and EBITDA of $96 million, so the instruments business has revenue of $828 million and EBITDA of $144 million. Oriel Capital has told Calloway Hart in confidence that it would pay 11.0 times EBITDA, or $1,056 million, subject to diligence. The estimated tax cost of a sale is $150 million. Corvane's public statements make clear that the services business is its main strategic attraction.

**Exhibit E: Antitrust.** Counsel estimates a 30 to 35 percent chance that a Corvane acquisition draws an in-depth review of the overlapping laser line, with a possible remedy of divesting about $40 million of revenue. Altair's portfolio is adjacent and the chance is much lower. The completion probabilities and timelines in Table 13.9 are Calloway Hart's judgments, not guarantees.

**Exhibit F: Discount rate.** Regression betas for Brightwell range from 0.80 to 1.12 depending on index and window; the peer-relevered average, preferred by Calloway Hart, is 0.91. Corvane's advisers may use a lower rate, which supports a higher price. The 8.5 percent WACC is a central estimate; the board should see the range.

**Exhibit G: Third-quarter results.** Unaudited Q3 revenue grew 5.2 percent and EBITDA margin was 20.4 percent, in line with plan. Bookings fell 3 percent year on year while backlog rose 6 percent. The data are not public, and Corvane's offer relies only on public information. Selective disclosure is not permitted, and any claim that prospects are better than the market believes must be supportable.

**Exhibit H: Shareholder dynamics.** Larkspur's letter asks the board to form an independent committee, obtain a fairness opinion and "engage constructively." Three arbitrage funds with a combined 4.1 percent have built positions since the bid. Holders of 10 percent can call a special meeting; Larkspur and the family trust hold 16.3 percent together, and Corvane plus the arbitrage funds could reach 9.0 percent. The staggered board means the May 2027 annual meeting elects only one class. Corvane has indicated it may propose a short slate.

**Exhibit I: An ambiguous signal.** A trade publication reports that a "person close to Corvane" said its board has discussed a price "in the high 30s" as an upper limit. Corvane declines to comment. Treat it as, at most, one input to the probability of a raise.

**Exhibit J: Measurement ambiguities.** Defensible choices differ on: (1) counting the RSUs as fully dilutive (they vest on change of control, so the case says yes); (2) the treasury stock method at each price tested (the case does this) versus constant 82.3 million shares; (3) whether pension and non-cash interest belong in net debt; (4) terminal value from FCF growth (as in the case) versus an exit multiple; (5) valuing at 31 December 2026 versus 2 November 2026, a difference of about 1.4 percent of value at 8.5 percent.

## Teaching Note and Full Solutions

### Learning objectives

After working through this case students should be able to:

1. Convert a headline offer into diluted equity value, enterprise value, multiples and premiums, and judge them against peers and precedents.
2. Build a stand-alone DCF under several cases with the treasury stock method, and report a probability-weighted value with its sensitivities.
3. Value a bidder's synergies, compute its maximum price and the share of synergies a price transfers.
4. Explain a rights plan, quantify its dilution of a bidder and recognize its limits.
5. Evaluate a leveraged recapitalization, a counter-bid and an asset sale on value, deliverability and signaling.
6. Compare a white knight with a hostile bid on a certainty- and time-adjusted basis, and decode a post-bid price into implied probabilities.

#### Question 1: Offer value and premium

**Step 1: diluted shares at $32.00.** Options are in the money (strike $18.00 below $32.00). Treasury stock method: the options bring in 3.0m x $18.00 = $54.0m of proceeds, which repurchase $54.0m / $32.00 = 1.6875m shares at the offer price. Net new shares = 3.0 - 1.6875 = 1.3125m. (Equivalently 3.0 x (1 - 18/32) = 3.0 x 0.4375 = 1.3125.) RSUs vest on a change of control: 1.0m. Diluted shares = 80.0 + 1.3125 + 1.0 = 82.3125m.

**Step 2: equity and enterprise value.** Equity value = 82.3125 x $32.00 = $2,634.0m. Enterprise value = 2,634.0 + 520.0 - 120.0 = $3,034.0m.

**Step 3: multiples.**

```
EV / FY2026E EBITDA      = 3,034.0 / 240.0 = 12.64x
EV / FY2027E EBITDA (mgmt)  = 3,034.0 / 272.2 = 11.15x
EV / FY2027E EBITDA (board) = 3,034.0 / 260.8 = 11.63x
P/E (FY2026E)           = 32.00 / 1.4625  = 21.88x
```

**Step 4: premiums.**

```
To undisturbed $25.00: 32.00 / 25.00 - 1 = 28.0%
To 30-day VWAP $24.60:  32.00 / 24.60 - 1 = 30.1%
To 52-week high $28.40: 32.00 / 28.40 - 1 = 12.7%
```

The range of precedent premiums (22 to 41 percent) implies $30.50 to $35.25. But the EV/EBITDA multiple of 12.6x is above the precedent median of 12.1x and 2.3 turns above the peer median of 10.3x. Both statements are true. Brightwell traded at a higher multiple than peers before the bid, so a normal premium produces a higher multiple. The premium looks light; the multiple looks full. Quoting only one tells half the story.

**Step 6: the $32.70 price.** $32.70 is a 30.8 percent premium to undisturbed and $0.70 (2.2 percent) above the offer. The market is saying that the probability-weighted outcome is above $32.00, which requires a material probability of a higher bid. Question 9 quantifies it.

#### Question 5: The rights plan

**Economics.** On a trigger each non-acquirer right allows the holder to pay $100 and receive $200 of stock at the then price. At $32.00 that is 6.25 shares per right. If every non-acquirer holder exercised, 68.0m rights (after Corvane reaches 12.0m shares, or 15 percent) would create 425m new shares, raising $6.8 billion. The threat, not the exercise, is the point.

**Dilution by exchange.** At the trigger Corvane holds 15% x 80.0 = 12.0m shares and the other holders 68.0m. An exchange of one share per non-Corvane right adds 68.0m shares: total 148.0m. Corvane's stake falls from 15.0 percent to 12.0/148.0 = **8.11 percent**. On the stand-alone board-case equity value of $2,315.5m, the stake falls in value from 0.15 x 2,315.5 = $347.3m to 0.0811 x 2,315.5 = $187.7m, a loss of **$159.6m**. (Under the flip-in exercise alternative Corvane's stake would fall to 12.0 / 505 = 2.4 percent.) To reach 50.1 percent after the exchange Corvane would need 74.1m shares instead of 40.1m, so it would have to buy 62.1m rather than 28.1m. The pill does not change what Brightwell is worth in total; it makes unapproved accumulation prohibitively costly.

**Limits.** Corvane's toehold is 4.9 percent, well below 15 percent, and the tender crosses the threshold only on completion, so the pill is not yet triggered; leaving the rights in place prevents any tender from completing. A pill buys time and leverage; it cannot defeat a bid shareholders want, because shareholders can replace the board. Directors who keep a pill against a premium offer must explain the threat (inadequate price, coercive structure, information asymmetry), which is easier if the board is also seeking alternatives. To let the tender complete, the board would have to redeem the rights or exempt the offer, and should do so only if it concludes that $32.00 is a price shareholders should be allowed to accept.

**(a) Leveraged recapitalization.** Dividend: 81.0m shares (basic plus RSUs) x $12.00 = $972m; fees 2.8 percent of $1,000m = $28m; total new debt $1,000m. Gross debt = 520 + 1,000 = 1,520 (6.33x FY2026E EBITDA); net debt = 1,400 (5.83x). Interest = 33.8 + 1,000 x 8.5% = $118.8m; EBITDA/interest = 240 / 118.8 = 2.02x.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Management | 272.2 | 5.14x | 2.29x | 55.3 |
| Board | 260.8 | 5.37x | 2.19x | 51.1 |

Stub value: enterprise value is the board case 2,715.5, plus the debt tax shield 0.25 x 1,000 = 250.0, minus expected distress cost 8% x 25% x 2,715.5 = 54.3, for 2,911.2. Less net debt 1,400 gives equity of 1,511.2. With options re-struck to a $6.00 exercise price (in the money) the per-share stub solves to **$18.20**, and the total value to a holder is 12.00 + 18.20 = **$30.20**, or $2.00 more than the stand-alone board case. That uplift is entirely the tax shield net of expected distress cost. At a distress probability of 15 percent the total is $29.64 and at 25 percent $28.83, close to the unlevered value. It is a credible value-neutral-to-modest-positive alternative that delivers 38 percent of the offer in cash immediately, but headroom against any plausible covenant is thin (current EBITDA of 240 is only 1.0 percent above the 237.6 that gives 2.0x cover, and leverage starts at 5.8x), and the board would be exposing the company to a cyclical downturn to defend against a bidder.

**(b) Pac-Man.** Buying 51 percent of Corvane at a 30 percent premium costs 0.51 x 7,200 x 1.30 = $4,773.6m, which is 2.3 times Brightwell's whole equity value of $2,046m and more than four times its debt capacity at 6x EBITDA (6 x 240 - 400 = $1,040m). Infeasible; also it would signal panic.

**(c) Sale of Calibration Services.** Net proceeds 1,056 - 150 tax = $906m, or $11.19 on 81.0m shares. The remaining business has EBITDA of $144m; at the undisturbed multiple of 10.19x its EV is $1,467.6m, equity (after the unchanged $400m net debt) $1,067.6m, or $13.18 a share (options are out of the money at that price). Total **$24.37**, below the undisturbed price. The division must trade at above 12.35x on the remainder to equal the board case $28.21. A crown-jewel sale is value-destructive here because of tax leakage and because the retained business loses its recurring revenue and its multiple.

**Ranking.** On value: recap ($30.20), then Pac-Man (not computable), then asset sale ($24.37). On deliverability: asset sale and recap above Pac-Man. On signaling: the recap shows willingness to return capital; the asset sale signals a break-up; the Pac-Man signals desperation. None beats a higher offer, but the recap is a credible fallback if no bidder emerges.

**Altair value by share price** (value = $20.00 + 0.40 x price):

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Value per Brightwell share | 31.20 | 32.00 | 32.80 | 34.00 | 35.20 |

The offer equals Corvane's $32.00 at an Altair price of $30.00; each $1 move in Altair moves the offer $0.40. At $34.00 headline: diluted shares 80 + 3.0 x (1 - 18/34) + 1.0 = 82.4118m, equity $2,802.0m, EV $3,202.0m, 13.3x FY2026E EBITDA. **Certainty and time adjustment.** Value = [p x offer + (1 - p) x fallback] / 1.09^t.

```
Corvane: [0.75 x 32.00 + 0.25 x 25.00] = 30.25;  1.09^(11/12) = 1.0822;  30.25 / 1.0822 = $27.95
Altair:  [0.90 x 34.00 + 0.10 x 25.00] = 33.10;  1.09^(7/12)  = 1.0516;  33.10 / 1.0516 = $31.48
```

Altair's proposal is worth $3.53 more on this basis. If Corvane raised to $36.00 its adjusted value would be (0.75 x 36 + 0.25 x 25) / 1.0822 = $30.72, still below Altair at $34.00. The two are equal when Altair's offer is [27.952 x 1.0516 - 2.5] / 0.9 = $29.88, i.e. an Altair share price of $24.70. The white knight is therefore robust to a 29 percent fall in Altair's shares. #### Question 8: Accept, reject or counter

Value of rejecting = q x 35.00 + (1 - q) x fallback. Set equal to 32.00.

```
(a) fallback 26.50: q = (32.00 - 26.50) / (35.00 - 26.50) = 5.50 / 8.50 = 64.7%
(b) fallback 28.23: q = (32.00 - 28.23) / (35.00 - 28.23) = 3.77 / 6.77 = 55.7%
(c) fallback 25.00: q = 7.00 / 10.00 = 70.0%
```

| q (raise) | EV of rejecting, fallback $26.50 | fallback $28.23 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 30% | 29.05 | 30.26 |
| 50% | 30.75 | 31.62 |
| 60% | 31.60 | 32.29 |
| 70% | 32.45 | 32.97 |

The board should reject only if it believes a raise is more likely than roughly 56 to 65 percent, ignoring time value. A flat "no" clears a high bar; pairing rejection with a live alternative (Altair) improves both the raise probability and the fallback value. Exhibit I raises the estimate modestly if credible, but it is anonymous and should not carry the decision. #### Question 9: Reading the market price

States: complete at $32.00 (probability a), bump to $36.00 (b), fail with fallback $26.00 (c = 0.12). Six months at 6 percent: discount factor 1/1.06^0.5 = 0.97129. Price = 0.97129 x (32a + 36b) + 26 x 0.12 = 32.70, with a + b = 0.88.

```
32a + 36b = (32.70 - 3.12) / 0.97129 = 30.454
32 x 0.88 = 28.160;  b = (30.454 - 28.160) / 4 = 0.5736
a = 0.88 - 0.5736 = 0.3064
```

The market implies a **57.4 percent** probability of a bump, 30.6 percent that Corvane completes at $32.00 and 12 percent failure. For an arbitrageur buying at $32.70 the return is -2.1 percent (-4.2 percent annualized) if the deal closes at $32.00, +10.1 percent (+21.2 percent annualized) at a bump to $36.00, and -20.5 percent on failure. Buyers at this price are betting on a higher offer, not on completion at $32.00. For the board this means that shareholders will not tender at $32.00 when they can sell at $32.70 and that Corvane's tender is unlikely to reach the 50.1 percent minimum unless it raises. The board's bargaining position is strong, but only while the market believes a rival or a raise is possible; a flat rejection that forces Corvane to withdraw would collapse the price toward $26.

#### Question 10: Directors' duties, process and recommendation

**Duties, in general terms (not legal advice).** Directors owe duties of care (act on an informed basis), loyalty (act in the company's interests, not their own) and good faith. In a takeover, those duties are normally scrutinized more closely than ordinary business decisions because the board may have an interest in its own continuance. Directors should be able to show a reasonable basis for concluding that an offer is inadequate or threatening, that any defensive measure is proportionate, and that they have not used it to entrench themselves. In some jurisdictions the duty becomes one of securing the best value reasonably available once a sale of control is likely; in others directors may weigh the interests of employees, customers and other stakeholders insofar as they bear on the long-term success of the company. A board need not accept any offer or auction the company, but it must be informed and document its process.

**Conflicts.** (1) Executive payments of $30.0 million (Exhibit B) and uncertainty over post-deal roles: Mr. Sandoval and Ms. Raman should not vote or lead the process. (2) Calloway Hart's success fee, contingent on a completed deal: obtain a second adviser on a fixed fee for a fairness opinion. (3) The family trust's 6.5 percent, held by the chief executive: disclose and treat the trust as an interested holder. (4) Directors' own holdings (1.2 percent): disclose. (5) The October rejection was made without a formal valuation: the special committee should revisit it and record the reasoning.

**Steps before 9 November.** Form a committee of independent directors chaired by Mr. Achterberg; hire independent counsel and a second adviser; obtain management's plan, a challenge to it, and a written valuation range; sign a confidentiality and standstill agreement with Altair and offer diligence; ask Corvane whether it would pay above $35 and on what conditions; meet Larkspur and the three largest passive holders; prepare a recapitalization contingency and a disclosure plan for Q3 results; minute each decision.

**Recommendation.** Reject the tender as inadequate; keep the rights plan in place pending the process; authorize engagement with both Altair and Corvane; authorize a recapitalization as a contingency but not as an immediate action; and ask for the tender period to be extended. The memo follows.

### Sensitivity discussion

The recommendation is most sensitive to four numbers. (1) WACC: a 0.6-point reduction takes the board case to $32.00; the same move on the management plan adds more than $4 (8.0 percent, g = 2.5 percent gives $37.29). (2) The plan: the spread between the management and the board cases ($5.54) is as large as the premium over the market ($7.00) less the probability-weighted value gap. (3) The completion probability for Corvane: at 75 percent the adjusted value is $27.95, at 100 percent it would be $32.00 / 1.0822 = $29.57, so antitrust risk alone costs about $1.62 a share. (4) Altair's share price, which moves the offer by $0.40 per dollar. The cheapest uncertainty to resolve, by asking, is Corvane's willingness to pay, and it dominates the decision tree.

### Common student errors

- Using 80.0m or 81.0m shares at every price instead of the treasury stock method at each price.
- Computing the premium against the current $32.70 price or the 52-week high rather than the undisturbed price.
- Calling the board case "the" intrinsic value; the weighted $28.23 and the range matter.
- Omitting the pension, or double counting it by also deducting the non-cash interest.
- Using the cost of equity instead of WACC, or discounting the terminal value one year too few.
- Treating EPS accretion as proof the bid creates value for Corvane.
- Analyzing the pill as if it changed the total value of Brightwell; it changes who can control the outcome.
- Comparing the Altair and Corvane offers on headline price without time, probability and stock-price risk.
- Treating the post-bid price as the stand-alone value.
- Recommending "just say no" without a fallback or a documented process.

### Alternative defensible answers

Three outcomes can be defended with the same numbers. **Accept**: if the board believes Corvane will not raise, Altair is not credible and the management plan is aspirational, then the $28.23 weighted value and $26.50 fallback make $32.00 fair, and the 56 to 65 percent raise threshold is too high. **Reject and counter**: the recommended course, relying on Altair, the market's pricing of a bump and the management plan's $33.75. **No recommendation**: the board states the facts and leaves the choice to shareholders, easier to defend if the board is split or conflicted. Grade the logic from numbers to choice, the treatment of conflicts, and whether the student states what evidence would change the answer.

**To:** Board of Directors, Brightwell Instruments. **From:** Calloway Hart. **Date:** 6 November 2026 (for the meeting of 9 November). **Subject:** Response to the Corvane tender offer. *Illustrative.*

**Recommendation.** Do not recommend that shareholders tender at $32.00. Authorize management and the independent committee to pursue a higher price from Corvane and a competing proposal from Altair, and extend the process by at least three weeks.

**Basis.** (1) The offer is a 28.0 percent premium, below the 32 percent precedent median, at 12.6x EBITDA, above the 12.1x precedent median and 10.3x peer median. (2) Our probability-weighted stand-alone value is $28.23 (range $21.66 to $33.75); the offer exceeds it by $3.77 but is below the management plan's $33.75. (3) Corvane can afford more: its break-even is $32.00 on our case and $37.53 on the management plan; a $32.00 price transfers essentially all of its synergies. (4) Altair's indication is worth $34.00 headline and $31.48 after risk and time, against $27.95 for the Corvane bid; the two are not yet comparable as Altair's is not committed. (5) The market price of $32.70 implies a 57.4 percent chance of a bump; rejecting is justified if the true chance exceeds 56 to 65 percent, which is why the board needs Altair as a live alternative.

**Actions.** Maintain the rights plan and do not trigger an exchange; sign a standstill and open diligence with Altair; invite Corvane to submit a revised price above $35.00; ready a $12.00 recapitalization (value to a holder $30.20; 5.8x net leverage) for use only if no bidder emerges; do not pursue a counter-bid or asset sale; manage conflicts as set out in Question 10; brief Larkspur and the largest holders; and publish the Q3 data only with the recommendation and with counsel's advice.

**Risks.** Corvane withdraws and the price falls toward $26; Altair does not commit; shareholders tender anyway; a recap leaves little headroom in a downturn. **Reversal trigger.** If neither bidder improves by the end of the extended period, the board should reconsider whether $32.00 is the best value reasonably available.
