# Appendix B: Glossary

Terms are listed alphabetically. Chapter numbers in parentheses point to the main discussion in the book.

**Abandonment option** – The right to shut down a project and recover salvage value if conditions deteriorate, which sets a floor on project value (Ch. 8).

**Abnormal return** – The actual return on a security minus the return expected from a benchmark model; the basic measure in event studies (Ch. 12).

**Accounts payable** – Amounts owed to suppliers for goods and services received on credit, a spontaneous source of short-term financing (Ch. 17).

**Accounts receivable** – Amounts owed to the firm by customers who bought on credit; an investment in working capital (Ch. 17).

**Accretion/dilution** – The change in the acquirer's earnings per share caused by an acquisition; it describes earnings effects, not value creation (Ch. 22).

**Accrual accounting** – Recording revenue when earned and expenses when incurred rather than when cash changes hands (Ch. 2).

**Activist investor** – A shareholder who takes a significant stake to press management for changes in strategy, capital allocation, or governance (Ch. 24).

**Adjusted present value (APV)** – A valuation method that adds the value of financing side effects, such as interest tax shields, to the all-equity value of a project (Ch. 11).

**Agency conflict** – A divergence of interests between a principal, such as shareholders, and an agent, such as management, that creates costs (Ch. 1).

**Agency cost** – The sum of monitoring, bonding, and residual losses arising from agency conflicts (Ch. 1).

**Alpha** – The return on an asset in excess of what its systematic risk would predict under a pricing model such as the CAPM (Ch. 10).

**American option** – An option that may be exercised at any time up to and including expiry (Ch. 18).

**Amortisation** – The gradual repayment of a loan through scheduled payments, or the allocation of an intangible asset's cost over its useful life (Ch. 3).

**Annuity** – A finite series of equal cash flows occurring at regular intervals (Ch. 3).

**Annuity due** – An annuity whose payments occur at the beginning of each period rather than the end (Ch. 3).

**Anti-takeover provision** – A charter or contractual device, such as a poison pill or staggered board, that makes hostile acquisition more difficult (Ch. 22).

**Arbitrage** – Earning a riskless profit with no net investment by exploiting price differences between equivalent assets (Ch. 18).

**Arbitrage pricing theory (APT)** – A model in which expected return is a linear function of exposures to several systematic risk factors (Ch. 10).

**Asset beta** – The beta of a firm's operating assets, free of the effect of financial leverage; also called unlevered beta (Ch. 11).

**Asset turnover** – Sales divided by assets, measuring how intensively assets generate revenue (Ch. 2).

**At-the-money** – Describes an option whose strike price equals the current price of the underlying (Ch. 18).

**Backwardation** – A market condition in which futures prices are below the spot price or decline with maturity (Ch. 19).

**Balance sheet** – A statement of a firm's assets, liabilities, and equity at a point in time (Ch. 2).

**Bankruptcy** – A legal process for resolving a firm's inability to pay its debts, through liquidation or reorganisation (Ch. 23).

**Basis** – The difference between the spot price of an asset and the price of a related futures contract (Ch. 19).

**Basis point** – One hundredth of one percent, or 0.0001, used to quote small changes in rates and spreads (Ch. 4).

**Basis risk** – The risk that a hedging instrument and the exposure being hedged do not move perfectly together (Ch. 19).

**Behavioural finance** – The study of how psychological biases and limits to arbitrage affect investors and prices (Ch. 12).

**Benchmark** – A reference portfolio or index against which performance is measured (Ch. 10).

**Beta** – A measure of an asset's sensitivity to market movements, equal to its covariance with the market divided by market variance (Ch. 10).

**Bid-ask spread** – The difference between the price at which a dealer will buy and the price at which it will sell, a transaction cost (Ch. 12).

**Binomial model** – An option pricing approach that models the underlying price as moving up or down in each discrete step (Ch. 8, 18).

**Black-Scholes model** – A closed-form formula for pricing European options, derived by Black and Scholes and extended by Merton (Ch. 18).

**Board of directors** – The body elected by shareholders to oversee management and approve major strategic and financial decisions (Ch. 24).

**Bond** – A debt security promising fixed or floating payments of interest and principal (Ch. 4).

**Bond covenant** – A contractual restriction or requirement in a debt agreement that protects lenders (Ch. 14).

**Bond indenture** – The legal contract between issuer and bondholders that specifies terms, covenants, and the trustee's role (Ch. 14).

**Book building** – A process in which underwriters collect investor orders to determine the offer price of a new issue (Ch. 13).

**Book value** – The accounting value of an asset or of equity, based on historical cost less accumulated depreciation (Ch. 2).

**Bootstrap effect** – An apparent rise in EPS after an acquisition caused by a high-P/E acquirer buying a low-P/E target, without any real synergy (Ch. 22).

**Break-even analysis** – Determining the sales volume at which a project's profit or NPV equals zero (Ch. 7).

**Bridge loan** – Short-term financing that provides funds until permanent financing is arranged (Ch. 14).

**Business risk** – The variability of operating income arising from the firm's industry, cost structure, and demand (Ch. 15).

**Call option** – A contract giving the holder the right, but not the obligation, to buy an asset at a fixed strike price (Ch. 18).

**Call provision** – A feature allowing the issuer to redeem a bond before maturity at a stated price (Ch. 14).

**Cap** – A series of interest rate call options that pays when a floating rate exceeds a strike rate (Ch. 19).

**Capital asset pricing model (CAPM)** – The model stating that expected return equals the risk-free rate plus beta times the market risk premium (Ch. 10).

**Capital budgeting** – The process of evaluating and selecting long-term investments (Ch. 7).

**Capital gains yield** – The expected percentage price appreciation of a security, equal to g in the constant-growth model (Ch. 5).

**Capital market line** – The line showing the best attainable risk-return combinations from the risk-free asset and the market portfolio (Ch. 10).

**Capital rationing** – A situation in which a firm has more positive-NPV projects than it can fund (Ch. 6).

**Capital structure** – The mix of debt, equity, and other securities used to finance a firm (Ch. 15).

**Carried interest** – The share of fund profits, commonly 20 percent, paid to private equity general partners above a hurdle (Ch. 23).

**Cash conversion cycle** – The days between paying suppliers and collecting from customers; inventory days plus receivable days minus payable days (Ch. 17).

**Cash flow statement** – A statement reporting cash generated and used in operating, investing, and financing activities (Ch. 2).

**Cash sweep** – A loan provision requiring excess cash to be applied to debt repayment (Ch. 23).

**CDS (credit default swap)** – A contract in which a buyer pays a periodic premium in exchange for compensation if a reference entity defaults (Ch. 19).

**Certainty equivalent** – The guaranteed amount that makes an investor indifferent to a risky payoff (Ch. 9).

**Clientele effect** – The tendency of investors to prefer firms whose dividend policy suits their tax position and income needs (Ch. 16).

**Collar** – A strategy combining a long position with a purchased put and a written call to bound outcomes (Ch. 18).

**Collateral** – Assets pledged to secure a loan, which the lender may seize on default (Ch. 14).

**Commercial paper** – Short-term unsecured promissory notes issued by creditworthy corporations (Ch. 17).

**Commodity risk** – Exposure to changes in the prices of raw materials or energy (Ch. 20).

**Common stock** – Equity ownership that carries residual claims on cash flows and assets and usually voting rights (Ch. 5).

**Comparable company analysis** – Valuing a firm by applying multiples observed in similar publicly traded firms (Ch. 21).

**Compensating balance** – A minimum deposit a borrower must keep at a bank, raising the effective cost of a loan (Ch. 17).

**Compounding frequency** – The number of times per year interest is added to principal (Ch. 3).

**Compound interest** – Interest earned on both the original principal and previously accumulated interest (Ch. 3).

**Conglomerate discount** – The tendency of diversified firms to trade below the sum of the values of their parts (Ch. 21).

**Contango** – A market condition in which futures prices exceed the spot price or rise with maturity (Ch. 19).

**Contingent claim** – A security whose payoff depends on the value of another asset, such as an option (Ch. 18).

**Control premium** – The excess over the unaffected market price paid to gain control of a company (Ch. 21).

**Conversion ratio** – The number of common shares received on conversion of one convertible bond or preferred share (Ch. 14).

**Convertible bond** – A bond that the holder may exchange for a fixed number of shares (Ch. 14).

**Convexity** – The curvature of the price-yield relationship of a bond, which refines duration estimates of price changes (Ch. 4).

**Corporate governance** – The system of rules, practices, and processes by which a company is directed and controlled (Ch. 24).

**Correlation** – A standardised measure, from −1 to +1, of how closely two variables move together (Ch. 9).

**Cost of capital** – The return required by investors on a firm's capital, used as the discount rate for projects of similar risk (Ch. 11).

**Cost of debt** – The effective rate a firm pays on its borrowing, usually measured as the yield to maturity before tax (Ch. 11).

**Cost of equity** – The return shareholders require for bearing the risk of owning the firm's stock (Ch. 11).

**Coupon rate** – The annual interest payment on a bond expressed as a percentage of face value (Ch. 4).

**Covariance** – A measure of how two random variables move together, unscaled by their standard deviations (Ch. 9).

**Covenant-lite loan** – A loan with few maintenance covenants, offering borrowers more flexibility and lenders less protection (Ch. 14).

**Covered call** – Holding an asset while writing a call on it to earn premium income (Ch. 18).

**Credit rating** – An independent opinion on an issuer's creditworthiness, expressed on a letter scale (Ch. 4).

**Credit risk** – The risk that a borrower or counterparty fails to meet its payment obligations (Ch. 4).

**Credit spread** – The yield on a risky bond minus the yield on a comparable risk-free bond (Ch. 4).

**Cross rate** – An exchange rate between two currencies derived from each one's rate against a third currency (Ch. 20).

**Cumulative abnormal return (CAR)** – The sum of abnormal returns over an event window (Ch. 12).

**Current ratio** – Current assets divided by current liabilities, a measure of liquidity (Ch. 2).

**Current yield** – A bond's annual coupon divided by its market price (Ch. 4).

**Days sales outstanding (DSO)** – The average number of days it takes to collect receivables (Ch. 17).

**Dead-weight cost** – A loss of value, such as the expense of financial distress, that benefits no party (Ch. 15).

**Debenture** – An unsecured bond backed only by the issuer's general creditworthiness (Ch. 14).

**Debt capacity** – The amount of debt a firm can carry while retaining acceptable credit quality and flexibility (Ch. 15).

**Debt overhang** – A situation in which existing debt discourages new investment because gains would accrue mostly to creditors (Ch. 15).

**Debt service coverage ratio (DSCR)** – Cash available for debt service divided by scheduled interest and principal (Ch. 14).

**Debt-to-equity ratio** – Total debt divided by total equity, a measure of financial leverage (Ch. 2).

**Default** – Failure to make a promised payment or to comply with a debt covenant (Ch. 4).

**Default risk premium** – The extra yield investors demand for bearing the chance that the issuer will not pay in full (Ch. 4).

**Delta** – The change in an option's price per unit change in the underlying asset's price (Ch. 18).

**Depreciation** – The allocation of the cost of a tangible asset over its useful life, a non-cash expense that creates a tax shield (Ch. 7).

**Depreciation tax shield** – The reduction in taxes from deducting depreciation, equal to depreciation times the tax rate (Ch. 7).

**Derivative** – A financial contract whose value derives from an underlying asset, rate, or index (Ch. 19).

**Dilution** – The reduction in existing shareholders' percentage ownership or per-share value when new shares are issued (Ch. 13).

**Discounted payback** – The time needed to recover an investment from discounted cash flows (Ch. 6).

**Discount factor** – The present value of one unit received in a future period, equal to 1/(1+r)^t (Ch. 3).

**Discount rate** – The rate used to convert future cash flows to present value, reflecting their risk and timing (Ch. 3).

**Diversification** – Spreading investments across assets to reduce risk that is specific to individual holdings (Ch. 9).

**Dividend** – A distribution of earnings by a company to its shareholders, usually in cash (Ch. 16).

**Dividend discount model** – A valuation model that sets a stock's price equal to the present value of expected dividends (Ch. 5).

**Dividend irrelevance** – The Modigliani-Miller result that, in perfect markets, dividend policy does not affect firm value (Ch. 16).

**Dividend payout ratio** – The fraction of earnings paid out as dividends (Ch. 16).

**Dividend yield** – Annual dividends per share divided by share price (Ch. 5).

**Dual-class shares** – A structure in which classes of stock carry different voting rights, separating control from cash-flow ownership (Ch. 24).

**Due diligence** – The investigation of a target or issuer's financial, legal, and operational condition before a transaction (Ch. 22).

**DuPont analysis** – A decomposition of return on equity into profit margin, asset turnover, and leverage (Ch. 2).

**Duration** – A measure of a bond's price sensitivity to yield changes, equal to the weighted average time to receipt of cash flows (Ch. 4).

**Earnings before interest and taxes (EBIT)** – Operating profit before financing costs and income taxes (Ch. 2).

**Earnings per share (EPS)** – Net income available to common shareholders divided by weighted average shares outstanding (Ch. 2).

**Earn-out** – A deal feature in which part of the purchase price depends on the target's future performance (Ch. 22).

**EBITDA** – Earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortisation, a common proxy for operating cash earnings (Ch. 2).

**Economic profit** – Operating profit after tax minus a charge for the capital employed; also called economic value added (Ch. 1).

**Effective annual rate (EAR)** – The annual rate that accounts for compounding within the year (Ch. 3).

**Efficient frontier** – The set of portfolios offering the highest expected return for each level of risk (Ch. 10).

**Efficient market hypothesis** – The proposition that security prices fully reflect available information to a specified degree (Ch. 12).

**Enterprise value** – The market value of a firm's operating business: equity plus debt and other claims minus cash (Ch. 21).

**Environmental, social and governance (ESG)** – A framework for assessing a firm's environmental impact, social relationships, and governance quality (Ch. 24).

**Equity multiplier** – Total assets divided by equity, a measure of leverage used in DuPont analysis (Ch. 2).

**Equity risk premium** – The expected excess return of equities over the risk-free rate (Ch. 11).

**Equivalent annual annuity** – The level annual cash flow with the same present value as a project's NPV over its life, used to compare projects of unequal lives (Ch. 6).

**Eurobond** – A bond issued in a currency other than that of the country where it is sold (Ch. 14).

**European option** – An option that may be exercised only at expiry (Ch. 18).

**Event study** – An empirical method measuring the effect of an announcement on security prices through abnormal returns (Ch. 12).

**Exchange rate** – The price of one currency in terms of another (Ch. 20).

**Ex-dividend date** – The first day on which a buyer of a stock is not entitled to the declared dividend (Ch. 16).

**Exercise price** – The fixed price at which an option holder may buy or sell the underlying; also called strike price (Ch. 18).

**Expected return** – The probability-weighted average of possible returns (Ch. 9).

**Expected shortfall** – The average loss in the tail beyond the value at risk threshold (Ch. 20).

**Expiration date** – The date on which an option or derivative contract ceases to exist (Ch. 18).

**Face value** – The principal amount a bond repays at maturity; also par value (Ch. 4).

**Fair value** – The price at which an asset would exchange between informed, willing parties in an orderly transaction (Ch. 2).

**Fama-French model** – An asset pricing model adding size and value factors to the market factor (Ch. 10).

**Financial distress** – A condition in which a firm has difficulty meeting obligations to creditors (Ch. 15).

**Financial flexibility** – The ability of a firm to raise capital or adjust operations to meet unforeseen needs (Ch. 15).

**Financial lease** – A long-term lease that transfers substantially all the risks and rewards of ownership to the lessee (Ch. 14).

**Financial leverage** – The use of debt to finance assets, which magnifies returns and risk to equity (Ch. 15).

**Firm commitment underwriting** – An arrangement in which the underwriter buys the whole issue and bears the risk of resale (Ch. 13).

**Fisher effect** – The relationship that the nominal interest rate approximately equals the real rate plus expected inflation (Ch. 3).

**Fixed costs** – Costs that do not vary with output in the short run (Ch. 7).

**Floating-rate note** – A bond whose coupon resets periodically relative to a reference rate (Ch. 4).

**Floor** – A series of interest rate put options that pays when a floating rate falls below a strike rate (Ch. 19).

**Flotation cost** – The expense of issuing new securities, including underwriting fees and legal costs (Ch. 13).

**Forward contract** – A customised agreement to buy or sell an asset at a fixed price on a future date (Ch. 19).

**Forward rate** – An interest rate for a future period implied by current spot rates (Ch. 4).

**Free cash flow** – Cash generated by operations after taxes and reinvestment in capital and working capital (Ch. 2, 7).

**Free cash flow to equity (FCFE)** – Cash flow available to shareholders after debt service and reinvestment (Ch. 21).

**Free cash flow to the firm (FCFF)** – Cash flow available to all capital providers before financing payments (Ch. 21).

**Free-rider problem** – The tendency of dispersed shareholders to leave costly monitoring to others (Ch. 24).

**Futures contract** – A standardised, exchange-traded agreement to buy or sell an asset at a set price on a future date, settled daily (Ch. 19).

**Future value** – The amount to which a present sum grows when invested at a given rate (Ch. 3).

**Gamma** – The rate of change of an option's delta with respect to the underlying price (Ch. 18).

**General partner** – The manager of a private equity or venture fund who makes investments and bears unlimited liability (Ch. 23).

**Geometric mean return** – The compound average growth rate of an investment over multiple periods (Ch. 9).

**Golden parachute** – A contractual payment to executives upon loss of their jobs after a change in control (Ch. 22).

**Goodwill** – The excess of an acquisition price over the fair value of identifiable net assets acquired (Ch. 22).

**Green bond** – A bond whose proceeds are earmarked for environmentally beneficial projects (Ch. 24).

**Greenium** – The lower yield, relative to comparable conventional bonds, that investors sometimes accept on green bonds (Ch. 24).

**Gross margin** – Sales minus cost of goods sold, as a percentage of sales (Ch. 2).

**Growth opportunities** – Future investments with positive NPV, whose value is included in a stock price beyond current earnings (Ch. 5).

**Hamada equation** – A formula relating levered beta to unlevered beta, the debt-equity ratio, and the tax rate (Ch. 11).

**Hedge ratio** – The quantity of a hedging instrument per unit of exposure needed to offset risk (Ch. 19).

**Hedging** – Taking an offsetting position to reduce exposure to price, rate, or currency movements (Ch. 19).

**Herding** – The tendency of investors to imitate the actions of others rather than act on their own analysis (Ch. 12).

**High-yield bond** – A bond rated below investment grade, offering higher yield for higher default risk (Ch. 14).

**Holding period return** – The total return from owning an asset for a given period, including income and price change (Ch. 9).

**Hostile takeover** – An acquisition attempt made without the agreement of the target's board (Ch. 22).

**Hubris hypothesis** – The idea that acquirers overpay because managers overestimate their ability to create value (Ch. 22).

**Hurdle rate** – The minimum return required for an investment to be accepted, or the preferred return in a private equity fund (Ch. 6, 23).

**Idiosyncratic risk** – Risk specific to a single firm or asset that can be diversified away; also unsystematic risk (Ch. 9).

**Implied volatility** – The volatility of the underlying asset that makes a model price equal to the observed option price (Ch. 18).

**Income statement** – A statement of revenues, expenses, and profit over a period (Ch. 2).

**Incremental cash flow** – The change in a firm's cash flows resulting from accepting a project (Ch. 7).

**Information asymmetry** – A situation in which one party, typically management, knows more than another, such as investors (Ch. 13).

**Initial public offering (IPO)** – The first sale of a company's shares to the public (Ch. 13).

**Insider trading** – Trading securities on material non-public information in breach of a duty, which is illegal in most jurisdictions (Ch. 12).

**Intangible assets** – Non-physical assets such as patents, brands, and software (Ch. 2).

**Interest coverage ratio** – EBIT divided by interest expense, a measure of ability to service debt (Ch. 2).

**Interest rate parity** – The condition that the forward exchange rate premium reflects the interest rate differential between two currencies (Ch. 20).

**Interest rate risk** – The risk that changes in interest rates affect the value of assets or the cost of liabilities (Ch. 4).

**Interest rate swap** – An agreement to exchange fixed-rate and floating-rate interest payments on a notional principal (Ch. 19).

**Interest tax shield** – The tax saving from the deductibility of interest expense (Ch. 15).

**Internal rate of return (IRR)** – The discount rate that makes a project's NPV equal to zero (Ch. 6).

**International Fisher effect** – The proposition that expected currency changes reflect nominal interest rate differentials (Ch. 20).

**In-the-money** – Describes an option that would have positive payoff if exercised immediately (Ch. 18).

**Intrinsic value** – The value of an asset based on fundamentals, or for an option the payoff if exercised immediately (Ch. 5, 18).

**Inventory turnover** – Cost of goods sold divided by average inventory (Ch. 2).

**Investment banker** – A financial intermediary that advises on and underwrites securities offerings and transactions (Ch. 13).

**Investment grade** – Credit ratings at or above BBB− (S&P scale) or Baa3 (Moody's scale), indicating relatively low default risk (Ch. 4).

**Jensen's alpha** – The excess return of a portfolio over its CAPM-predicted return (Ch. 10).

**Junk bond** – Informal term for a high-yield bond (Ch. 14).

**Key performance indicator (KPI)** – A measurable value used to track progress toward an objective, often linked to sustainability-linked financing (Ch. 24).

**Lease** – A contract in which the owner of an asset grants another party the right to use it for payments (Ch. 14).

**Letter of credit** – A bank's guarantee of payment on behalf of a customer, common in trade finance (Ch. 17).

**Leveraged buyout (LBO)** – An acquisition financed largely with debt secured on the target's assets and cash flows (Ch. 23).

**Leverage ratio** – A measure of the extent to which debt finances a firm, such as debt to EBITDA (Ch. 2).

**Limited partner** – An investor in a private equity fund who provides capital and has limited liability and no management role (Ch. 23).

**Limits to arbitrage** – Frictions such as risk and costs that prevent arbitrageurs from eliminating mispricing (Ch. 12).

**Line of credit** – A bank commitment to lend up to a stated amount, drawn as needed (Ch. 17).

**Lintner model** – A description of dividend policy in which firms gradually adjust dividends toward a target payout (Ch. 16).

**Liquidation value** – The net amount realised by selling a firm's assets piecemeal (Ch. 23).

**Liquidity** – The ease with which an asset can be converted to cash without significant loss of value (Ch. 2).

**Liquidity premium** – The extra return demanded for holding assets that are hard to sell quickly (Ch. 4).

**Lock-up period** – A period after an IPO during which insiders may not sell their shares (Ch. 13).

**Long position** – Ownership of an asset or a contract that gains when the underlying price rises (Ch. 19).

**Macaulay duration** – The present-value-weighted average time until a bond's cash flows are received (Ch. 4).

**Management buyout (MBO)** – An acquisition of a company by its own managers, usually with outside financing (Ch. 23).

**Management fee** – The annual fee a fund manager charges, typically a percentage of committed or invested capital (Ch. 23).

**Marginal tax rate** – The tax rate on the next dollar of income (Ch. 7).

**Margin call** – A demand to deposit additional funds when a margin account falls below the required level (Ch. 19).

**Market capitalisation** – Share price multiplied by shares outstanding (Ch. 2).

**Market efficiency** – The degree to which prices reflect information (Ch. 12).

**Market model** – A regression relating a security's return to the market return, used in event studies (Ch. 12).

**Market portfolio** – The portfolio of all risky assets weighted by market value (Ch. 10).

**Market risk** – Systematic risk that affects all assets and cannot be diversified away (Ch. 9).

**Market risk premium** – The expected return on the market portfolio minus the risk-free rate (Ch. 10).

**Market-to-book ratio** – The market value of equity divided by its book value (Ch. 2).

**Maturity** – The date on which a debt instrument's principal becomes due (Ch. 4).

**Mean-variance analysis** – Portfolio selection based on expected return and variance, introduced by Markowitz (Ch. 10).

**Merger** – A combination in which one firm absorbs another and only one legal entity survives (Ch. 22).

**Mezzanine financing** – Subordinated debt or preferred equity that ranks between senior debt and common equity (Ch. 23).

**Minimum-variance portfolio** – The portfolio on the efficient frontier with the lowest possible risk (Ch. 10).

**Modified duration** – Macaulay duration divided by one plus the yield per period, giving percentage price change per unit yield change (Ch. 4).

**Modified internal rate of return (MIRR)** – An IRR variant that assumes explicit reinvestment and financing rates (Ch. 6).

**Modigliani-Miller theorems** – Propositions showing that, in perfect markets, capital structure and dividend policy do not affect firm value (Ch. 15, 16).

**Momentum** – The tendency of recent winning securities to continue outperforming in the short to medium term (Ch. 12).

**Money market** – The market for short-term, highly liquid debt instruments (Ch. 17).

**Moral hazard** – The temptation for an insured or protected party to take more risk because someone else bears the cost (Ch. 1).

**Mortgage bond** – A bond secured by a lien on specific real property (Ch. 14).

**Multiple of invested capital (MOIC)** – Total value returned to investors divided by capital invested (Ch. 23).

**Mutually exclusive projects** – Alternatives for which accepting one precludes accepting the others (Ch. 6).

**Net advantage to leasing (NAL)** – The present value of the benefits of leasing versus buying, after taxes (Ch. 7).

**Net debt** – Total debt minus cash and equivalents (Ch. 21).

**Net income** – Revenue minus all expenses, including interest and taxes (Ch. 2).

**Net present value (NPV)** – The present value of a project's cash inflows minus the present value of its outflows (Ch. 6).

**Net working capital** – Current assets minus current liabilities (Ch. 2, 17).

**Nominal rate** – A stated interest rate not adjusted for compounding or inflation (Ch. 3).

**Non-recourse debt** – Debt for which the lender's claim is limited to specified assets or project cash flows (Ch. 14).

**NOPAT** – Net operating profit after tax: EBIT multiplied by one minus the tax rate (Ch. 21).

**Normal distribution** – A symmetric bell-shaped probability distribution fully described by its mean and standard deviation (Ch. 9).

**Notional principal** – The reference amount on which payments in a swap or other derivative are calculated (Ch. 19).

**Operating cycle** – The time from acquiring inventory to collecting cash from its sale (Ch. 17).

**Operating lease** – A lease that does not transfer most of the risks and rewards of ownership (Ch. 14).

**Operating leverage** – The degree to which fixed operating costs magnify the effect of sales changes on operating income (Ch. 7).

**Opportunity cost** – The value of the best alternative forgone when a resource is used for a project (Ch. 7).

**Option premium** – The price paid by an option buyer to the seller (Ch. 18).

**Out-of-the-money** – Describes an option that would have no payoff if exercised immediately (Ch. 18).

**Overconfidence** – A bias in which individuals overestimate their knowledge or the precision of their forecasts (Ch. 12).

**Over-the-counter (OTC)** – Describes trades negotiated directly between parties rather than on an exchange (Ch. 19).

**Par value** – The face value of a bond or the nominal value assigned to a share (Ch. 4).

**Payback period** – The time required for a project's cumulative cash flows to recover the initial investment (Ch. 6).

**Pecking order theory** – The theory that firms prefer internal funds, then debt, and issue equity only as a last resort due to information asymmetry (Ch. 15).

**Perpetuity** – A stream of equal cash flows continuing indefinitely (Ch. 3).

**Poison pill** – A defensive measure that dilutes a hostile bidder's stake if it crosses a stated ownership threshold (Ch. 22).

**Political risk** – The risk that government actions harm the value of an investment abroad (Ch. 20).

**Portfolio** – A collection of investments held by an investor or institution (Ch. 9).

**Portfolio weight** – The proportion of a portfolio's value invested in a given asset (Ch. 10).

**Post-money valuation** – The value of a company immediately after a financing round, including new money (Ch. 13).

**Preferred stock** – An equity security with a fixed dividend and priority over common stock in dividends and liquidation (Ch. 5).

**Premium** – The amount by which a price exceeds a reference value, such as an offer above market price (Ch. 22).

**Pre-money valuation** – The value of a company before a new financing round (Ch. 13).

**Present value** – The current worth of a future cash flow discounted at an appropriate rate (Ch. 3).

**Price-earnings ratio (P/E)** – Share price divided by earnings per share (Ch. 5).

**Primary market** – The market in which new securities are sold by issuers to investors (Ch. 13).

**Prime rate** – A benchmark rate that banks use as a base for loans to creditworthy customers (Ch. 17).

**Principal-agent problem** – The difficulty of motivating an agent to act in the principal's interest (Ch. 1).

**Private equity** – Investment in companies not traded on public markets, typically through funds (Ch. 23).

**Private placement** – A sale of securities directly to a small group of institutional investors rather than the public (Ch. 14).

**Probability distribution** – A listing of the possible outcomes of a random variable and their probabilities (Ch. 9).

**Profitability index** – The present value of future cash flows divided by the initial investment (Ch. 6).

**Pro forma statements** – Projected financial statements prepared under specified assumptions (Ch. 7).

**Project finance** – Financing of a standalone project primarily from its own cash flows, usually through a special-purpose vehicle (Ch. 14).

**Prospect theory** – A descriptive theory of choice, by Kahneman and Tversky, in which people value gains and losses relative to a reference point and fear losses more (Ch. 12).

**Prospectus** – A legal document describing a securities offering to prospective investors (Ch. 13).

**Proxy fight** – An effort by dissident shareholders to win board seats or votes by soliciting proxies (Ch. 22).

**Put-call parity** – The no-arbitrage relationship linking the prices of European calls and puts with the same strike and expiry (Ch. 18).

**Put option** – A contract giving the holder the right, but not the obligation, to sell an asset at a fixed strike price (Ch. 18).

**Quick ratio** – Liquid current assets, excluding inventory, divided by current liabilities (Ch. 2).

**Random walk** – A process in which price changes are unpredictable and independent of past changes (Ch. 12).

**Real option** – The flexibility to alter a real investment, such as to expand, delay, or abandon, valued like a financial option (Ch. 8).

**Real rate** – An interest rate adjusted to remove the effect of inflation (Ch. 3).

**Recapitalisation** – A significant change in a firm's capital structure, such as a leveraged payout (Ch. 23).

**Recovery rate** – The fraction of a claim recovered by creditors after default (Ch. 4).

**Reinvestment risk** – The risk that cash flows from an investment must be reinvested at lower rates than expected (Ch. 4).

**Repurchase (buyback)** – A transaction in which a company buys its own shares from shareholders (Ch. 16).

**Residual dividend policy** – A policy of paying dividends only from earnings left over after funding all positive-NPV investments (Ch. 16).

**Residual income** – Earnings in excess of the required return on book equity, used in valuation (Ch. 5).

**Restructuring** – A significant change to a firm's operations, assets, or financing to improve value or avoid failure (Ch. 23).

**Return on assets (ROA)** – Net income divided by total assets (Ch. 2).

**Return on equity (ROE)** – Net income divided by shareholders' equity (Ch. 2).

**Return on invested capital (ROIC)** – NOPAT divided by invested capital, measuring operating returns to all capital providers (Ch. 2).

**Revolving credit facility** – A committed credit line that the borrower may draw, repay, and redraw (Ch. 14).

**Rights offering** – An issue of new shares offered first to existing shareholders in proportion to their holdings (Ch. 13).

**Risk aversion** – A preference for a certain outcome over a risky one with the same expected value (Ch. 9).

**Risk-free rate** – The return on an asset with no default risk, usually proxied by government securities (Ch. 10).

**Risk-neutral probability** – An artificial probability under which expected payoffs discounted at the risk-free rate give correct prices (Ch. 18).

**Risk premium** – The return earned over the risk-free rate as compensation for bearing risk (Ch. 9).

**Roadshow** – A series of presentations by company management and underwriters to prospective investors before an offering (Ch. 13).

**Salvage value** – The estimated resale value of an asset at the end of its useful life (Ch. 7).

**Scenario analysis** – Evaluating a project's outcomes under a small number of coherent combinations of assumptions (Ch. 8).

**Seasoned equity offering (SEO)** – A sale of additional shares by a firm that is already publicly listed (Ch. 13).

**Secondary market** – The market in which investors trade previously issued securities among themselves (Ch. 13).

**Secured debt** – Debt backed by specific collateral (Ch. 14).

**Security market line (SML)** – The graph of the CAPM relating expected return to beta (Ch. 10).

**Senior debt** – Debt with priority over other claims in payment and liquidation (Ch. 14).

**Sensitivity analysis** – Examining how a project's NPV changes when one input varies while others are held constant (Ch. 8).

**Shareholder value** – The value of the firm's equity to its owners, the central objective in corporate finance (Ch. 1).

**Sharpe ratio** – A portfolio's excess return over the risk-free rate divided by its standard deviation (Ch. 9).

**Short position** – A position that gains when the underlying price falls, often created by borrowing and selling an asset (Ch. 19).

**Signalling** – Actions, such as dividend changes, that convey private information to investors (Ch. 16).

**Simulation (Monte Carlo)** – A technique that evaluates outcomes by repeatedly sampling inputs from probability distributions (Ch. 8).

**Sinking fund** – A provision requiring the issuer to retire a portion of a bond issue periodically (Ch. 14).

**Social bond** – A bond whose proceeds fund projects with positive social outcomes (Ch. 24).

**Sovereign wealth fund** – A state-owned investment fund financed from national surpluses such as commodity revenues (Ch. 23).

**Special purpose vehicle (SPV)** – A separate legal entity created to isolate financial risk or hold specific assets (Ch. 14).

**Spot rate** – The interest rate or exchange rate for immediate settlement (Ch. 4, 20).

**Spread** – The difference between two prices or rates, such as the bid-ask spread or credit spread (Ch. 4).

**Stakeholder** – Any group with an interest in the firm, including employees, customers, suppliers, and communities (Ch. 1).

**Standard deviation** – The square root of variance, a common measure of total risk (Ch. 9).

**Statement of cash flows** – See Cash flow statement (Ch. 2).

**Stock dividend** – A distribution of additional shares to existing shareholders in proportion to holdings (Ch. 16).

**Stock split** – A division of existing shares into a larger number, lowering the price per share (Ch. 16).

**Straddle** – An option strategy combining a call and a put with the same strike and expiry (Ch. 18).

**Strategic buyer** – An acquirer motivated by operational synergies in its own business, as opposed to a financial sponsor (Ch. 22).

**Strike price** – See Exercise price (Ch. 18).

**Structured finance** – Techniques that pool assets and issue tranched securities with different risk profiles (Ch. 14).

**Subordinated debt** – Debt that ranks below senior debt in claims on assets (Ch. 14).

**Sunk cost** – A cost already incurred that cannot be recovered and should be ignored in decisions (Ch. 7).

**Supply chain finance** – Arrangements in which buyers' credit strength is used to give suppliers cheaper early payment (Ch. 17).

**Sustainability-linked loan** – A loan whose interest margin varies with the borrower's performance against sustainability targets (Ch. 24).

**Sustainable growth rate** – The maximum growth rate a firm can sustain without raising new equity or changing leverage or payout (Ch. 2).

**Swap** – An agreement to exchange cash flows according to a formula over time (Ch. 19).

**Swaption** – An option to enter into a swap at a specified rate in the future (Ch. 19).

**Synergy** – The additional value created by combining two firms beyond the sum of their stand-alone values (Ch. 22).

**Systematic risk** – Risk common to all assets in the economy that cannot be eliminated by diversification (Ch. 9).

**Takeover** – The acquisition of control of a target firm, friendly or hostile (Ch. 22).

**Tangency portfolio** – The risky portfolio with the highest Sharpe ratio, the optimal risky holding when a risk-free asset exists (Ch. 10).

**Tax shield** – A reduction in taxable income from a deductible item such as interest or depreciation (Ch. 7, 15).

**Tender offer** – A public offer to buy shares directly from shareholders at a stated price (Ch. 22).

**Terminal value** – The value of cash flows beyond an explicit forecast horizon (Ch. 21).

**Term structure of interest rates** – The relationship between yields and maturities of bonds of similar quality (Ch. 4).

**Theta** – The rate at which an option loses value as time passes (Ch. 18).

**Time value of money** – The principle that a sum received today is worth more than the same sum received later (Ch. 3).

**Tobin's q** – The ratio of the market value of a firm's assets to their replacement cost (Ch. 5).

**Tracking error** – The standard deviation of the difference between a portfolio's return and its benchmark's (Ch. 10).

**Trade credit** – Credit extended by suppliers allowing delayed payment for goods (Ch. 17).

**Trade-off theory** – The theory that firms balance the tax benefits of debt against the costs of financial distress (Ch. 15).

**Tranche** – A slice of a securitised or structured instrument with a specific risk and priority (Ch. 14).

**Transaction exposure** – The risk that exchange-rate changes alter the value of contracted foreign-currency cash flows (Ch. 20).

**Translation exposure** – The effect of exchange-rate changes on reported consolidated statements (Ch. 20).

**Treasury bill** – A short-term government security issued at a discount (Ch. 17).

**Treasury function** – The part of the finance organisation managing cash, funding, and financial risk (Ch. 1).

**Treasury stock** – Shares repurchased by the issuing company and held in its treasury (Ch. 16).

**Triangular arbitrage** – Profiting from inconsistent exchange rates among three currencies (Ch. 20).

**Underpricing** – The tendency of IPO shares to be offered below their first-day trading price (Ch. 13).

**Underwriter** – A financial institution that markets a securities offering and often guarantees its sale (Ch. 13).

**Unlevered beta** – See Asset beta (Ch. 11).

**Value at Risk (VaR)** – The loss that is not expected to be exceeded over a given horizon at a given confidence level (Ch. 9, 20).

**Value driver formula** – A valuation expression linking terminal value to growth, return on invested capital, and the cost of capital (Ch. 21).

**Variable costs** – Costs that vary in proportion to output (Ch. 7).

**Variance** – The expected squared deviation of a random variable from its mean (Ch. 9).

**Vega** – The sensitivity of an option's price to changes in the volatility of the underlying (Ch. 18).

**Venture capital** – Financing provided to early-stage, high-potential companies in exchange for equity (Ch. 13).

**Volatility** – The degree of variation in an asset's returns, usually measured as annualised standard deviation (Ch. 18).

**Warrant** – A long-dated option issued by a company to buy its own shares at a fixed price (Ch. 18).

**Weighted average cost of capital (WACC)** – The blended after-tax cost of a firm's debt, equity, and other capital, weighted by market values (Ch. 11).

**White knight** – A friendly acquirer sought by a target to avoid a hostile bid (Ch. 22).

**Working capital management** – Planning and control of current assets and liabilities to balance liquidity and profitability (Ch. 17).

**Yield curve** – A graph of yields against maturities for bonds of similar credit quality (Ch. 4).

**Yield to call** – The yield earned if a callable bond is held until its call date and redeemed (Ch. 4).

**Yield to maturity (YTM)** – The discount rate that equates a bond's price with the present value of its promised cash flows (Ch. 4).

**Zero-coupon bond** – A bond that pays no periodic interest and is issued at a discount to face value (Ch. 4).

**Z-score** – Either a count of standard deviations from a mean, or the Altman score used to predict bankruptcy (Ch. 9, 15).
