ASBA: Application Supported by Blocked Amount

ASBA is a mechanism prescribed by SEBI for applying in an IPO. Instead of handing over a cheque or draft with the application, the investor authorises their own bank to block the application money in their account until just before allotment. The money does not leave the investor’s account — and continues to belong to them — until the shares are actually allotted.

The benefits are substantial. The investor keeps the money earning interest while the issue is open, the issuer does not hold large sums that must be refunded, and refunds for unsuccessful bids become simple unblocking. For these reasons, all investors other than anchor investors must participate in an IPO only through ASBA. Anchors, who bid on a separate day with large amounts, pay through the escrow account.

An ASBA application can be made in one of two ways — as the handbook’s diagram shows, an online process (the investor’s bank or broker platform, or a UPI ID) and an offline process (a physical form submitted to a bank branch or intermediary).

UPI for retail investors

With effect from 1 January 2019, retail individual investors may also bid and block funds using a UPI ID. In this version a Sponsor Bank — a banker to the issue appointed by the issuer — acts as the conduit between the stock exchanges and the National Payments Corporation of India (NPCI): it pushes the mandate request to the investor’s UPI app, and the investor approves the blocking of funds. The money remains in the investor’s account until allotment.

Who can collect bids?

Application forms in a public issue can be collected by a range of designated intermediaries:

IntermediaryWhat it does
SCSBs (Self-Certified Syndicate Banks)Banks that offer the ASBA facility; the only channel for retail bids in the handbook’s description (plus UPI from 2019)
Syndicate membersSEBI-registered intermediaries (who can also act as underwriters) that collect bids from applicants other than retail investors and deposit them with SCSBs
Registered brokersStock brokers with nationwide terminals, other than syndicate members
CDPs (Collecting Depository Participants)Depository participants eligible to collect bids at designated locations
RTAs (Registrar and Share Transfer Agents)SEBI-registered agents eligible to collect bids at designated locations

Each of them enters the bid details into the electronic bidding system of the stock exchanges and carries out a preliminary verification. The issuer does not sign separate agreements with SCSBs, RTAs, CDPs and registered brokers; their obligations are “deemed agreements” arising from SEBI circulars and are recognised in clauses of the issue agreement.

The flow of money

  • Anchor investors pay their bid amounts into the Escrow Account held with an Escrow Collection Bank.
  • ASBA bidders have an equivalent amount blocked in their own accounts by the SCSB (and, for UPI, by the Sponsor Bank mechanism).
  • After the Prospectus is filed with the RoC, funds are transferred from the Escrow Account and the ASBA accounts to the Public Issue Account on the Designated Date. Only then does the Board allot the shares.
  • Excess money, and money for unsuccessful bids, is refunded (escrow) or unblocked (ASBA).

From closing to listing

Once the issue closes, a tightly sequenced set of actions takes place. Delay at any step postpones listing.

{{FIG:offertimeline}}
  1. The registrar obtains the electronic bid details from the stock exchange and reconciles them with the physical and electronic forms.
  2. The Issue Price is finalised and the underwriting agreement is executed.
  3. The Prospectus is filed with and registered by the RoC.
  4. The registrar submits the final Basis of Allotment to the designated stock exchange for approval.
  5. The registrar and BRLMs issue funds-transfer instructions to the collecting banks and SCSBs to credit funds to the Public Issue Account.
  6. The issuer allots the shares. Under the 2019 framework, allotment must be done within four working days of the Issue Closing Date.
  7. Successful bidders’ demat accounts are credited within two working days of allotment.
  8. The issuer makes the listing and trading application; the exchanges grant final listing and trading approval and trading begins.

Within ten days of completing various post-issue activities, the BRLMs must ensure that an advertisement giving details of subscription, the basis of allotment, the number, value and percentage of applications and of successful allottees, the date of dispatch of refunds or instructions to SCSBs, the date of credit of securities and the date of the listing application is released in the newspapers in which the pre-issue advertisement appeared (Regulation 51).

Since 2019

The post-closing timeline has been compressed dramatically since 2019. Listing within three working days of the issue closing date (“T+3”) was introduced as an option and later made mandatory; UPI-based bidding has been extended and standardised for retail and, later, for other investor categories with caps; and the process has moved towards 100% digital. The sequence of steps remains similar, but the days are fewer. See Chapter 29.

Chapter summary

  • ASBA blocks application money in the investor’s own bank account until allotment; all investors except anchors must use it.
  • From 1 January 2019, retail investors can use UPI via a Sponsor Bank; SCSBs, syndicate members, brokers, CDPs and RTAs collect and enter bids on the exchange system.
  • Funds move to the Public Issue Account on the Designated Date, after the Prospectus is registered.
  • Under the 2019 framework allotment is within four working days of closing and demat credit within two working days of allotment; a post-issue advertisement follows.
