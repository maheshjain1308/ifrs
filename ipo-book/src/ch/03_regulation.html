Four layers of rules

An Indian IPO is not governed by a single code. It sits inside four layers, and a transaction team has to satisfy all of them at once.

  1. Statute. The Companies Act, 2013 (and its rules), the Securities Contracts (Regulation) Act, 1956 (SCRA), the SEBI Act, 1992 and, for foreign money, the Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999 (FEMA).
  2. SEBI regulations and circulars. Above all the SEBI (ICDR) Regulations, 2018; the SEBI (LODR) Regulations, 2015; and regulations covering insider trading, takeovers, research analysts, merchant bankers and bankers to an issue.
  3. Stock-exchange requirements. Listing conditions, in-principle and final approvals, bidding platform rules and the format of listing agreements.
  4. Market practice. Not law, but expected by intermediaries: comfort letters, due-diligence back-up certificates, standard agreements, roadshow protocols.

The regulators and what each one decides

BodyRole in an IPO
SEBIReviews the DRHP and issues observations; may reject an offer document if disclosure is inadequate; enforces misstatement and fraud provisions (power conferred through Section 24 of the Companies Act in respect of listed or to-be-listed companies); registers and supervises intermediaries.
Stock exchanges (BSE / NSE)Grant in-principle approval on the DRHP and final listing and trading approval after allotment; run the electronic bidding system and the designated-exchange role in the basis of allotment.
Registrar of Companies (RoC)Registers the RHP and the Prospectus under the Companies Act; no Prospectus is valid without registration.
Reserve Bank of IndiaForeign-exchange aspects: issues to non-residents, and approvals where sector rules require them.
Sector regulators and ministriesAdditional approval in regulated industries — for example, the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India for an insurer’s IPO.
Depositories (NSDL, CDSL)Dematerialise the securities and credit them to investors’ accounts.

Practical note — reading regulations by function

When you meet a new rule, ask four questions: Which regulator wrote it? Who does it bind — the issuer, promoters, intermediaries or investors? At what stage — before filing, between filing and opening, during the issue, or after listing? What is the consequence of breach? This habit stops you treating every rule as equally important and makes the structure of the whole IPO visible.

Since 2019

Instruments cited in this book have been amended repeatedly. The ICDR Regulations 2018 have been amended many times, SEBI now consolidates many circulars into master circulars, and the exchange-based rules have evolved. Always work from the current consolidated text. Chapter 29 lists the changes that matter most.

Chapter summary

  • Four layers apply simultaneously: statute, SEBI regulations, exchange requirements and market practice.
  • SEBI reviews the offer document; the exchanges grant in-principle and final listing approvals; the RoC registers the RHP and Prospectus; the RBI and sector regulators add approvals where relevant.
  • Learn the short names — ICDR, LODR, SCRR, PAS Rules — because offer documents and advisers use them constantly.
