Basics

Can an unlisted company list its equity without an IPO?

No. An unlisted company can list its equity shares (or securities convertible into equity) only pursuant to a public issue. Debt securities can be listed by private placement.

What is the difference between an IPO and an FPO?

An IPO is the first public offer by an unlisted company; an FPO (further public offer) is a public offer by an already listed company.

What is an OFS?

An Offer For Sale: existing shareholders sell shares to the public through the IPO. Proceeds go to the sellers, not the company.

What are the three offer documents?

DRHP (filed with SEBI), RHP (filed with the RoC before the issue), and the Prospectus (final, with the Issue Price).

Must shares be in demat form?

Yes — every issuer making a public offer must issue securities in dematerialised form only.

Eligibility

Who cannot do an IPO?

Issuers (or their directors, promoters, promoter group or selling shareholders) debarred by SEBI; those linked to debarred companies; wilful defaulters; fugitive economic offenders; and issuers with outstanding convertibles or option rights (with ESOP and conversion-before-RHP exceptions).

What profit track record is needed?

Average operating profit of at least ₹15 crore over the preceding three years, with operating profit in each year; plus NTA of ₹3 crore and net worth of ₹1 crore in each year.

What if we do not meet the track-record test?

You may still list through a book-built IPO with at least 75% of the offer to QIBs (retail ≤ 10%, NII ≤ 15%).

How much of the company must be offered to the public?

25% (post-IPO capital up to ₹1,600 crore); an amount equal to ₹400 crore (₹1,600–₹4,000 crore); or 10% (₹4,000 crore and above). If below 25%, reach 25% within three years.

Can we do a pre-IPO placement?

Yes, between filing the DRHP and filing the RHP, up to a maximum number or amount disclosed in the DRHP; convertibles must convert before the RHP.

Promoters and lock-ins

Who is a promoter?

A person named as promoter in the offer document or annual return, who has control, or whose advice the board is accustomed to follow. Certain institutions are exempt.

Can a company list without a promoter?

Yes, if no one controls it (a “professionally managed” company). It is a factual test decided case by case.

How much must promoters contribute and lock in?

20% of post-IPO fully diluted capital, locked in for three years; the rest of promoters’ shares for one year.

Are other shareholders locked in?

Yes: all pre-IPO shares held by non-promoters are locked in for one year, with exemptions for ESOP shares and for VC/AIF/FVCI shares (one year from purchase).

Who can sell in an OFS?

Holders who have held the shares for at least one year before the DRHP, with exceptions (government/infrastructure entities, court-approved scheme shares, certain bonus shares).

Investors, price and bidding

Who are QIBs?

Institutions such as mutual funds, FPIs, banks, insurers, public financial institutions, AIFs, VCFs, FVCIs and large provident/pension funds.

What is a retail investor?

An individual (including HUFs and NRIs) bidding for up to ₹2 lakh in any option.

How is the offer split among investors?

Qualifying issuers: retail ≥ 35%, NII ≥ 15%, QIB ≤ 50%. Others: retail ≤ 10%, NII ≤ 15%, QIB ≥ 75%.

Who is an anchor investor?

A QIB bidding at least ₹10 crore, allocated before the issue opens; up to 60% of the QIB portion; one-third for mutual funds; 30-day lock-in.

What is ASBA?

Application Supported by Blocked Amount: the money stays in your bank account (blocked) until allotment. Mandatory for all but anchor investors.

How is the price decided in book building?

Bids at prices within the band are collected; after closing, the issuer, sellers and BRLMs analyse the demand and set the Issue Price and category allocations.

What happens to bids below the final price?

Only the maximum number of shares bid at or above the Issue Price counts; the rest are rejected.

What is a Green Shoe Option?

An over-allotment of up to 15% from borrowed shares with 30 days of stabilisation purchases by a BRLM. Rarely used in India.

Process, documents and liability

How long is SEBI’s final observation valid?

Twelve months.

Who are the intermediaries?

BRLMs, registrar, escrow collection bank, sponsor bank, advertising agency, monitoring agency (if applicable), syndicate members, SCSBs, brokers, CDPs, RTAs, auditors and legal counsel.

When is a monitoring agency needed?

If the fresh issue (excluding OFS) exceeds ₹100 crore.

What agreements are signed?

Issue, registrar and service-provider agreements before the DRHP; cash escrow, share escrow (OFS) and syndicate agreements before the RHP; underwriting agreement after pricing.

How old can the financial statements be?

Not more than six months old at the date of the offer document and the Issue Opening Date.

What happens if the offer document is misleading?

Civil and criminal liability under the Companies Act, SEBI enforcement, BRLM liability, and private actions by investors.

Can SEBI reject an offer document?

Yes, if disclosure is inadequate (vague objects, complex business, unidentified promoters). The issuer is then barred for at least a year.

Publicity, governance and after listing

Can management give interviews during an IPO?

Not about the IPO, financial forecasts or valuation, and only after consulting legal counsel. Previously scheduled interviews are allowed if the IPO is not discussed.

When can research be published?

Not from the day before DRHP filing (black-out), nor for 40 days after pricing; 25 days from opening if a BRLM underwrites; 15 days around a lock-up.

What governance must be in place at filing?

Independent directors, committees, at least one woman director, independent director on material subsidiaries, and policies under the Listing Regulations.

What are the results deadlines?

Quarterly results within 45 days; last quarter and annual results within 60 days.

Can we change the use of IPO proceeds?

Yes, with a special resolution by postal ballot and an exit offer for dissenting shareholders.

Other routes

What is a QIP?

A private placement to QIBs only by a listed company: special resolution, one-year listing, six-month gap, discount up to 5%.

What is a fast-track rights issue?

A rights issue where the Letter of Offer is filed with the exchanges, not SEBI, for companies meeting 13 listing, liquidity and compliance conditions.

How is a preferential issue priced?

At not less than the higher of the 26-week and 2-week average of weekly high and low VWAP, measured from the relevant date.

What do I need to list NCDs?

A credit rating, a debenture trustee, dematerialisation with both depositories, a disclosure document or prospectus, exchange approvals and a debt listing agreement.

What are REITs and InvITs?

SEBI-registered trusts that own real estate (REITs) or infrastructure assets (InvITs) and issue listed units to investors.
