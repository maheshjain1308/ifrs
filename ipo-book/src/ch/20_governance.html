Governance first, listing second

A listed company must comply with the Corporate Governance requirements of the SEBI Listing Regulations. The handbook is explicit that an issuer should be in compliance at the time of filing the DRHP with SEBI and the stock exchanges. This catches many first-time issuers by surprise: they assume they have until listing day. In practice, this means the board and committees must be reconstituted, and policies adopted, months before the IPO launch.

In concrete terms, the issuer must:

  1. appoint independent directors;
  2. constitute the necessary committees — audit, nomination and remuneration, stakeholders’ relationship, risk management (if applicable) and corporate social responsibility;
  3. appoint at least one woman director;
  4. appoint at least one independent director on the issuer’s board to the boards of its material subsidiaries; and
  5. formulate the policies prescribed under the Listing Regulations (for example, materiality of related-party transactions, determination of materiality of events, archival and a whistleblower / vigil mechanism, and the insider trading codes).

Composition of the board

The board must be a mix of executive and non-executive directors, with not less than 50% non-executive directors. The proportion of independent directors depends on who chairs the board:

ChairmanIndependent directors required
Non-executiveAt least one-third of the board
ExecutiveAt least one-half of the board
Non-executive and a promoter, or related to a promoter or to a person in management on or below the boardAt least one-half of the board
Worked example — sizing the board

Cedar Industries has a board of nine directors. How many independent directors does it need?

ScenarioCalculationIndependent directors
Chairman is a non-executive, non-promoter professional1/3 × 9 = 3.03
Chairman is the executive promoter-managing director1/2 × 9 = 4.5 → round up5
Chairman is the non-executive father of the promoter1/2 × 9 = 4.5 → round up5

In every scenario, at least 50% of the board (five of nine) must also be non-executive; independent directors count as non-executive, so the second and third scenarios satisfy both tests with five independents. The first scenario needs at least two further non-executive directors (promoter nominees or investor nominees) besides the three independents to reach five non-executives.

Constitution of the committees

CommitteeSize and compositionChair
Audit CommitteeMinimum three directors; two-thirds independentIndependent director
Nomination and Remuneration CommitteeAt least three directors, all non-executive; at least half independentIndependent director
Stakeholders’ Relationship CommitteeAt least three directors, with at least one independent directorNon-executive director
Risk Management Committee (if applicable)Majority of members from the boardA member of the board

The audit committee is the most demanding: it oversees financial reporting, internal controls, related-party transactions and the auditors’ independence, and in an IPO it reviews the draft financial information. A well-run audit committee with relevant financial expertise is the best evidence of governance quality.

Memorandum and Articles of Association

The issuer may have to amend its constitutional documents before filing:

  • the memorandum of association, to alter the capital clause for the new capital structure;
  • the memorandum and articles, to conform with the Companies Act and rules, if not already done;
  • the articles, to conform to the articles mandated by the stock exchanges; and
  • to amend or delete special, management or ownership rights that some shareholders hold — such as information rights or veto rights — before listing. A listed company cannot give some shareholders such rights, because all shareholders must stand on an equal footing.
Practical note — shareholders’ agreements

Venture and private-equity investors usually hold special rights under shareholders’ agreements that must fall away on listing. Typically, the agreement provides for automatic termination at the IPO, with specific rights (such as board nomination) surviving only in a form permitted for listed companies. Raise this with each investor early: their consent to the new articles and to the IPO itself is often needed.

Outstanding convertible securities

As explained in Chapter 4, an issuer cannot undertake an IPO if there are outstanding convertible securities or other rights entitling anyone to receive equity shares, except (i) options granted to employees under an employee stock option scheme (whether the employees are currently employed or not), and (ii) fully paid-up convertible securities that will be converted on or before filing of the RHP. The governance review must therefore include a clean-up of the capital structure — preference shares, convertible notes, warrants and side letters.

Governance readiness checklist

  • Board size and independent-director count meet the chairman-based test
  • At least one woman director appointed
  • Audit Committee, Nomination and Remuneration Committee, Stakeholders’ Relationship Committee constituted correctly; Risk Management Committee if applicable; CSR committee
  • At least one independent director of the issuer on the board of each material subsidiary
  • Required policies adopted (related-party transactions, materiality, whistleblower, insider trading, dividend, etc.)
  • Memorandum and articles amended and conform to exchange requirements
  • Special shareholder rights terminated or amended to listed-company form
  • No outstanding convertibles other than permitted ESOPs and convertibles to be converted before RHP
  • Company secretary and compliance officer in place
Since 2019

SEBI’s governance requirements have continued to expand after the Kotak Committee reforms — for example on the minimum number of directors, independent-director appointments and approval of related-party transactions — and the Listing Regulations have been amended many times. Re-check every committee and board rule against the current regulation.

Chapter summary

  • Governance must be in place when the DRHP is filed, not only at listing.
  • Board: at least 50% non-executive; one-third independent if the chairman is non-executive, one-half if the chairman is executive or a promoter-related non-executive.
  • Committees have prescribed sizes, independence thresholds and chairs; the audit committee requires two-thirds independent members.
  • Amend the memorandum and articles, remove special shareholder rights and clean up convertible securities before filing.
