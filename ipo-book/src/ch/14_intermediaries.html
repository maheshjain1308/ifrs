An IPO is a team effort. SEBI-registered intermediaries run the process, verify the disclosures, handle the money and carry the application forms to the stock exchange. Understanding each role helps an issuer allocate responsibility, manage cost and recognise where legal exposure lies.

Intermediaries appointed by the issuer

Book Running Lead Managers (BRLMs)

The issuer must appoint one or more merchant bankers registered under the SEBI (Merchant Bankers) Regulations, 1992 as BRLMs, and at least one of them must be the lead merchant banker. Their role is central.

  • Gatekeeping. A DRHP can be filed with SEBI only through a BRLM.
  • Independence. If a BRLM is an associate of the issuer, its role is limited to marketing the IPO. At least one BRLM must not be an associate of the issuer.
  • Due diligence. BRLMs are expected to exercise due diligence and must file a due diligence certificate with SEBI along with the DRHP and at later stages.
  • Structuring and drafting. They help structure the transaction, draft the DRHP, RHP and Prospectus, and advise on the appointment of the other intermediaries.
  • Interface. They deal with SEBI and the stock exchanges throughout — receiving SEBI’s observations and the in-principle and final listing approvals — and help the issuer organise roadshows.
  • Continuing responsibility. Responsibility continues until completion of the IPO and afterwards for IPO-related matters, such as handling of investor grievances (share certificates, credit to demat accounts, refunds), and monitoring the flow of applications from syndicate members, collecting banks and SCSBs.

Registrar to the IPO

The Registrar, an entity registered with SEBI, processes and reconciles the application forms received from the syndicate, SCSBs and other intermediaries, and coordinates allotment and refunds. It must accurately maintain physical and electronic bid data, including the forms received. In practice the registrar prepares the basis of allotment, runs the allotment process and deals with investors’ queries after the issue.

Escrow Collection Bank(s)

A scheduled bank registered under the SEBI (Bankers to an Issue) Regulations, 1994, it acts as escrow agent for the application money received from anchor investors. The issuer opens the Escrow Accounts with it. It also handles refunds of excess amounts. The Escrow Account is the account from which money is either refunded to applicants or transferred to the Public Issue Account.

Sponsor Bank(s)

Appointed by the issuer, a Sponsor Bank is a banker to the issue that acts as the conduit between the stock exchanges and the National Payments Corporation of India to push the mandate-collect request and payment instructions in the UPI mechanism (Chapter 12).

Advertising agency

It provides advertising and public-relations services for the IPO and gives the BRLMs the information they need to submit a compliance certificate to SEBI on certain news reports, as required under Regulation 42 read with Schedule IX of the SEBI Regulations (Chapter 19).

Monitoring agency

If the size of the IPO, excluding the Offer For Sale component, exceeds ₹1,000 million (₹100 crore), the proceeds must be monitored by a public financial institution or a scheduled commercial bank named as banker to the issuer in the DRHP, RHP and Prospectus. It submits reports to the issuer in the prescribed format at prescribed intervals. The point is to make sure the money is actually spent on the stated objects.

Syndicate members

SEBI-registered entities permitted to carry on business as underwriters. They collect application forms from applicants (other than retail individual investors) during the IPO period and are bound by the syndicate agreement with the BRLMs.

Statutory auditors

The issuer’s auditors play a distinctive role in an IPO, going beyond the annual audit.

  • They audit and restate the issuer’s financial statements and deliver a report for the offer documents. The financial statements are prepared under the Companies Act and applicable accounting standards, and are restated as required by the SEBI Regulations so that all periods are comparable.
  • They prepare a statement of special tax benefits, if any, available to the issuer, its material subsidiaries and its shareholders.
  • Under market practice they deliver comfort letters to the BRLMs at various stages. These give “negative assurance” (nothing has come to their attention suggesting adverse changes) on line items after the date of the last audit, and confirm financial and operating data in the offer documents.
  • The statutory auditors are required to be peer reviewed.

Legal counsel

Though not a regulated intermediary, legal counsel is indispensable. The issuer’s counsel and the BRLMs’ counsel run legal due diligence, draft and negotiate the agreements and advise on structuring. Legal counsels also pre-clear advertisements and research reports (Chapter 19).

Intermediaries not appointed by the issuer

SCSBs, registered brokers, CDPs, RTAs and syndicate members collect application forms from applicants during the issue period, enter details into the exchange’s electronic bidding system and do a preliminary verification. Retail individual investors could, in the handbook’s framework, submit bids only with the SCSBs (with UPI added from 2019). The issuer does not enter into agreements with these intermediaries; deemed agreements arise from SEBI circulars and are recognised in the issue agreement (Chapter 15).

Practical note — choosing and managing the team

Typical selection factors for BRLMs are sector experience, distribution strength with domestic and foreign institutions, research reach and the quality of the individual deal team. Fees are not fixed in the regulations; they are set in separate engagement letters, and the obligations of BRLMs under the issue agreement are typically several and not joint — each BRLM answers for its own obligations. Appoint a coordinating BRLM and a clear working-group structure; a transaction with six banks and four law firms needs explicit ownership of every deliverable.

Chapter summary

  • BRLMs (SEBI-registered merchant bankers) run the IPO and certify due diligence; at least one must not be an associate of the issuer; their duty continues after listing.
  • The registrar reconciles applications and runs allotment; escrow and sponsor banks hold anchor money and enable UPI; a monitoring agency is required where fresh issue size exceeds ₹100 crore.
  • Statutory auditors restate the accounts, prepare the tax-benefit statement and deliver comfort letters.
  • SCSBs, brokers, CDPs and RTAs collect bids but are not appointed by the issuer.
