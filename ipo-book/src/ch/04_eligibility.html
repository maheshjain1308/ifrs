Eligibility has two parts. First, the issuer must not fall into any of the categories that bar it from the market altogether. Second, it must satisfy one of the tests on financial track record. This chapter takes them in that order.

Part 1: Who is barred?

An unlisted issuer covered by any one of the following cannot undertake an IPO:

  1. Debarment of the issuer or its people. The issuer, any of its directors, its promoters, its promoter group, or any selling shareholder is debarred from accessing the capital markets by SEBI.
  2. Contagion through directorships. A director or promoter of the issuer is also a promoter or director of another company that is debarred from the capital markets under a SEBI order or direction.
  3. Wilful default. The issuer, or any of its directors or promoters, has been declared a wilful defaulter by a bank or financial institution (or a consortium of them) in accordance with the Reserve Bank of India’s guidelines.
  4. Fugitive economic offender. Any director or promoter has been declared a fugitive economic offender under the Fugitive Economic Offenders Act, 2018.
  5. Outstanding convertibles or option rights. There are outstanding convertible securities, or any other right that gives any person an option to receive equity shares of the issuer.

The fifth bar is the one that most often surprises companies, because it is a matter of capital-structure housekeeping rather than of reputation. Convertible preference shares, convertible debentures, warrants and similar rights must be resolved before the offer document is finalised. Two exceptions soften the rule:

  • outstanding options granted to employees (whether still employed or not) under a stock-option scheme; and
  • fully paid-up convertible securities that will be converted before filing of the Red Herring Prospectus (in a book-built issue) or the Prospectus (in a fixed-price issue).
Warning

Items (a) to (d) look at people, so a problem with any one director or promoter can make the whole company ineligible. Screen every director, promoter, promoter-group entity and selling shareholder early, including their directorships in other companies. A problem found in the middle of the process costs months.

Part 2: The track-record route

An unlisted issuer may undertake an IPO if it satisfies all four of the conditions below. They are tested on a restated and consolidated basis — that is, on the figures as adjusted for consistent accounting policies and presentation in the offer document, covering the issuer and its subsidiaries.

Track-record conditions (as at 31 July 2019)
ConditionRequirementIn ₹ crore
Net tangible assetsAt least ₹30 million in each of the preceding three full years (12 months each), of which not more than 50% may be held in monetary assets₹3 crore
Operating profitAverage of at least ₹150 million over the preceding three full years, with operating profit in each of those years₹15 crore
Net worthAt least ₹10 million in each of the three full years₹1 crore
Name changeIf the name changed within the last one year, at least 50% of revenue for the preceding full year (restated, consolidated) must come from the activity indicated by the new name—

Understanding the monetary-assets limit

Net tangible assets are meant to represent real, operating substance. A company that holds most of its net tangible assets as cash and cash-like items could look eligible without having a business behind the numbers. The 50% cap addresses that. If more than 50% of net tangible assets are held in monetary assets, the issuer must have made firm commitments to utilise the excess monetary assets in its business or project. And if the entire IPO is an Offer For Sale, the 50% limit does not apply, because no fresh money is going into the company.

Why “each year” matters

Three conditions use the words “in each” of the three years. A single weak year can disqualify a company even when the three-year average is comfortable. Operating profit is the strictest: the average must reach ₹15 crore and every year must be positive.

Net worth: all years exceed ₹1 crore ✔.
Conclusion: Nova qualifies, provided it documents firm commitments for the excess monetary assets (unless the issue is a pure OFS).

Part 3: What if the issuer does not meet the track-record tests?

A growing company may fail the three-year profit test — because it is young, investing heavily in growth, or loss-making — and still be a serious business. The regulations do not close the door. The handbook’s answer appears in its allocation rule: an issuer that does not meet the track-record conditions can still undertake a book-built IPO, but the share of the offer that must go to institutions rises sharply.

Key facts — the two regimes
  • Meets the track-record conditions: not less than 35% to retail individual investors, not less than 15% to non-institutional investors, not more than 50% to QIBs.
  • Does not meet them: not more than 10% to retail, not more than 15% to non-institutional investors, not less than 75% to QIBs.

The logic is investor protection. Retail investors are the least equipped to assess an unproven business, so the regulator steers the bulk of such an offer to sophisticated institutions that can analyse the risk. Chapter 9 explains the allocation mechanics in detail.

Practical note — choosing between the two regimes

Even a company that meets the track-record tests may choose its allocation structure within the permitted bands, and a company that cannot meet them must plan its marketing around institutional demand: roadshows, anchor investors and a strong equity story matter more. Bankers will usually test institutional appetite informally before the DRHP is filed.

Since 2019

The core track-record thresholds (₹3 crore net tangible assets, ₹15 crore average operating profit, ₹1 crore net worth) have remained recognisable in later amendments. The institutional route has been used extensively by loss-making and high-growth businesses. Verify the current text before relying on any figure.

Eligibility checklist

  • No SEBI debarment against issuer, directors, promoters, promoter group or selling shareholders
  • No director or promoter linked to another debarred company
  • No wilful-defaulter declaration against issuer, directors or promoters
  • No fugitive-economic-offender declaration against directors or promoters
  • No outstanding convertibles or option rights (except permitted ESOPs and convertibles to be converted before RHP/Prospectus)
  • Net tangible assets, operating profit and net worth tests met in each required year, or institutional route chosen
  • Monetary assets ≤ 50% of net tangible assets, or firm commitments made, or issue is a pure OFS
  • If the name changed within the last year: 50% revenue test satisfied

Chapter summary

  • Five disqualifications bar an issuer completely — debarment, contagion through directorships, wilful default, fugitive-economic-offender status and outstanding convertibles or option rights.
  • The track-record route needs ₹3 crore NTA (≤ 50% monetary), ₹15 crore average operating profit (each year positive) and ₹1 crore net worth, each over three years on a restated, consolidated basis.
  • A name change within the last year triggers a 50% revenue test.
  • Issuers outside the track-record route may still list, but with at least 75% of the offer reserved for QIBs.
