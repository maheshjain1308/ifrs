Besides the offer document, an IPO is held together by a series of contracts. They allocate responsibilities, define conditions under which bankers will go ahead, fix indemnities and describe how money moves. Knowing what each does — and when it is signed — is essential for managing the timetable.

The agreements, one by one

1. Issue Agreement (offer agreement)

Entered into among the BRLMs, the issuer and the selling shareholders (if any), before the DRHP is filed with SEBI. It sets out:

  • the roles and responsibilities of the BRLMs;
  • conditions precedent to the BRLMs’ obligations;
  • representations and warranties from the issuer, the selling shareholders and the BRLMs;
  • the indemnity given by the issuer (and sellers) to the BRLMs; and
  • termination rights.

The BRLMs’ obligations are typically several and not joint. Fees are not in this agreement; they sit in separate engagement letters. If the IPO has an OFS component, selling shareholders are also parties.

2. Escrow agreements

AgreementPartiesPurpose
Cash escrow agreementIssuer, selling shareholders (if any), BRLMs, Escrow Collection Banks, RegistrarGoverns collection of application money from anchor investors; arrangement for moving funds from the Escrow Account to the refund account, the Public Issue Account or the sellers’ account
Share escrow agreement (OFS only)Issuer, selling shareholders, escrow agentDeposit of the shares being sold in the OFS, typically before the RHP is filed, so they are available for transfer to allottees

3. Syndicate Agreement

Sets out the obligations between the BRLMs and the rest of the syndicate. Syndicate members collect bids from applicants other than retail investors and deposit them with SCSBs. The agreement lists each member’s role and obligations; the issuer (and the sellers) are confirming parties.

4. Underwriting Agreement

Entered into among the issuer, the selling shareholders and the underwriters after the IPO price is determined but before the Prospectus is filed with the RoC. The underwriters agree to ensure payment or subscription for the shares allocated to investors they have procured; if any investor defaults, the underwriter must find purchasers or buy the shares to the extent of the default. It also contains conditions precedent, representations, covenants and indemnities.

5. Registrar Agreement

Among the issuer, selling shareholders and the Registrar: the appointment of the registrar and its rights and obligations — issuing procedural instructions, rejecting invalid bids, helping finalise the basis of allotment and coordinating refunds.

6. Service Provider / Advertising Agency Agreement

Among the issuer, the sellers, the BRLMs and the advertising agency: it sets out the agency’s services in advertising and media relations, and requires it to provide certification so the BRLMs can submit their advertisement compliance certificate to SEBI.

7. Monitoring Agency Agreement

Between the issuer and the monitoring agency, defining the agency’s obligations to monitor the use of IPO proceeds. Needed where the size of the IPO (excluding OFS) exceeds ₹100 crore.

8. Deemed agreements

The issuer does not sign agreements with SCSBs, Registrar and Share Transfer Agents, CDPs and registered brokers. It must however take cognisance of the deemed agreements arising from SEBI’s rules, typically by clauses in the issue agreement.

Certificates and comfort letters

The contracts are supported by a stack of confirmations, which BRLMs rely on to discharge their due diligence duty:

  • Standard certificates from the issuer, its directors, subsidiaries, promoters, promoter group, group companies and selling shareholders, executed to give the underwriters a back-up for due diligence.
  • Comfort letters and certificates from the statutory auditors, delivered at several points, and refreshed as the offer document is updated (Chapter 14).
  • The due diligence certificate of the BRLMs, filed with SEBI.
  • Consents of experts and other persons named in the offer document.
Warning — the indemnity

The indemnity in the issue and underwriting agreements is broad. The issuer (and sellers) agree to compensate the BRLMs and underwriters for losses arising out of the offer document. Because the issuer’s directors, promoters and selling shareholders sign the representations behind it, negotiation of its scope, caps and exceptions deserves senior attention, not just legal drafting time.

Practical note — conditions precedent and termination

Underwriting and issue agreements typically allow the bankers to terminate if certain events occur between signing and closing — material adverse change, market disruption, loss of an approval. Issuers should therefore keep the gap between pricing and allotment as short as possible, and should understand which events are in their control.

Chapter summary

  • The Issue Agreement (before DRHP) is the core contract; cash and share escrow, syndicate and underwriting agreements follow as the timetable proceeds.
  • Underwriting is signed after pricing and before the Prospectus is filed with the RoC.
  • No agreement is signed with SCSBs, RTAs, CDPs and brokers; deemed agreements are recognised in the issue agreement.
  • A web of certificates and comfort letters backs up the BRLMs’ due diligence.
