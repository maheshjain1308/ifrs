The handbook behind this book was updated until 31 July 2019. Indian securities regulation moves quickly: SEBI amends the ICDR Regulations several times a year and issues circulars that change procedure. The items below are drawn from the author’s general knowledge of later developments, not from the handbook. They are indicative, not exhaustive, and the detailed parameters (percentages, periods, thresholds) should always be confirmed in the current text of the regulations and circulars before they are used.

The big picture

Since 2019, the direction of change has been consistent: faster timelines, more digital processes, more room for new-economy companies, tighter rules on the use of proceeds and on promoters’ exits, and shorter lock-ins. The basic architecture of the IPO — DRHP, SEBI observations, RHP, book building, ASBA, Prospectus — has been preserved.

What did not change

  • The need for restated, audited financials, a due diligence certificate and SEBI observations on the DRHP.
  • The role of SEBI-registered merchant bankers as BRLMs, registrars, bankers and auditors.
  • The book-building structure with a price band, and the basic QIB / NII / retail architecture.
  • Core liability provisions under the Companies Act.
  • The principle that equity listing is possible only by a public issue.

A verification routine

  1. Identify the instrument. For an IPO, start with the current consolidated SEBI (ICDR) Regulations, 2018, and its schedules.
  2. Read the latest circulars. SEBI issues master circulars that consolidate procedures. Check any circular relating to the stage you are at: filing, bidding, allotment, listing.
  3. Check the exchanges. Listing requirements and bidding procedures are also in BSE and NSE notices.
  4. Check the Companies Act and rules. MCA notifications may change prospectus or private placement mechanics.
  5. Confirm with counsel. Ask the BRLMs’ and issuer’s counsel to confirm the current rule against the specific fact pattern and to flag any pending amendments.
Where to look

Primary sources: the SEBI website (legal framework: regulations, circulars, master circulars); BSE and NSE websites (listing and public-issue notices); the Ministry of Corporate Affairs (Companies Act, rules and notifications); and the Gazette of India for notified amendments. Secondary sources: law-firm updates and practitioner commentaries — useful for explanation but never a substitute for the text.

Chapter summary

  • The handbook reflects 31 July 2019; many numbers have since been amended.
  • Main trends: shorter timelines (T+3), digital bidding (UPI), refined NII and anchor rules, shorter lock-ins, caps on certain objects, confidential filing and wider access for new-age companies.
  • Always confirm current parameters in the regulations, circulars and exchange notices.
