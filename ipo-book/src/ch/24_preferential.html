What is a preferential issue?

A preferential issue is an issue of specified securities by a listed issuer to a select group of persons — in preference to all the existing shareholders — in accordance with the Companies Act and the SEBI Regulations. It is the instrument for bringing in a strategic investor, converting dues into equity, increasing the promoters’ holding or paying for an acquisition with shares.

Eligibility conditions

  1. A special resolution of the shareholders, among other things specifying the class of persons to whom the allotment is proposed.
  2. Compliance with the conditions for continuous listing under the SEBI Listing Regulations.
  3. All equity shares already held by the proposed allottees in the issuer must be in dematerialised form.
  4. All equity shares allotted must be fully paid-up at the time of allotment.
  5. Subject to certain exceptions, no preferential issue may be made to any person who has sold or transferred any equity shares of the issuer during the six months preceding the relevant date.

Completion within 15 days

The allotment under the special resolution must be completed within 15 days from the date of the resolution. If it is not, the issuer must pass a fresh special resolution. The short window keeps the pricing in line with market reality.

Pricing

The “relevant date” is 30 days prior to the date of the shareholders’ resolution approving the issue. If the shares have been listed for 26 weeks or more as on the relevant date, the price must be not less than the higher of:

  1. the average of the weekly high and low of the volume-weighted average price (VWAP) during the 26 weeks preceding the relevant date; or
  2. the average of the weekly high and low of the VWAP during the two weeks preceding the relevant date.

If the shares have been listed for less than 26 weeks, the price at which the shares were issued in the IPO (or the value per share determined in the scheme of compromise, arrangement or amalgamation under which the shares were listed) must also be considered.

Worked example — floor price for a preferential allotment

Tara Chemicals Ltd, listed for several years, plans a preferential issue to a strategic investor. Using exchange data before the relevant date:

MeasureResult (₹)
Average of weekly high and low of VWAP over the 26 weeks148.00
Average of weekly high and low of VWAP over the 2 weeks156.00
Minimum price = higher of the two156.00

Even though the longer-term average is ₹148, the shorter-term average is higher, so the floor is ₹156. If the share price has just risen sharply, the two-week average protects existing shareholders against a cheap issue; if it has just fallen, the 26-week average protects them against an issue priced at a temporary low.

Timing check: if the shareholders’ resolution is on 30 September, the relevant date is 31 August. The allotment must be completed by 15 October (15 days after the resolution).

Lock-in

  • Securities allotted on a preferential basis to the promoters or promoter group are locked in for three years from the date of trading approval, but not more than 20% of the total capital of the issuer is locked in for three years. Shares allotted in excess of 20% are locked in for one year from the date of trading approval.
  • Convertible securities or warrants that are not listed on the stock exchanges are locked in for one year from the date of allotment of the instrument.
Worked example — lock-in on a promoter preferential allotment

A company has total capital (post-issue) of 100 crore shares. Promoters receive 30 crore shares in a preferential allotment. Lock-in: 20% of total capital = 20 crore shares locked for three years; the remaining 10 crore shares are locked for one year.

Practical note — keeping the clock

Because the price depends on market data and the allotment must follow the resolution within 15 days, preferential issues run to a tight calendar: board approval, notice to shareholders (with explanatory statement), shareholders’ meeting, in-principle approval from the exchanges, receipt of funds and allotment. Plan the sequence so that the relevant date, the meeting date and the 15-day window fit together, and make sure that every allottee’s demat holdings and trading history are checked against the six-month rule.

Chapter summary

  • A preferential issue is a listed issuer’s allotment to a chosen group, approved by special resolution.
  • Allotment must be completed within 15 days of the resolution; otherwise a new resolution is needed.
  • Minimum price is the higher of the 26-week and 2-week averages (of weekly high and low VWAP) at the relevant date; IPO or scheme price is considered for recently listed shares.
  • Promoters’ shares are locked in for three years (up to 20% of total capital) and one year (excess); warrants for one year.
