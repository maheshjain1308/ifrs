What is a REIT?

A Real Estate Investment Trust (REIT) is a trust, set up under the Indian Trusts Act, 1882 and registered under the Registration Act, 1908, that invests in real-estate properties in accordance with the SEBI REIT Regulations. A REIT must be registered with SEBI. Other laws that apply include the FEMA and the 2017 non-resident securities regulations, and the Income-tax Act, 1961.

The idea is familiar from other markets: owners of large, rent-yielding properties — typically office parks and malls — transfer them into a trust, and investors buy units of the trust on the exchange. The trust distributes most of its rental income to unitholders. For investors, a REIT offers exposure to commercial real estate with liquidity and professional management; for developers it releases capital for new projects. Fiscal 2019 saw India’s first listed REIT (Chapter 1).

Eligibility criteria

Requirements
SponsorMinimum individual net worth ₹200 million (₹20 crore) and collective net worth ₹1,000 million (₹100 crore); at least five years’ experience in development of real estate or fund management in the real-estate industry; if the sponsor is a developer, at least two projects completed
ManagerIndividual net worth of ₹100 million (₹10 crore) if a company or body corporate, or net tangible assets of ₹100 million if an LLP; the manager or its associate must have five years’ experience in fund management, advisory services, property management or development; at least two employees with five years’ experience; at least 50% of directors (or governing board members) independent
TrusteeRegistered as a debenture trustee under SEBI Debenture Trustee Regulations; must not be an associate of the sponsor or manager

Offer and listing

A REIT can make its initial offer of units only by a public issue. Conditions include owning assets worth ₹5,000 million (₹500 crore) and a minimum offer size of ₹2,500 million (₹250 crore). Later issues can be by follow-on offer, preferential allotment, qualified institutional placement, rights issue, bonus issue, offer for sale or other mechanisms specified by SEBI.

Disclosure

As with an equity IPO, the REIT Regulations require detailed disclosure in the offer documents. But, unlike company IPOs, REIT offer documents must include forward-looking disclosures: properties intended to be acquired, estimated monthly market rent, expected completion of under-construction projects, and projections of cash flows and income for the next three years. The filing and IPO process is similar to an equity IPO.

Investment restrictions

Investments by the REIT, directly or through holding companies and special purpose vehicles, are restricted:

  • At least 80% of the value of the REIT’s assets must be invested in completed, rent-generating or income-generating properties, subject to conditions.
  • Not more than 20% may be invested in other permissible assets, including under-construction projects and completed but not rent-generating properties, in accordance with the REIT Regulations.
Worked example — testing the 80/20 rule

Crest REIT has assets valued at ₹6,000 crore: completed and fully leased office parks ₹4,700 crore; a completed mall not yet leased ₹600 crore; an under-construction tower ₹700 crore.

Rent-generating completed assets = ₹4,700 crore = 78.3% of ₹6,000 crore — below 80%. The permitted ceiling for the “other” bucket is 20% = ₹1,200 crore; but the actual other bucket is 600 + 700 = ₹1,300 crore (21.7%), exceeding the ceiling by ₹100 crore. To comply, the REIT could (i) lease the mall so that its value counts as rent-generating — raising the rent-generating share to (4,700 + 600) ÷ 6,000 = 88.3% — or (ii) acquire further stabilised, rent-generating assets of value X, where (4,700 + X) ÷ (6,000 + X) ≥ 80% requires X ≥ ₹500 crore.

Since 2019

Several REIT parameters have been revised since 2019 — including the minimum asset size and offer size, the permitted investment in under-construction assets, leverage and distribution rules and the introduction of a framework for small and medium REITs. Always check the current SEBI REIT Regulations.

Chapter summary

  • A REIT is a SEBI-registered trust owning income-producing real estate, with sponsor, manager and trustee, each meeting eligibility tests.
  • Initial units are offered by public issue: assets of ₹500 crore and a minimum offer of ₹250 crore.
  • Offer documents include three-year forward-looking projections.
  • At least 80% of value must be in completed, income-generating assets; no more than 20% in other permitted assets.
