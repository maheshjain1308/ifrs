The handbook describes an IPO as a sequence of five phases: (i) the period before filing the DRHP; (ii) filing the DRHP with SEBI; (iii) SEBI’s review and final observations; (iv) events after final observations, including filing and registration of the RHP with the Registrar of Companies; and (v) the issue period and the post-issue phase. For practical planning it divides these into six stages, shown below.

Stage 1: The period before filing the DRHP

  • Appoint the BRLMs and legal counsel. Engagement letters set fees; the working group is formed.
  • Kick-off meeting. Senior management gives the BRLMs and counsel an overview of the issuer and its business, and the IPO timetable is discussed.
  • Due diligence and drafting. The due diligence exercise starts together with drafting of the DRHP.
  • Agreements and certificates. The issue agreement, registrar agreement and service-provider agreement are executed. The issuer, its directors, subsidiaries, promoters, promoter group, group companies and selling shareholders sign standard certificates as back-up for the underwriters’ due diligence, and the auditors deliver a comfort letter and certificates.

Alongside, the company completes the preparatory work covered in earlier chapters: eligibility tests (Chapter 4), cap-table and lock-in planning (Chapter 7), board and committee set-up (Chapter 20), restated financials (Chapter 16), and corporate approvals (Chapter 5).

Stage 2: Filing the DRHP

  • The DRHP is filed with SEBI, through a BRLM and with the due diligence certificate.
  • Applications are filed with the stock exchanges for in-principle listing approval, together with the DRHP.

This stage does not apply to fast-track issues. The DRHP is typically made available for public comments on the websites of SEBI, the exchanges and the BRLMs.

Stage 3: SEBI review and final observations

  • SEBI issues interim observations; the issuer and BRLMs reply and update the DRHP.
  • SEBI issues its final observations, which are valid for twelve months.

The twelve-month validity gives the issuer a window: it can launch when the market is favourable, subject to refreshing the offer document. This stage too is not applicable in fast-track issues.

Stage 4: After final observations — RHP filed with the RoC

  • The issuer files its reply to the final observations and an updated DRHP (incorporating SEBI’s observations and updates required by the passage of time) with SEBI, and receives noting of the changes from SEBI.
  • The cash escrow agreement (and share escrow agreement if there is an OFS) and the syndicate agreement are executed.
  • Updated standard certificates and the comfort letter are delivered.
  • The RHP is filed with and registered by the RoC.

A book-built issue has a three-stage filing (DRHP, RHP, Prospectus); a fixed-price issue has two (DRHP, Prospectus). The RHP must be filed with the RoC at least three days before the issue opens.

Stage 5: The issue period

  • The issuer announces the price band at least two working days before the issue opens.
  • Anchor investors bid for one day only, a day before the issue opens.
  • The bidding period for all other investors is at least three working days.
  • Bidding closes. The Registrar obtains the electronic bid details from the exchange.
  • The Issue Price is finalised and the underwriting agreement is executed.
  • The Prospectus is filed with and registered by the RoC.

Stage 6: The post-issue period

  • The Registrar submits the final basis of allotment to the designated stock exchange for approval.
  • The Registrar and BRLMs issue funds-transfer instructions to the collecting banks and SCSBs, crediting funds into the Public Issue Account.
  • The issuer allots shares; credit to the demat accounts of successful bidders begins.
  • The issuer makes the listing and trading application to the exchanges; they grant final listing and trading approval; trading commences.

Practical note — plan backwards from the listing window

Because financial statements go stale after six months and SEBI’s observations lapse after twelve, the sensible way to plan is backwards from the intended listing window. Fix the target opening date, subtract the offer period, the RHP filing lead time and the updating work, and then check the latest date by which SEBI’s final observations and audited stub accounts must be in hand. Most slippage in IPOs comes from financial updates and SEBI queries, not from the offer period itself.

Chapter summary

  • An IPO runs through six stages: preparation, DRHP filing, SEBI review, RHP filing, issue period and post-issue.
  • SEBI’s final observation is valid for twelve months; the RHP is filed with the RoC at least three days before opening.
  • Price band two working days before opening; anchor bidding one day before; public bidding for at least three working days.
  • After closing: pricing, underwriting agreement, Prospectus, basis of allotment, allotment (within four working days), demat credit (within two), listing.
