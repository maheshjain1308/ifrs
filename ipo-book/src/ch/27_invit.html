What is an InvIT?

Like a REIT, an Infrastructure Investment Trust (InvIT) is a trust set up under the Indian Trusts Act, 1882 and registered under the Registration Act, 1908, but it invests in infrastructure projects — roads, power transmission, pipelines, renewable energy and similar — in accordance with the SEBI InvIT Regulations. It must be registered with SEBI, and the same additional laws (FEMA and the 2017 non-resident securities regulations, Income-tax Act) apply.

InvITs give the owners of mature, cash-generating infrastructure assets a way to recycle capital and give investors a yield-oriented product with predictable cash flows, which is why the handbook describes them as “the Indian equivalent of a business trust” and a “new asset class with a focus on yield”. In 2018 India saw the listing of the first privately placed InvIT, followed by two public InvITs (Chapter 1).

Parties

PartyRole
SponsorA company, LLP or body corporate that sets up the InvIT and is designated as such in the application
Investment managerA company, LLP or body corporate that manages the assets and investments of the InvIT and undertakes its activities
TrusteeHolds the InvIT’s assets in trust for the benefit of unitholders
Project managerResponsible for achieving execution of a project; in PPP projects, may be the entity responsible for execution or management under the concession agreement
AdvisersAuditors, valuers and merchant bankers for compliance with the InvIT Regulations

Note the difference from a REIT: an InvIT has a project manager as a separate role, reflecting that infrastructure projects often need execution and operation expertise distinct from financial asset management.

Offer and listing

An InvIT may offer and list its units if its asset size is at least ₹5,000 million (₹500 crore) and the offer size is at least ₹2,500 million (₹250 crore). The offering can be made through a private placement or a public offer.

Worked example — can this InvIT go public?

Northway Roads InvIT proposes to hold assets worth ₹2,000 crore: operating toll roads ₹1,500 crore; roads under construction ₹500 crore.

Revenue-generating share = 1,500 ÷ 2,000 = 75% — below 80%. Under-construction share = 500 ÷ 2,000 = 25% — above the 10% cap for public InvITs.

Conclusion: it does not qualify for a public offer. It can, however, be a privately placed InvIT because under-construction exposure above 10% forces the private route. If it invests at least 80% of asset value in eligible infrastructure projects (here, 100%), minimum investment per investor would be ₹25 crore, and it needs 5–1,000 institutional investors. To go public later, it would need to complete its under-construction roads or restructure the portfolio so that at least 80% of value is in completed and revenue-generating projects and under-construction assets do not exceed 10%.

Disclosure requirements

The InvIT Regulations prescribe limited disclosures compared with an equity IPO: material litigation and regulatory actions, summary financial statements and projections for three years. Like REIT documents, InvIT offer documents must include forward-looking disclosures — projected revenue and operating cash flows over the next three years, details of the holdcos or project SPVs, and expected completion of under-construction projects. The process for a public InvIT is similar to an equity IPO; for a private InvIT it is similar to a QIP.

Chapter summary

  • An InvIT is a SEBI-registered trust owning infrastructure assets, with sponsor, investment manager, trustee and project manager.
  • Asset size at least ₹500 crore and offer size at least ₹250 crore; public or private offering.
  • Public InvITs: 80% in completed, revenue-generating assets, under-construction ≤ 10%, at least 25% public, 75% minimum subscription, 20+ subscribers.
  • Private InvITs: 5–1,000 institutional investors, minimum ₹1 crore (₹25 crore where 80%+ is invested).
