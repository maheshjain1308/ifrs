Routes and rules

Listed debt is raised in India through non-convertible debentures (NCDs) — debt instruments that are not convertible into equity — which a company can list on a recognised stock exchange whether it issues them to the public or places them privately with selected investors. The governing rules are the SEBI Debt Regulations, which apply to public issues of NCDs and to private placements of NCDs that are to be listed; the Companies Act and rules; and requirements prescribed by the stock exchanges on which the NCDs will be listed.

Corporate approvals

  • Private placement. Board authorisation. The company also needs shareholder approval for its borrowing limits under Section 180(1)(c) of the Companies Act. If the NCDs are secured, shareholder approval under Section 180(1)(a) (to create security over its assets) is needed.
  • Public issue. Similar requirements to a private placement.

Disclosure requirements

For a private placement, the offer document must contain the disclosures specified in Schedule I of the SEBI Debt Regulations, the latest annual report and the private placement offer cum application letter in the PAS Rules form.

For a public issue, the document must contain Schedule I disclosures and those under Chapter III of the Companies Act. The handbook notes a transitional wrinkle: the Companies (Amendment) Act, 2017 removed the prospectus disclosure requirements of Section 26, and the 2018 amendments removed some disclosures under the PAS Rules; but BSE and NSE circulars of June 2018 said that, until the revised guidelines come into effect, issuers must keep complying with the Companies Act prospectus disclosures as they stood before the amendments.

The statutory auditors deliver a report on the audited financial statements, reformatted to show the last five years together in a columnar manner for the draft prospectus and the prospectus, prepared per the Companies Act and applicable accounting standards. The process

Private placement

  1. Obtain corporate approvals for the issue and borrowing limits.
  2. Appoint intermediaries and finalise term sheets.
  3. Commence due diligence; obtain consents from existing lenders, regulators, statutory authorities and third parties.
  4. Finalise the disclosure document under the SEBI Debt Regulations and the PAS Rules.
  5. Obtain in-principle approval from the exchange.
  6. Sign tripartite agreements with the depositories and obtain the ISINs (open demat accounts).
  7. Sign the debt listing agreement with the exchange.
  8. Issue the disclosure document and application forms (serially numbered, addressed to named persons).
  9. Receive applications with subscription money through banking channels.
  10. Credit the NCDs into investors’ demat accounts.
  11. Obtain final listing approval.
  12. Maintain a complete record of the private placement offer in Form PAS-5.
  13. File the return of allotment within 15 days of allotment. The company may access the net issue proceeds only after filing the return of allotment.

Public issue

  1. Obtain corporate approvals; appoint intermediaries.
  2. Start due diligence and drafting of the draft prospectus; obtain consents from lenders, regulators and others; obtain certificates and consents from the issuer, directors and key managerial personnel, and relevant third parties.
  3. Execute the issue agreement, registrar agreement and debenture trustee agreement; obtain comfort letters from the current and previous statutory auditors and from the independent chartered accountant.
  4. File the draft prospectus with the designated exchange through the lead merchant banker, simultaneously forwarded to SEBI. It is posted on the exchange’s website for seven working days for public comments, and the lead merchant banker ensures that all comments are suitably addressed.
  5. Apply for and obtain in-principle approval from the exchanges; sign the debt listing agreement; execute the public issue account agreement and consortium agreement.
  6. File the prospectus with SEBI for its records along with the due diligence certificate; file the prospectus with the RoC and obtain its approval.
  7. The debenture trustee files its due diligence certificate before the issue opens.
  8. Publish an issue advertisement in a national daily newspaper with wide circulation.
  9. The issue opens and closes; applications are received.
  10. The issuer allots the NCDs in consultation with the designated exchange; file the final listing application; exchanges give final listing and trading approval.
  11. Execute the debenture trust deed within the applicable timelines.
Practical note — equity listing versus debt listing

Debt investors care about security and cash flows, not upside. A rating, a trustee and a clear security package matter more than the equity story. Debt listing is also the only route by which an unlisted company can list any securities without a public offer, and companies often list NCDs first and equity later. A listed debt issuer is bound by the Debt Listing Agreement and continuing disclosure norms even though its equity is unlisted.

Chapter summary

  • NCDs can be listed through a private placement or public issue under the SEBI Debt Regulations, the Companies Act and exchange rules.
  • A debenture trustee and a credit rating are mandatory; the debt must be dematerialised with both depositories.
  • Private placements follow an offer-letter process and need a return of allotment within 15 days before proceeds can be used; public issues need a draft prospectus with seven working days of public comments and RoC registration.
  • The debenture trust deed must be executed within 60 days of allotment.
