Purpose

An anchor investor is a QIB that commits to a large block of the offer before the issue opens to the public. Because anchors are large, experienced institutions, their participation tells the rest of the market that sophisticated investors have looked at the company and are willing to buy at the price. It also reduces execution risk for the issuer: a meaningful part of the QIB book is already placed before the retail and general bidding begins.

The handbook describes the option as follows: an issuer undertaking a public issue has the option of allocating up to 60% of the portion available to QIBs to anchor investors.

Pricing consequences

Anchors are allocated shares at a price fixed on the anchor day. The final IPO price is determined only after the public book closes, so the two prices may differ:

  • If the final IPO price is higher than the anchor allocation price, anchors must pay the difference.
  • If the final IPO price is lower, the excess is not refunded; the anchor receives shares at its allocation price.

This asymmetry means that anchors take the risk that the market will price below the anchor price. In practice the anchor price is usually the upper end of the price band or very close to it.

Who cannot be an anchor?

To keep anchor demand genuine, certain persons are excluded:

  • the BRLMs and any associate of a BRLM; and
  • any person related to the promoter or promoter group of the issuer.

The exclusion for BRLM associates has carve-outs for entities that are genuinely independent pools of money: mutual funds sponsored by associates of the BRLMs, insurance companies promoted by associates, and AIFs and FPIs (other than Category III FPIs) sponsored by entities associated with the BRLMs.

Confirmation of allocation note

At the end of the anchor bidding period, once the allocation is finalised, the anchors receive a Confirmation of Allocation Note (CAN) — an advice of allocation. Other investors receive an “allotment advice”. A CAN is not itself a share certificate; the shares are credited to the anchor’s demat account on allotment.

Worked example — an anchor book for a ₹500 crore QIB portion

Maple Healthcare Ltd has a QIB portion of ₹500 crore. It elects to allocate the maximum 60% to anchors: ₹300 crore. One-third (₹100 crore) must be reserved for domestic mutual funds.

Number of anchors. ₹300 crore exceeds ₹250 crore, so the third row of the table applies. For the first ₹250 crore, the number of anchors must be between five and 15; for the remaining ₹50 crore (“part thereof”), up to ten more may be added. The number of anchors can therefore be up to 25, with each allotted at least ₹5 crore.

Price. The anchors are allocated at ₹320 per share. After the public book closes, the issue is priced at ₹330. Anchors must pay the extra ₹10 per share. Had the issue been priced at ₹310, they would have kept their shares at ₹320 without any refund.

Lock-in. Anchor shares cannot be sold for 30 days after allotment.

Practical note — what the anchor book signals

Market participants read the anchor list as a quality signal. A balanced list — long-only mutual funds, insurers, sovereign and pension funds — is better than a book dominated by one type of investor. Anchor allocation also determines how much of the QIB book is left for the open period, so the issuer must keep enough supply for institutions that prefer to bid on the main days.

Since 2019

The anchor regime has been refined since 2019: the single 30-day lock-in was later replaced by a split lock-in (a portion for 30 days and the rest for 90 days), and the reservation for domestic mutual funds has been broadened in some respects. Check the current rules before planning an anchor book; see Chapter 29.

Chapter summary

  • Anchors are QIBs bidding at least ₹10 crore, up to 60% of the QIB portion, one day before the issue opens.
  • One-third of the anchor portion is reserved for Indian mutual funds; allocation is discretionary within limits on the number of anchors and a ₹5 crore minimum each.
  • Anchors pay any upward price difference; they receive no refund if the price falls.
  • BRLMs, their associates (with limited exceptions) and promoter-related persons are excluded; the lock-in was 30 days at 2019.
