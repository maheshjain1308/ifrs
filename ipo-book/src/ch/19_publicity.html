Investors must decide on the basis of the offer document, not on hype. Indian law therefore restricts what an issuer says publicly during an IPO and what research can be published about it. The rules sit in Schedule IX of the SEBI Regulations and in the SEBI Research Analysts Regulations, 2014. A breach can delay the IPO and may attract SEBI action.

Part A: Publicity

When do the restrictions apply?

The restrictions apply to all public communications and publicity material, including corporate advertisements and advertisements for products or services, from the date of commencement of the IPO process up to the date of allotment. If the offering also takes place in other jurisdictions such as the United States, those jurisdictions may impose further publicity restrictions, including different periods.

The pre-filing period

  • Advertisements must be consistent with past practice. If an advertisement is not consistent with past practice, it must carry a disclaimer that the issuer is proposing, subject to approvals, market conditions and other considerations, a public issue in the near future and is in the process of filing a DRHP with SEBI. The disclaimer must be legible and prominent, not disproportionate to the rest of the communication.
  • The issuer must ensure that all advertisements to be released (including past ones that are circulated again) are pre-cleared by the BRLMs and the legal counsel.
  • Advertisements must not refer to the IPO (other than the disclaimer), the valuation of the shares or future projections of financial performance.

The four mandatory advertisements

  1. Pre-issue advertisement. After registering the RHP (book-built) or the Prospectus (fixed price) with the RoC, the issuer publishes an advertisement in one English national daily, one Hindi national daily and one regional-language newspaper with wide circulation at the place of the registered office. It must comply with Section 30 of the Companies Act and Regulation 43 and Part A of Schedule X.
  2. Price band advertisement. The floor price or price band must be announced (Regulation 29(4)) in all newspapers in which the pre-issue advertisement appeared, at least two working days before the issue opens.
  3. Issue opening and closing advertisements. Optional but typically used (Regulation 43(3)): formats in Parts B and C of Schedule X.
  4. Post-issue advertisement. The BRLMs must ensure that an advertisement giving the details of subscription, basis of allotment, number, value and percentage of applications (including ASBA), number, value and percentage of successful allottees, the date of completion of dispatch of refund orders or instructions to SCSBs, the date of credit of securities and the date of filing the listing application, is released within ten days of completing the relevant activities (Regulation 51).
Warning — the “innocent” interview

The most common violations are not formal advertisements but casual remarks: a founder telling a journalist the company will “list this year”, a chief executive giving a growth forecast at a conference, an HR campaign on social media that mentions the offering. All of these can attract SEBI’s attention and have, in the market’s experience, delayed IPOs. The safe approach is a written communications protocol applying to every executive, signed off by counsel.

Part B: Research

Which law applies?

Research and related activity by research analysts must comply with the SEBI Research Analysts Regulations, 2014 (in force from 30 November 2014). The Research Analysts Regulations, the SEBI Regulations and the Companies Act together govern the preparation and dissemination of research reports connected with an IPO.

To whom do they apply?

  • the issuer;
  • its associate companies, promoters, directors, officers and affiliates, and each of their affiliates;
  • all persons acting on behalf of the issuer (including any public relations firm); and
  • the BRLMs, their connected analysts, the syndicate members and their affiliates and associates.

Black-out periods

The black-out period for the distribution of research reports typically begins at 12.00 a.m. IST on the day before the DRHP is filed with SEBI. Research reports cannot be distributed during:

PeriodRule
After pricing40 days immediately following the day on which the securities are priced in the IPO
If a BRLM underwrites25 days from the Issue Opening Date
Around a lock-up15 days before and 15 days after the expiry, termination or waiver of a lock-up arrangement (or entering into it) between the research analyst or entity and the issuer

Restrictions on research reports

  • BRLMs or affiliates may not distribute a research report anywhere in the world if the person responsible for it has had access to material non-public information that will not be in the offer documents.
  • A report must present itself as an outsider’s view and be independently produced; sources must be acknowledged.
  • Reports must not contain projections, expectations, forecasts, valuations or recommendations on the securities.
  • Reports are reviewed by counsel to the BRLMs before publication.
  • Any conflict arising from a relationship between the research entity and the issuer must be disclosed.

Dealing restrictions on analysts

  • Analysts employed by BRLMs, and their associates, must not trade in the issuer’s securities within 30 days before and five days after the date of the research report.
  • They must not trade in a manner contrary to their recommendations.
  • They must not purchase or receive securities of the issuer before the IPO if they follow companies principally engaged in the same type of business as the issuer.

Other jurisdictions

Some foreign laws (for example, the United States securities laws applicable to offshore marketing) have extra-territorial effect and impose their own research and publicity restrictions. Offerings with an international tranche should be reviewed by international counsel.

Key facts — publicity and research in one glance
  • Publicity restrictions run from commencement of the IPO process to allotment.
  • Pre-issue advertisement: English + Hindi + regional newspapers; price band two working days before opening; post-issue advertisement within ten days.
  • No projections, valuation opinions or IPO mentions; website must match the offer document.
  • Research black-out: from the day before DRHP filing; 40 days after pricing; 25 days after opening if a BRLM underwrites; 15 days either side of a lock-up event.

Chapter summary

  • Public communications must be consistent with the offer document, truthful and free of projections or valuations; counsel must pre-clear.
  • Four sets of advertisements: pre-issue, price band, opening/closing (optional) and post-issue.
  • Research analysts are regulated; black-out periods and dealing restrictions limit reports and trading around an IPO.
