About the source

This book is an independent study guide built from the Handbook on Listing in India, 3rd edition, published by Cyril Amarchand Mangaldas and updated until 31 July 2019. The structure, explanations, worked examples, diagrams, checklists, practice problems and commentary are new. The rules described come from the handbook; where the book adds material from outside the handbook, it is labelled (for example, “Since 2019” boxes and Chapter 29). The book is not endorsed by, and is not affiliated with, the original publisher.

Not legal advice

Securities law changes frequently. Almost every rule in this book is stated as it stood on 31 July 2019, and several have since been amended. Use this book to understand concepts and structure. Before taking any decision on a real transaction, check the current text of the SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2018, the SEBI (Listing Obligations and Disclosure Requirements) Regulations, 2015, the Companies Act, 2013, the relevant circulars, and consult qualified legal, tax and financial advisers. The author and publisher accept no liability for any loss arising from use of this book.

Amounts and conventions

The handbook states monetary thresholds in millions of rupees. This book gives them in rupees and converts to crore and lakh, which Indian readers use daily: ₹1 crore = ₹10 million = ₹100 lakh, and ₹1,000 million = ₹100 crore. “Fiscal” means the twelve months ending 31 March. “Working day” follows the usage in the SEBI regulations. Defined terms such as BRLM, DRHP, RHP, QIB, NII and ASBA are explained when first used and collected in the glossary in Appendix D.

Illustrations

All company names, prices, share counts and timelines used in worked examples and practice problems are hypothetical and are included only to show arithmetic and procedure.

Preface

For most founders, an initial public offering is the most consequential financial event in the life of a company. It converts a private business into a public one: its accounts, promoters, litigation, related-party dealings and strategy are laid open in a document that investors, regulators and the courts will read closely. It also opens a far larger pool of capital, gives shareholders a way to realise value, and imposes a permanent discipline of disclosure and governance.

In India that journey is governed by a dense mesh of statutes, SEBI regulations, stock-exchange requirements and market practice. The rules are not secret, but they are scattered, written for lawyers, and full of cross-references, thresholds and exceptions. The handbook that this book draws on does something valuable: it answers the practical questions in a short question-and-answer format. This book keeps that substance and turns it into a structured, teachable narrative.

What this book does

  • Explains the logic, not only the rule. Every threshold is accompanied by the reason for it and the practical consequence.
  • Shows the arithmetic. Minimum dilution, promoters’ contribution, lock-ins, anchor allocations, allotment splits and price discovery are worked through with numbers.
  • Draws the process. Diagrams show the six IPO stages, the intermediary ecosystem, the allocation structure, the flow of money in ASBA/UPI bidding, and the timeline of an offer.
  • Covers the whole family of offerings. Besides IPOs, it treats offers for sale, QIPs, rights issues, preferential issues, listed non-convertible debentures, REITs and InvITs, and helps you choose between routes.
  • Flags what has moved on. Boxes marked “Since 2019” and a dedicated chapter point out the main changes after the handbook’s cut-off, so you know where to verify.
  • Lets you test yourself. Practice problems with solutions and checklists turn knowledge into skill.

How to read it

Readers new to the subject should read Parts I to V in order; they follow the life of an IPO. Practitioners can jump straight to the chapter they need, and use Appendix B as a one-page numbers sheet. Students preparing for an examination will find the chapter summaries, the practice problems in Chapter 30, and the glossary the most useful.

Colour code of the boxes

Key facts (teal) summarise the numbers and rules. Worked examples (green) show arithmetic. Practical notes (amber) give commentary that goes beyond the text of the rules. Since 2019 (purple) flags later developments. Warnings (red) highlight common mistakes and serious consequences.
